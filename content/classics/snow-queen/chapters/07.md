# The Prince, the Princess and the Golden Coach

The bedroom was very big. The ceiling looked like a tall palm tree with leaves of glass. In the middle of the room, two beds hung from a thick golden stem, like two big lilies. One was white, and the princess slept in it. The other was red, and Gerda hoped to find Kai there. She pushed one red leaf aside. She saw a brown neck.

"It is Kai!" she cried, and she held the lamp near him. The dreams rushed back into the room on their horses. The young man woke up. He turned his head, and she saw his face.

It was not Kai.

It was a prince. He looked at her, and the princess woke up too. She sat up in her white lily bed and asked, "Who is there?"

Little Gerda began to cry. She told them her whole story. She told them about Kai, and about the crows who had helped her.

"Poor little girl," said the prince and the princess. They praised the crows. They said that they were not angry, but that the crows must not do it again. This time, they would get a reward.

"Do you want to fly away free?" asked the princess. "Or do you want a place at the court, with all you can eat from the kitchen?"

The crow and his tame sweetheart bowed. They chose the second. They were thinking of their old age. "It is good to be sure of something in the winter," they said.

The prince got out of his bed, and he let Gerda sleep in it. That was all that he could do. She folded her little hands, and she thought, "How good people and animals are!" Then she closed her eyes and slept very sweetly. The dreams came flying back in. They looked like angels now, and they drew a little sled. On it sat Kai, and he nodded to her. But it was only a dream. It was all gone as soon as she woke up.

The next day, they dressed her from her head to her feet in silk and velvet. They asked her to stay at the palace and have a good time. But she asked only for a little carriage with a horse in front, and a pair of small boots. Then she would drive out into the wide world to look for Kai.

They gave her boots, and a muff, and she was dressed beautifully. When she was ready to go, a new coach of pure gold stopped at the door. The arms of the prince and the princess shone on it like a star. The driver, the footmen, and the riders all had golden crowns on their heads. The prince and the princess themselves helped her into the coach and wished her good luck.

The crow from the wood, who was now married, went with her for the first three miles. He sat next to her, because he could not bear to ride with his back to the horses. The other crow stood in the doorway and clapped her wings. She did not go with them, because she had a headache, ever since she got a place at court and had too much to eat. Inside, the coach was lined with sugar cakes, and in the seat there was fruit and gingerbread.

"Goodbye, goodbye!" cried the prince and the princess. Little Gerda wept, and the crow wept too. After three miles, the crow said goodbye. It was a sad parting. He flew up into a tree and beat his black wings as long as he could see the coach, which shone like the bright sun.
