# The Flowers' Stories and the Crow

Gerda lived in the old woman's house for many days. It was a pleasant life. She ate sweet cherries, and she played in the garden, and the old woman combed her hair every evening. But one morning, when she looked at the old woman's sun hat, which was painted with flowers, she saw a rose on it. The old woman had forgotten to make that one go away.

It is like that with people who are not careful. Gerda looked at the rose, and she thought of her own roses at home. Suddenly, she remembered everything.

"Oh!" she cried. "How long I have stayed here! I have forgotten little Kai!" She ran all around the garden. "Where is he? Do you know where he is?" she asked the flowers. "Is he dead and gone?"

"No," said the roses. "He is not dead. We have been in the ground, and all the dead are there. Kai is not there."

"Thank you," said Gerda. She went to the other flowers. She looked into their cups. "Do you know where little Kai is?"

But every flower stood in the sun and dreamed its own story. Gerda heard a great many of them, but none of them said anything about Kai.

The tiger lily said, "Hear the drum, boom, boom." The morning glory said, "A beautiful castle stands on the hill." The snowdrop said, "Between the trees, there is a swing, and two little girls sit on it." Each flower told a long tale, sad or happy. But none of them knew Kai.

At last, Gerda tied up her dress and ran to the garden gate. It was locked. But she pushed at the rusty lock, and it opened. She ran out into the wide world on her bare little feet. Three times, she looked behind her, but nobody was following her.

At last, she was too tired to run, and she sat on a big stone. When she looked around, she saw that the summer was over. It was late autumn. In the garden of the old woman, it had always been summer.

"Oh, how long I stayed there!" said Gerda. "Autumn is here. I must not rest."

She got up, but her feet were sore and tired. How cold and wet everything was! The long leaves of the willow were quite yellow, and the fog dripped from them. One leaf fell after another. Only the sloe tree still had fruit, but it was so sour that it hurt the mouth. Oh, how grey and dull it was in the wide world!

She had to sit down again to rest. A big crow hopped on the snow in front of her. He had been looking at her for a long time. He nodded his head and said, "Caw, caw! Good day, good day!" He could not say it better. But he meant well by the little girl, and he asked her where she was going all alone in the wide world.

The word "alone" Gerda understood very well. She told the crow the whole story of her life and asked if he had seen Kai.

The crow nodded thoughtfully and said, "Maybe, maybe!"

"What? Do you think that you have?" cried Gerda. She nearly kissed the crow to death.

"Easy, easy!" said the crow. "I think that it may be little Kai. But he has forgotten you by now, for the princess."

"Does he live with a princess?" asked Gerda.

"Yes, listen," said the crow. "But it is so hard for me to speak your language. If you understood crow language, I could tell it better."

"No, I have not learned it," said Gerda. "But my grandmother knows it, and she can also speak the language of nonsense. I wish that I had learned it."

"It does not matter," said the crow. "I will tell you as well as I can. But it will not be good."

And he told her all that he knew.

"In this kingdom, where we are now, there lives a princess who is very, very clever. She has read all the newspapers in the world, and she has forgotten them again, so clever she is. Not long ago, she sat on her throne. That is not so much fun, they say. And she began to sing a song. The song was, 'Why should I not marry?' 'That is not a bad idea,' she thought, and she wanted to marry. But she wanted a husband who could answer when she spoke to him. She did not want a man who only looked grand. That is so boring.

"So she had all the ladies of the court called together. When they heard what she wanted, they were very pleased. 'I like that,' they said. 'I thought of that very thing the other day.' You can believe every word that I say. I have a tame sweetheart, who goes freely about in the palace. She told me everything."

His sweetheart was, of course, a crow.
