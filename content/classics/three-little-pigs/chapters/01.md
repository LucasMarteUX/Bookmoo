# Three Little Pigs Go Out into the World

Once upon a time, when pigs could talk and monkeys chewed tobacco, there was an old mother pig. She lived in a small, warm house with her three little pigs. It was a very busy house. The little pigs ate and slept and played in the mud, and the old mother pig worked all day to find food for them.

But the little pigs grew bigger every month. They ate more and more. They ate so much that the old mother pig could not find enough food for all of them. One morning, she sat at the kitchen table and looked at the empty bowls.

"My dear children," she said, "you are big pigs now. I love you very much, but I do not have enough food for you. You must go out into the world and find your fortune. You must each build a house, and take care of yourselves."

The three little pigs looked at each other. They were a little afraid. They had never been away from home before. But they were also excited.

"Where will we go?" asked the first little pig.

"Wherever the road goes," said the old mother pig. "Be careful, be clever, and be kind to everyone. And always remember, a wolf lives in the wood. Do not talk to him. Do not trust him. He is very hungry, and he likes pigs for dinner."

She kissed each of her children on the nose. She gave each of them a little bag with some bread and a red apple. Then she waved goodbye at the door, and she cried a little, but she smiled.

The three little pigs set off together. They walked along the dusty road with their bags on their backs. It was a bright, sunny morning. The birds were singing. They sang a song as they walked:

"Off we go, off we go,
Down the road, and don't be slow,
Each will build a house today,
Then we will work and then we will play."

They walked until noon, and then they sat down under a tree to eat their bread. They talked about their plans.

"I will build my house first," said the first little pig. "I will build it quickly, so that I can play."

"I will build mine with care," said the second. "I want it to be strong."

"I will build mine very, very well," said the third. "I will take my time. It is more important to have a good house than a fast one."

The other two laughed at him. "You are too slow," they said. "You will be building all summer."

"Maybe," said the third little pig. "But I will be safe."

After lunch, they came to a place where the road divided into three. There was a road to the left, a road to the right, and a road that went straight ahead. They each took a different road.

"Goodbye!" they said. "We will visit each other soon!"

"Be careful of the wolf!" said the third little pig.

"We will!" they said. And they went away.
