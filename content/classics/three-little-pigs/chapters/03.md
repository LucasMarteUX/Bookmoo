# The House of Sticks

The second little pig walked along his road. He was not as lazy as his brother, but he did not want to work too hard. He thought, "A house of straw is too weak. But a house of brick is too much work. I will find something in between."

Soon, he met a man with a big bundle of sticks. They were strong sticks, as thick as a finger, and they were brown and rough.

"Good afternoon, sir," said the second little pig. "Please, give me those sticks. I want to build a house."

"Sticks?" said the man. "That is better than straw. But it is still not very strong."

"It will be strong enough," said the little pig. "I will make it well."

"Very well," said the man, and he gave him the sticks.

The little pig took them to a spot near a stream. He worked all afternoon. He put the sticks in the ground, and he tied them together with long grass. He made a floor and four walls and a pointed roof. When he was finished, he stood back and looked at his house. It was a small brown house, rough and a little crooked. But it was a real house, with a door and a window and a chimney of mud.

"It is a very fine house," he said. "It is a good deal stronger than straw."

He went inside. He sat down by the window and ate his apple. He felt very proud. "Now I can rest," he said.

But he did not rest for long. As the sun went down, he heard a heavy step on the path. A shadow fell across the window. A face looked in at him. It was a long, grey face with sharp teeth.

"Little pig, little pig," said the wolf, "let me come in."

The second little pig held his breath. He ran to the door and leaned against it with all his strength.

"No, no," he said, "not by the hair of my chinny chin chin!"

"Then I will huff, and I will puff, and I will blow your house in!" said the wolf.

He huffed, and he puffed. He puffed and huffed. He took the biggest breath of his life, and he blew with all his might.

The sticks cracked. The walls shook. The roof began to move. The wolf blew once more, and the whole house fell down with a great crash. Sticks flew in all directions. The little pig ran out of the door and tried to hide behind a tree, but the wolf was too fast.

And the wolf ate up the second little pig.

"Two," said the wolf, and he patted his stomach. "That was a better meal than the first. But I am still hungry. There is one more pig in this wood. I will find him."

And he walked off in the dark, singing a little song to himself.
