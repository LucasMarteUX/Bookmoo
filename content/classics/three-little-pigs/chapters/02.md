# The House of Straw

The first little pig walked along the road for an hour. He was hot, and he was tired, and he wanted to build his house as fast as he could. He did not want to work hard. He wanted to eat his apple and lie down in the sun.

Soon, he met a man. The man was walking along the road with a big bundle of straw on his back. It was dry, golden straw, and it smelled like summer.

"Good afternoon, sir," said the first little pig. "Please, give me that straw. I want to build a house."

The man looked at the little pig. "A house of straw?" he said. "It is not very strong."

"I do not mind," said the little pig. "It is quick, and it is easy."

"Very well," said the man. He laughed, and he gave him the straw.

The little pig was very pleased. He carried the straw to a green field next to the road. He tied it into bundles, and he made four walls and a roof. In one afternoon, his house was ready. It was a round, yellow house, and it smelled sweet. It had a little round door and one small window.

"What a good house!" said the little pig. He went in, and he lay down on a soft pile of straw. He ate his apple. Then he went to sleep.

In the evening, he heard a noise outside. It was a knock at the door.

Knock, knock, knock.

The little pig woke up. He went to the door and looked out through a small crack. On the step, there was a big grey wolf. He had long sharp teeth and yellow eyes. His tongue hung out. He looked very hungry.

"Little pig, little pig," said the wolf, in a sweet voice, "let me come in."

The little pig's heart jumped. He remembered what his mother had said. He was very frightened, but he did not open the door.

"No, no," he said. "Not by the hair of my chinny chin chin!"

The wolf's smile became thin. "Then I will huff, and I will puff, and I will blow your house in!" he said.

He took a deep breath. He huffed. He puffed. He blew as hard as he could.

The straw house shook. The walls began to move. They flew apart like a pile of leaves, and the golden straw went up in the air and floated away over the field. The little pig stood in the middle of the field with nothing around him. He did not even have time to run.

And the wolf ate up the first little pig.

When the wolf had finished, he licked his lips. He sat in the field for a moment and looked at the stars. Then he stood up. He was still hungry. "That was only a little snack," he said. "I wonder where the other pigs are."
