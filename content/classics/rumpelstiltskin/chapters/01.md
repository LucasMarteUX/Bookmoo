# The Miller's Boast

Once upon a time, there was a poor miller. He had a small mill by a river, and he ground wheat into flour for the farmers. It was hard work, and he did not earn much money. But he had one great treasure. He had a beautiful daughter.

The miller was very proud of his daughter. She was kind, and she was clever, and she was very pretty. He liked to talk about her to everyone he met. He talked about her at the market, and at the church, and at the inn. He told people that she could sing like a bird and cook like a queen. And as he told it more and more, the stories grew bigger.

One day, the miller had to go to the king's castle. He had to pay a small tax. He stood in the great hall, with his hat in his hands. He was very nervous. The king looked at him from his high chair. The king was a tall man with a hard face, and he liked gold more than anything in the world.

"Well, miller," said the king. "Is business good?"

"Oh, yes, Your Majesty," said the miller. He wanted to seem important. He wanted the king to think well of him. His mouth ran faster than his mind. "I am a poor man, but I have a daughter who is very special. She is so clever that she can spin straw into gold."

The king sat up. His eyes became bright.

"What did you say?" he said. "Say it again."

"My daughter, Your Majesty," said the miller. His face went red. He wished that he could take back his words. "She can... she can spin straw into gold."

"That is a very useful art," said the king slowly. "I like it. If your daughter is as clever as you say, bring her to my castle tomorrow. I will see what she can do."

"Yes, Your Majesty," said the miller. His knees were shaking.

He ran home. He was afraid. "What have I done?" he said to himself. "It was only a story. It was only something to say. Now the king wants my daughter, and she cannot do it. Nobody can do it."

He did not sleep that night. In the morning, he told his daughter everything. She listened, and her face became white.

"Father," she said. "How could you tell the king such a thing?"

"I did not think," said the miller. He put his face in his hands. "I am so sorry. But we have no choice. The king ordered it. If we do not go, he will be very angry."

So the next morning, the miller took his daughter to the castle. They walked through the big gate and across the courtyard. The servants looked at the girl and whispered. In the great hall, the king was waiting for them. He looked at the miller's daughter. Then he smiled.

"Come with me," he said.

He took her up a long stair, to a room at the top of a tower. It was a big, bare room with a stone floor. In the middle of the room, there was a small wooden spinning wheel. All around, there were heaps and heaps of straw. It was piled up to the ceiling.

"Here is a wheel," said the king. "And here is the straw. You must begin to work at once. If by tomorrow morning, all this straw is not spun into gold, you will die."

The miller's daughter stared at him. She could not say a word.

The king went out and turned the key in the lock. She heard his steps go down the stairs. Then it was silent.
