# The Little Man Comes Back

A year went by. The queen lived in the castle, with fine clothes and good food and many servants. The king was kind to her, in his own way. She did not think about the little man any more. She was almost happy.

Then she had a baby. It was a beautiful baby boy, with a round face and bright eyes. The queen held him in her arms, and she cried for joy. She sang to him and rocked him. The king was proud, and the whole country celebrated. Bells rang, and there were fires on the hills.

The queen loved her son more than anything in the world. She did not leave his side. She held him all day, and she looked at his little hands. She had forgotten her promise completely.

One evening, the queen was sitting in her room with the baby. The servants had gone, and the candles were burning low. Suddenly, the door opened. She looked up.

It was the little man.

He stood in the middle of the room, with his red hat and his long beard. He looked at her with his bright eyes, and he did not smile.

"Good evening, Your Majesty," he said. "I have come for what you promised me."

The queen's face became white. She held the baby tighter. "What do you want?" she said. Her voice was very small.

"You know very well," said the little man. "Three nights, I spun your straw into gold. You gave me your necklace, and your ring. And on the third night, you made me a promise. You said that when you were queen, you would give me your first child."

"No," said the queen. "No, please. I did not mean it. I was so afraid. I did not know what I was saying."

"A promise is a promise," said the little man. "Give me the child."

The queen began to cry. She put her cheek against the baby's soft head. "Please," she said. "Please do not take him. I will give you anything else. I will give you all the riches of the kingdom. I will give you gold, and jewels, and land. You can have it all. Only leave my child."

The little man shook his head. "I do not want your riches," he said. "Gold I can make myself, as you know. But something that lives is dearer to me than all the treasure in the world. A living child is worth more than all the gold of this earth. I want your son."

The queen cried and begged. She cried so hard that the little man felt something move inside him. It was a feeling that he did not often have. He watched her for a moment, and he frowned.

"Well," he said at last. "I will give you a chance. I am not a hard-hearted man. I will give you three days. If in three days, you can find out my name, then you may keep your child. But if you cannot, he is mine."

"Three days," said the queen. She wiped her eyes. "Yes. I will find out. What is your name?"

"Ah," said the little man. He smiled for the first time. "That is for you to find out. I will come again tomorrow evening. You may guess as many times as you like. Good night, Your Majesty."

He went out. The door closed behind him, and the room was silent.

The queen sat for a long time without moving. The baby slept. The candle burned down. Then she stood up, and she went to the window. The stars were shining over the dark garden. She said to herself, "I must find the name. I must."

All night, she sat up. She wrote down every name that she knew. She wrote the names of the saints and the names of the kings. She wrote the names of her servants and her old neighbors. She wrote names that she had heard in songs. She wrote names from the Bible and names from the old tales. She wrote until her hand hurt, and the paper was full.

In the morning, she called her most trusted messenger. He was a loyal man, and he knew the land well.

"I need you to do something for me," she said. "Go out into the country. Ask in every village, in every town. Ask people what strange names they know. Bring me every name that you find. Do not stop. It is very important."

"Yes, Your Majesty," said the messenger. He bowed, and he went out. He took his horse and rode away along the road.
