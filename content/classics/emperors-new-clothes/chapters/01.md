# The Emperor Who Loved Clothes

Many, many years ago, there lived an emperor. He loved new clothes more than anything in the world. He spent all his money on them. He did not care about his soldiers. He did not like to go to the theater. He did not want to take a walk in the park. The only thing that he enjoyed was to drive through the city in his carriage, in a new suit of clothes, and to show it to the people.

He had a different coat for every hour of the day. When people say of a king, "The king is in his council room," in this country they said of the emperor, "The emperor is in his dressing room."

The emperor's dressing room was the biggest room in the palace. It had a hundred wooden doors, and behind every door, there were clothes. There were red coats and blue coats, green coats and golden coats. There were hats with feathers, and shoes with silver buckles. There were long cloaks for the winter and light shirts for the summer. In the middle of the room, there was a great mirror, as tall as a tree. Every morning, the emperor stood in front of it, and his servants dressed him.

"Is this coat too red?" he asked.

"It is perfect, Your Majesty," said the servants.

"Is this hat too tall?"

"It is perfect, Your Majesty."

The emperor smiled at himself in the mirror. Then he went out to show his clothes to the world.

The city where he lived was a big, happy city. It was full of music and markets and flags. Every day, many visitors came from far countries. They stood in the streets to see the emperor drive by. When the emperor passed in his golden carriage, wearing a new suit, they cheered and waved their hats. This made the emperor very happy. He thought that he was the best-dressed man in the world.

One day, two men came to the city. They were not ordinary visitors. They were swindlers, and they were very clever. They had come to trick people and take their money. They knew that the emperor loved new clothes, and they had a plan.

They went to the market square, and they stood on a box. "Come, come!" they cried. "We are weavers from a faraway land! We make the finest cloth in the world!"

The people of the town looked at them and listened.

"Our cloth is more beautiful than any other," said the first swindler. "The colors are so bright that they hurt the eyes. The patterns are like magic."

"And that is not all," said the second swindler. He lowered his voice. "Our cloth has a secret. It has a wonderful power. The clothes made from this cloth cannot be seen by a man who is not fit for his job. They cannot be seen by a man who is very stupid."

"Ohhh," said the people.

The news went through the town very quickly. In a few hours, it was in the palace. And the emperor heard it. He put down his comb, and he listened.
