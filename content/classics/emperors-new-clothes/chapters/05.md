# "But He Has Nothing On!"

The emperor walked in the procession, under a beautiful canopy. It was a day of bright sun, and the whole city was in the streets. People stood on the sidewalks, and they looked out of the windows. They had been waiting for hours.

When they saw the emperor, they all began to cheer. "How beautiful the emperor's new suit is!" they cried. "What a long train he has! How well it fits him!"

Nobody wanted to say that he saw nothing. If he did, people would think that he was not fit for his job, or that he was very stupid. No suit of the emperor had ever been so much admired.

The emperor walked slowly, with his head high and his back straight. The two servants behind him carried the train that did not exist. The old minister walked at his side. The swindlers stood on the steps of the palace, and they smiled and bowed.

The procession came to a big square. In the front row of the crowd, there was a little child. She was sitting on her father's shoulders, and she could see the emperor very well. She looked at him, and she looked at him again. She was too young to know about court and offices and important positions. She only knew what she saw.

"But he has nothing on at all!" she cried.

"Listen to the voice of an innocent child!" said her father. He was surprised, and he laughed. Then he whispered to the man next to him, and he told him what the child had said.

"He has nothing on," said the man. He told the woman next to him.

"The child says that he has nothing on at all!"

The words went through the crowd like fire. One person whispered it to another. A man at the back of the square said it aloud. A woman laughed. A boy shouted, "It is true!" Then all the people, young and old, rich and poor, began to say it, louder and louder.

"He has nothing on! He has nothing on at all!"

The emperor heard them. He felt a shiver go down his back. He felt very cold. Suddenly, he knew that they were right. He looked down at his arms and his legs. He saw only his own bare skin.

"Oh no," he thought. "They are right. I have nothing on. I am walking in the street without any clothes."

But he thought to himself, "I must go on to the end of the procession. I cannot stop now. If I stop, everyone will laugh at me even more." So he lifted his head higher, and he walked on with great dignity.

The two servants behind him walked on too, with even more care than before. They held up the long train that did not exist. They did not look at anyone.

The sun shone down on the square. The people laughed, and the child on her father's shoulders clapped her hands. The emperor walked on, step by step, with a very red face. He did not look to the right or to the left. And that is the end of the story.
