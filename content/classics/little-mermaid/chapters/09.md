# The Wedding Night

The wedding bells rang. The streets were full of flowers and flags. In the great church, the priest joined the hands of the prince and the princess, and the whole town sang. The little mermaid stood behind them in a dress of silk and gold. She held the long cloth of the bride. But she did not hear the music, and she did not see the holy ceremony. She thought only about her last night, and about everything that she had lost.

That same evening, the prince and his bride went on board the ship. The cannons were fired, and the flags waved. On the deck, there was a royal tent of gold and purple, with soft beds. There the bride and groom would sleep in the cool, clear night.

The sails filled with wind. The ship ran lightly over the clear sea. When it grew dark, colored lamps were lit, and the sailors danced a merry dance on the deck.

The little mermaid remembered the first time that she had come up out of the sea. Then, too, she had seen a party full of joy. Now she joined the dance. She flew over the floor like a swallow that someone is chasing. Everyone shouted with joy. Never had she danced so beautifully. Sharp knives seemed to cut her tender feet. But she did not feel it. The pain in her heart was much greater.

She knew that this was the last evening. It was the last time that she would breathe the same air as the one for whom she had left her family and her home. She had given up her beautiful voice. She had suffered terrible pain every day. And he did not know. It was the last night that she would see the same stars and the deep sea.

A long night, without thoughts and without dreams, was waiting for her. She had no soul. She had not been able to win one.

The party on the ship went on until long after midnight. She laughed and danced, with the thought of death in her heart. The prince kissed his beautiful bride, and she played with his black hair. Then, hand in hand, they went to rest in the splendid tent.

It became quiet on the ship. Only the man at the wheel was awake. The little mermaid put her white arms on the side of the ship, and she looked toward the east for the first pink light of morning. The first ray of the sun, she knew, would kill her.

Then she saw her sisters come up out of the sea. They were as pale as she was. Their long, beautiful hair did not blow in the wind. It was cut off.

"We have given our hair to the witch," they said. "We did it so that you do not have to die tonight. She has given us a knife. Look, here it is. It is very sharp. Before the sun rises, you must put it into the prince's heart. When his warm blood falls on your feet, they will grow together into a fish tail. You will be a mermaid again. You can come down to us, and live your three hundred years, before you turn into dead sea foam."

"Hurry," said the sisters. "Either he or you must die before the sun comes up. Our old grandmother is so sad that her white hair has fallen out, as ours fell under the witch's scissors. Kill the prince, and come back to us. Hurry! Do you see the red light in the sky? In a few minutes, the sun will rise, and then you will die."

They gave a deep, strange sigh, and they sank under the waves.

The little mermaid pulled back the purple curtain of the tent. She saw the beautiful bride, who was sleeping with her head on the prince's chest. She bent down. She kissed his forehead. She looked at the sky, where the pink light was growing brighter and brighter. She looked at the sharp knife. And again she looked at the prince.

In his sleep, he said the name of his bride. She was the only one in his thoughts. The knife shook in the mermaid's hand.

Then she threw it far out over the waves. The water turned red where it fell. It looked as if drops of blood were bubbling up. With eyes that were already half dead, she looked at the prince one last time. Then she threw herself from the ship into the sea. She felt her body turn to foam.
