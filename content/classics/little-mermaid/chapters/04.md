# The Prince in the Storm

At first, the little mermaid was happy. She thought, "Now the prince will come down to me, and he will live in my father's palace." Then she remembered that human beings cannot live in the water. Only as a dead man could he come to the sea king's castle.

"No!" she said. "He must not die!"

She swam between the beams and the planks that floated on the waves. She forgot that they could crush her. She dived deep, and she came up again, high on a wave. At last, she found the prince. He could no longer swim in the angry sea. His arms and his legs were tired. His beautiful eyes were closed. He would have drowned, if the little mermaid had not come.

She held his head above the water. Then she let the waves carry them both wherever they went.

In the morning, the storm was over. There was not one piece of the ship to be seen. The sun rose red and bright out of the water. It seemed to give new color to the prince's cheeks, but his eyes were still closed.

The little mermaid kissed his high, beautiful forehead. She stroked back his wet hair. He looked like the marble statue in her little garden. She kissed him again. She wished with all her heart that he would live.

At last, she saw land. High blue mountains rose up before her, and white snow lay on top of them, like swans resting. Down at the shore, there were green woods. And near the woods, there was a large building. She did not know if it was a church or a convent. Lemon trees and orange trees grew in the garden, and tall palm trees stood by the gate. The sea made a little bay there. The water was still and very deep. She swam to the white sand, and she laid the prince on it. She took care that his head lay high, in the warm sunshine.

Bells began to ring in the big white building. A number of young girls came out into the garden. The little mermaid swam out farther, behind some high rocks. She covered her hair and her chest with sea foam, so that no one could see her face. She waited to see who would find the poor prince.

Soon a young girl came near. She seemed frightened, but only for a moment. Then she ran to get other people. The little mermaid saw that the prince came to life, and that he smiled at all around him. But he did not smile at her. He did not know that she had saved him. She felt very sad. When he was carried into the large building, she dived down sorrowfully into the water, and she went home to her father's palace.

She had always been quiet and thoughtful. Now she was even more so. Her sisters asked her what she had seen on her first journey up. But she did not tell them anything.

Many evenings and many mornings, she rose to the place where she had left the prince. She saw the fruit in the garden ripen, and she saw it gathered. She saw the snow melt on the high mountains. But she never saw the prince, and so she always went home even sadder than before.

Her only comfort was to sit in her little garden and put her arms around the beautiful marble statue, which was so like the prince. She did not take care of her flowers anymore. They grew wild over the paths, and they twisted their long stems and leaves around the branches of the trees, so that it was quite dark there.

At last, she could not keep her secret any longer. She told one of her sisters. Soon all the others knew, but no one else. They told it to a few other mermaids, who did not tell it to anyone except their closest friends. One of them knew who the prince was. She had seen the party on the ship. She knew where he came from, and where his kingdom lay.

"Come, little sister!" said the other princesses. They put their arms around each other's shoulders, and they rose in a long line to the surface. They stopped in front of the place where they knew the prince's palace stood.

It was built of light yellow stone. A flight of white steps went down to the sea. Gold domes rose over the roof, and between the pillars stood marble statues. Through the tall glass windows, you could see beautiful rooms, with silk curtains and rich paintings on the walls. In the middle of the biggest room, a fountain threw its water high into the air.

Now that she knew where he lived, the little mermaid went there on many evenings and many nights. She went much nearer the land than any of the others dared to go. She even went into the narrow river under the marble balcony. There she sat and looked at the young prince, who thought that he was quite alone in the moonlight.

On many evenings, she saw him sail in his fine boat, with music playing and flags flying. She hid in the green reeds, and if the wind caught her long silver veil, those who saw it thought that it was a swan opening its wings.
