# Five Sisters and Their Stories

In that family, there was a rule. Each princess could go up to the top of the sea on her fifteenth birthday, and not before. The sisters were one year apart. So the oldest sister went first, and the youngest had to wait five long years.

When the oldest sister came back, she had a hundred things to tell. But the best thing, she said, was to lie on a sandbank in the calm sea, in the moonlight, and to look at a big city on the coast. There, the lights shone like hundreds of stars. She could hear music, and the noise of carriages, and the bells of the churches.

The little mermaid listened to every word. In the evening, she stood at the open window, and she looked up through the dark blue water. She could see the moon and the stars, but they looked pale. If a dark shape went over them, she knew that it was a whale, or a ship full of people. Those people did not know that a little mermaid was standing far below, with her white hands stretched up toward their ship.

The next year, the second sister had her birthday. She swam up just as the sun was going down. It was the most beautiful thing that she had ever seen. The whole sky was gold, she said, and the clouds were red and purple. A flock of white swans flew over the water, like a long white scarf. She swam toward the sun, but it sank, and the pink light on the sea slowly went away.

The year after, the third sister went up. She was the bravest. She swam up a wide river. She saw green hills with vines on them, and big houses between the trees. She heard birds sing. The sun was so hot that she had to dive under the water to cool her face. In a small bay, she saw some human children. They were playing in the water. She wanted to play with them, but they were afraid, and they ran away. Then a little black animal came. It was a dog, but she did not know it. It barked so loudly at her that she swam away in fear. But she never forgot the green hills and the dear children who could swim without a fish tail.

The fourth sister was not so brave. She stayed far out in the open sea, and she said that it was the best place of all. You could see for miles and miles, and the sky looked like a big glass bell. She had seen ships, but they were far away and looked like seagulls. Dolphins had jumped and turned over in the waves, and big whales had blown water out of their noses, like hundreds of fountains.

The fifth sister's birthday was in the winter. So she saw something that the others did not see. The sea was green, and big icebergs floated on it. Each one looked like a pearl, she said, but they were much bigger than a church. Some of them looked like strange animals, and they sparkled like diamonds. She sat on one of the biggest, and the wind blew her long hair. All the ships sailed quickly away from her. In the evening, clouds covered the sky. There was thunder and lightning, and the black sea lifted the blocks of ice high in the air. Then they shone in the red light. The sailors were afraid. But she sat quietly on her iceberg and watched the blue lightning strike the water.

At first, each sister was happy about the new things that she saw. But when they were older, and they could go up when they wanted, they were soon bored. They wished to go home. After a month, they said that it was better down there, and that there is no place like home.

On many evenings, the five sisters went up together, arm in arm. They had beautiful voices, much more beautiful than any human voice. When a storm came, and a ship was in danger, they swam in front of it, and they sang about the beauty of the deep sea. They told the sailors, "Do not be afraid to sink here." But the sailors could not understand the song. They thought that it was the noise of the storm. They never saw the beauty of the deep, because when the ship sank, they drowned.

When the sisters went up in the evening, the little mermaid stayed behind, all alone. She looked after them. She wanted to cry. But a mermaid has no tears, and so she suffers even more.

"Oh, if I were only fifteen!" she said. "I know that I will love the world up there, and all the people who live in it."
