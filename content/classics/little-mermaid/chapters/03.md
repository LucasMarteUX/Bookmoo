# The Fifteenth Birthday

At last, the day came. The little mermaid was fifteen years old.

"Now you are a big girl," said her grandmother. "Come here. Let me dress you like your sisters." She put a wreath of white flowers on the girl's hair. Every petal was half a pearl. Then she put eight big oysters on the little mermaid's tail, to show that she was a princess.

"It hurts!" said the little mermaid.

"You must suffer a little to look beautiful," said the old lady.

The little mermaid did not care about her fine clothes. She would have been glad to take off the wreath and the oysters. She liked the red flowers in her garden better. But she did not say anything. "Goodbye," she said, and she rose through the water, as light as a bubble.

The sun had just gone down when she put her head above the water. The clouds were red and gold. In the pale pink sky, the evening star shone clear and beautiful. The air was soft and fresh, and the sea was as smooth as a mirror.

A big ship with three masts lay on the water. Only one sail was open, because there was no wind. The sailors sat on the ropes and on the sides of the ship. There was music and singing. When it became dark, hundreds of lamps were lit. They were red and yellow and blue and green, and the whole ship looked like a floating garden of lights.

The little mermaid swam close to the window of the biggest room. Every time that the sea lifted her, she could look in. She saw many people in fine clothes. But the most handsome of all was a young prince, with big black eyes. He was only sixteen. It was his birthday, and that was why there was a party on the ship.

The sailors danced on the deck. When the young prince came out, more than a hundred rockets went up in the sky. They burst, and they were as bright as day. The little mermaid was so afraid that she dived under the water. But she put her head up again at once. It looked as if all the stars of heaven were falling around her. She had never seen such fire. Great suns turned round and round. Fire-fish flew in the clear blue air, and the calm sea shone with every color.

It was very late, but the little mermaid could not look away from the ship and the beautiful prince. The lamps were put out. No more rockets went up. The cannon stopped. But in the depth of the sea, there was a rumbling and a groaning.

She did not go. She floated up and down on the water, so that she could look into the cabin. But the ship began to move faster. The sails opened one after another. The waves rose higher. Great clouds came. There was lightning far away.

"A storm is coming!" cried the sailors, and they took in the sails.

The big ship rocked and ran on through the wild sea. The waves rose like black mountains. They wanted to break over the masts. But the ship dived like a swan between them, and it rose again on the high white water.

To the little mermaid, this seemed like a good game. But the sailors did not think so. The ship cracked and groaned. The thick planks bent under the hard hits of the sea. The main mast broke in the middle, like a thin stick. The ship fell over on its side, and the water came into it.

Now the little mermaid saw that the people were in danger. She herself had to be careful of the beams and pieces of wood that floated on the water. For a moment, it was so dark that she could not see anything. Then the lightning came again, and it was so bright that she knew everyone on the ship. She looked for the young prince.
