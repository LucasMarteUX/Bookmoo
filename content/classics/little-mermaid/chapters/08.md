# The Prince's Secret

"Do you not love me best of all?" the little mermaid's eyes seemed to ask, when the prince took her in his arms and kissed her forehead.

"Yes," said the prince, "you are dearest to me. You have the best heart of all. You are so faithful to me. You are like a young girl that I once saw, but I will never find her again. I was on a ship that sank. The waves threw me onto a beach near a holy house, where many young girls live and pray. The youngest of them found me on the sand, and she saved my life. I saw her only two times. She is the only person in the world that I can love. But you are like her. You almost take her picture out of my heart. She belongs to the holy house. So good luck has sent you to me. We will never part."

"He does not know that I saved his life," thought the little mermaid. "I carried him over the sea to the wood where the holy house stands. I sat behind the foam, and I waited to see if anyone would come. I saw the pretty girl that he loves more than me." The mermaid sighed deeply, but she could not cry. "He says that the girl belongs to the holy house. She will never come out into the world. They will never meet again. But I am with him. I see him every day. I will take care of him, and love him, and give my life for him."

Then people began to say that the prince was going to marry. The king of the next country had a beautiful daughter. The prince was going to visit her, on a splendid ship. He said to the little mermaid, "I must go. My parents want it. I must see the princess. But they cannot make me bring her home as my wife. I cannot love her. She is not like the beautiful girl from the holy house, and you are like her. If I must choose a wife, I will choose you, my dumb girl with the speaking eyes."

He kissed her red mouth and played with her long hair. He put his head near her heart, and she dreamed of human happiness and of a soul.

"You are not afraid of the sea, my dumb child?" he said, when they stood on the splendid ship. He told her about storms and calm days, and about the strange fish in the deep sea. She smiled, because she knew more than anyone about the bottom of the sea.

On a clear night, with the moon shining, everyone slept except the man at the wheel. The little mermaid sat at the side of the ship. She looked down through the clear water. She thought that she could see her father's palace. Her old grandmother stood high above it with a silver crown. She looked up toward the ship. Then her sisters came up on the waves. They looked at her sadly, and they wrung their white hands. She waved to them, and smiled, and wanted to tell them that she was well and happy. But the cabin boy came near, and her sisters dived down. He thought that the white thing that he saw was only foam.

The next morning, the ship came into the harbor of the neighboring king's beautiful city. All the church bells rang. Trumpets sounded from the high towers. Soldiers stood with flying flags and shining spears. Every day there was a party. But the princess had not come. People said that she was being brought up far away, in a holy house, where she was learning everything that a princess must know. At last, she came.

The little mermaid was eager to see her. She had to say that she had never seen a lovelier face. Her skin was fine and clear, and behind her long dark eyelashes, her blue eyes smiled, faithful and pure.

"It is you!" cried the prince. "You are the one who saved me, when I lay almost dead on the beach!" He took his blushing bride in his arms. "Oh, I am so happy," he said to the little mermaid. "The best thing that I ever hoped for has come true. You will be glad for my happiness, because you love me more than all the others."

The little mermaid kissed his hand. She felt as if her heart was already breaking. The morning of his wedding would bring her death. She would turn into foam on the sea.
