# What the Grandmother Said

The little mermaid loved the human world more and more. She wanted to know everything about people. They could sail across the sea in ships. They could climb mountains above the clouds. Their land was so big that she could not see the end of it. Her sisters could not answer all her questions. So she went to her grandmother, who knew the "lands above the sea" very well.

"Grandmother," she said, "if people do not drown, do they live forever? Do they never die, like us?"

"Oh, yes," said the old lady. "They die too. Their lives are even shorter than ours. We live for three hundred years. But when our time is over, we do not have a grave. We turn into foam on the sea. We do not have a soul that lives forever. We are like green seaweed. When it is cut, it never grows again."

"And people?"

"People have a soul. It lives forever, even after the body turns to dust. It goes up through the clear air, to the stars. We come up out of the sea and look at the land of people. In the same way, their souls go up to beautiful places that we will never see."

The little mermaid was very sad. "Why do we not have a soul?" she said. "I would give all my three hundred years to be a human being for one day. I would give them to go to that happy world above the stars."

"You must not think about that," said the old woman. "We are happier than the people up there."

"So I will die," said the little mermaid. "I will be foam on the sea. I will never hear the music of the waves again. I will never see the flowers or the red sun. Is there nothing that I can do to get a soul?"

"There is only one way," said the grandmother. "A man must love you more than his father and his mother. All his thoughts and all his love must be for you. Then a priest must put his right hand in yours, and the man must promise to love you always. Then his soul will come into your body, and you will have a part of the happiness of people. But that will never happen. Your fish tail is beautiful to us. On land, people think that it is ugly. They do not know any better. They think that you must have two strong things, which they call legs, to be beautiful."

The little mermaid sighed, and she looked sadly at her tail.

"Let us be happy," said the grandmother. "Let us swim and jump for the three hundred years that we have. It is a long time. After that, we can rest. And tonight, there is a ball at the palace."

It was the most beautiful ball that anyone can imagine. The walls and the ceiling of the great ballroom were made of clear crystal. Hundreds of huge shells, pink and green, stood in rows. Blue fire burned in them, and it lit the whole room. The light went out through the crystal walls, and the sea outside shone with it. Fish of every size swam past. Some of them were purple, and some were silver, and some were gold. A wide stream ran through the room, and the mermen and mermaids danced in it and sang. No human voice is so beautiful. The little mermaid sang the most sweetly of all. Everyone clapped. For a moment, she was happy.

But then she thought again about the world above, and about the handsome prince. She was sad because she did not have a soul like his. So she left the palace without a sound. While everyone was laughing and singing, she sat alone in her little garden.

Then she heard a horn, far above her in the water.

"He is sailing up there," she thought. "He is the one that I love. I would give him my whole life. I will do anything to win him, and to win a soul. My sisters are dancing in my father's palace. I will go to the sea witch. I have always been afraid of her. But perhaps she can help me."
