# The Daughters of the Air

The sun came up out of the sea. Its warm light fell softly on the cold foam. The little mermaid did not feel that she was dying. She saw the bright sun. And above her, hundreds of clear, beautiful shapes were floating in the air. They were so light that she could see the white sails of the ship through them, and the red clouds in the sky. Their voices were like music, but so soft that no human ear could hear it, just as no human eye could see them. They had no wings, but they floated in the air, because they were so light.

The little mermaid saw that she had a body like theirs. She rose higher and higher, out of the foam.

"Where am I going?" she asked. Her voice sounded like theirs, so lovely that no music on earth can be compared to it.

"To the daughters of the air," the others answered. "A mermaid has no soul. She can only get one if a human being loves her. The daughters of the air have no soul either. But we can win one by good deeds. We fly to the hot countries, where the bad air kills people. There we bring them cool winds. We carry the smell of flowers through the air, and we bring comfort and health. When we have tried for three hundred years to do all the good that we can, we get a soul that lives forever, and we share the happiness of people. You, poor little mermaid, have tried with all your heart to do good. You have suffered, and you have been strong. Now you have come up into the world of the spirits of the air. By good deeds, you can win a soul in three hundred years."

The little mermaid lifted her clear eyes toward the sun. For the first time, she felt tears in them.

On the ship, there was life and noise again. She saw the prince and his beautiful bride, looking for her. They looked sadly at the foam on the water, as if they knew that she had thrown herself into the waves. Nobody could see her. She kissed the forehead of the bride. She smiled at the prince. Then she rose with the other children of the air, up to a pink cloud that sailed through the sky.

"After three hundred years," she said, "we will float like this into the kingdom of God."

"We may get there sooner," whispered one of them. "Nobody sees us, but we float into the houses of people, where there are children. Every day that we find a good child, who gives joy to his parents and deserves their love, God makes our time shorter. The child does not know when we fly through the room. But when we smile at him with joy, one year is taken from the three hundred. When we see a naughty or wicked child, we must cry tears of sorrow. And every tear adds one day to our time."
