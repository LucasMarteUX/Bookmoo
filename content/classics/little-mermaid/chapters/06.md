# The Sea Witch

The little mermaid left her garden. She swam toward the loud whirlpools. The sea witch lived on the other side of them. The mermaid had never gone that way before. No flowers grew there, and no grass. There was only grey sand, and in front of it the whirlpools. The water turned round and round, like the wheel of a mill, and it pulled everything down into the deep.

She had to swim through these terrible whirlpools. After that, there was a long road over hot, bubbling mud. The witch called it her bog.

Behind the mud, there was a strange forest. The trees were half animal and half plant. They looked like snakes with a hundred heads. Their branches were long, wet arms with fingers like worms. They moved all the time. Anything that they could catch in the sea, they held tight, and they never let it go.

The little mermaid stopped in front of the forest. Her heart was beating very fast. She wanted to go back. But she thought of the prince, and of the soul that she wanted. And she was brave again.

She tied her long hair around her head, so that the arms could not catch it. She crossed her hands on her chest. Then she swam between the ugly arms, as fast as a fish. They stretched out toward her, but she passed them. She saw that each arm held something. Some held the white bones of sailors. Some held old chests from ships. One held a little mermaid, who was dead. That was the most terrible thing that she saw.

At last, she came to a big, wet clearing. Fat water snakes rolled about in it. In the middle, there was a house, made of the bones of dead men. The sea witch sat in front of it. She was feeding a toad from her own mouth, the way you give sugar to a little bird. She called the ugly snakes her "little chickens," and she let them crawl over her chest.

"I know what you want," said the witch. "It is very silly. But you will have it, and it will bring you great trouble, my pretty princess. You want to lose your fish tail. You want two legs like a human being. Then the young prince will love you, and you will get a soul."

The witch laughed a loud, horrible laugh. The toad and the snakes fell to the ground and rolled about.

"You come at the right time," she said. "Tomorrow, when the sun comes up, I could not help you for a year. I will make you a drink. You must swim with it to land before sunrise. Then sit on the shore and drink it. Your tail will split in two, and it will become what people call pretty legs. But it will hurt. It will feel like a sharp sword in your body. Everyone who sees you will say that you are the loveliest girl that they have ever seen. You will walk more lightly than any dancer. But with every step, you will feel as if you were walking on sharp knives, and your feet will bleed. If you can bear all this, I will help you."

"Yes," said the little mermaid, with a shaking voice. She thought of the prince, and of the soul.

"But remember this," said the witch. "When you have the shape of a human being, you can never be a mermaid again. You can never come back to your sisters, or to your father's palace. And if the prince does not love you more than his father and mother, if he does not marry you, you will not get a soul. On the first morning after he marries another woman, your heart will break. You will turn into foam on the water."

"I will do it," said the little mermaid. She was as white as a dead person.

"And you must pay me," said the witch. "I do not ask for a little. You have the most beautiful voice of all the people of the sea. You think that you will win the prince with it. But you must give it to me. I will take the best thing that you have. I must put my own blood in the drink, to make it sharp."

"But if you take my voice," said the little mermaid, "what will I have left?"

"Your beautiful body," said the witch. "Your light walk, and your eyes that can speak. With these, you can win a human heart. Have you lost your courage? Put out your little tongue. I will cut it off. Then you will have the drink."

"Do it," said the little mermaid.

The witch put her kettle on the fire. "Cleaning is a good thing," she said, and she cleaned the kettle with the snakes, which she tied in a knot. Then she cut her own chest, and her black blood fell into the kettle. The steam made terrible shapes. It made the mermaid shiver. The witch threw new things into the kettle every minute. When the drink was boiling, it sounded like a crocodile crying. At last, it was ready. It looked as clear as water.

"Here it is," said the witch. And she cut out the little mermaid's tongue.

Now the little mermaid was dumb. She could not speak, and she could not sing.
