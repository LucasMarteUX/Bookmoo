# Legs Like Knives

The little mermaid swam back, through the forest of arms. She passed the whirlpools and the warm mud. The arms stretched out behind her, but they could not hold her. She had the bottle in her hand, and it shone like a star.

Soon she saw her father's palace. The lights in the ballroom were out. Everyone was asleep. She did not dare to go in. She could not speak, and she was going to leave them forever. Her heart was almost breaking.

She went into the garden. She took one flower from each of her sisters' beds. She sent a thousand kisses to the palace with her hand. Then she swam up through the dark blue water.

The sun had not risen when she came to the prince's palace. She climbed out onto the white marble steps. The moon was clear and bright. She drank the strong, burning drink. It felt like a sharp sword going through her body. She fainted, and she lay on the stones as if she were dead.

When the sun came up, she woke. She felt a sharp pain. But in front of her stood the handsome young prince. He looked at her with his black eyes. She looked down. Her fish tail was gone. She had the prettiest little white legs that any girl could want. But she had no clothes, so she wrapped herself in her long hair.

The prince asked her, "Who are you? How did you come here?" She looked at him gently and sadly with her dark blue eyes. She could not say a word. He took her hand, and he led her into the palace. With every step, she felt as if she was walking on sharp knives. The witch had told her this. But she bore the pain, and she walked at his side, as lightly as a bubble. He and everyone who saw her wondered at her beautiful, gliding walk.

In the palace, they gave her a dress of silk. She was the most beautiful girl there. But she was dumb. She could not speak or sing.

That evening, some girls in silk and gold came and sang for the prince and the king and the queen. One of them sang more sweetly than the others. The prince clapped his hands and smiled at her. This made the little mermaid very sad. She knew that she had once sung much more sweetly. "Oh," she thought, "if he only knew that I gave my voice away forever to be near him!"

Then the girls danced to lovely music. The little mermaid lifted her white arms. She rose on her toes, and she danced. She glided over the floor as nobody had danced before. With every step, she looked more beautiful. Her eyes spoke to the heart better than the songs of the singers.

Everybody was charmed, and the prince most of all. He called her his little lost child. She danced again and again, although every time that her foot touched the floor, it felt like a sharp knife. The prince said that she must always stay with him. She could sleep outside his door, on a velvet cushion.

He had boy's clothes made for her, so that she could ride with him. They rode through the green woods. The green branches touched her shoulders, and the little birds sang in the fresh leaves. She climbed high mountains with him. Her tender feet bled, and the others saw it, but she only laughed. She followed him up, until they could see the clouds under their feet, like a flock of birds flying to far countries.

At night, when everyone else was asleep, she went down the broad marble steps. She cooled her burning feet in the cold sea water. And then she thought of her family in the deep.

One night, her sisters came up, arm in arm. They sang a sad song as they swam on the water. She waved to them. They knew her, and they told her how sad they all were. After that, they came to see her every night. Once, far out at sea, she saw her old grandmother, who had not been on the surface for many years. And she saw the sea king, with his crown on his head. They stretched out their hands to her, but they did not dare to come as near the land as her sisters did.

Every day, she was dearer to the prince. He loved her, but only as you love a sweet child. He never thought of making her his wife. And yet she must become his wife, or she would not get a soul. If he married someone else, she would turn into foam on the sea.
