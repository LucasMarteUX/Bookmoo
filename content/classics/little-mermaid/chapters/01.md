# The Palace Under the Sea

Far out in the ocean, the water is as blue as a flower. It is as clear as glass. And it is very, very deep. It is so deep that no anchor can touch the bottom. Down there, far under the waves, the sea people live.

Do not think that the bottom of the sea is only sand. It is not. There are wonderful trees and flowers down there. Their stems and leaves are so soft that they move with every small wave, as if they were alive. Fish swim between the branches, big and small, like birds in the trees of our world.

In the deepest part of the sea, there is a palace. It is the home of the sea king. The walls are made of red coral. The tall windows are made of yellow amber, which is a shining stone. The roof is made of shells. They open and close with the water, and in each shell there is a bright pearl. One of those pearls is worth a queen's crown.

The sea king was an old man. His wife had died many years before, and he lived with his mother. She was a wise old woman. She was proud of her family, and she wore twelve oysters on her tail. Other ladies could only wear six. She kept the house, and she loved her six granddaughters very much.

The six little princesses were all beautiful. They had long hair and sweet voices. But the youngest was the loveliest of all. Her skin was soft like a rose petal. Her eyes were as blue as the deep sea. Like her sisters, she did not have legs. Her body ended in a long fish tail.

All day, the princesses played in the big halls of the palace. On the walls, living flowers grew. When the sisters opened the big windows, the fish swam in, like birds that fly into our rooms. They came up to the princesses, they ate from their hands, and they let the girls touch them.

Outside the palace, there was a big garden. The trees in it were red and dark blue. Their fruit shone like gold, and their flowers moved like fire. The ground was covered with soft blue sand, and over everything there was a strange blue light. You could think that you were high in the sky, and not at the bottom of the sea. On a quiet day, you could see the sun far above. It looked like a purple flower, with light coming out of its middle.

Each princess had her own little place in the garden. She could dig and plant anything she liked. One princess made her flower bed in the shape of a whale. Another thought that it was nicer to make hers like a little mermaid. But the youngest made hers round, like the sun. She planted only red flowers in it, because they were the color of the sun.

She was a quiet, thoughtful girl. Her sisters liked to put in their gardens all the beautiful things that came from sunken ships. But she wanted nothing but her red flowers and one white marble statue. It was the statue of a handsome boy. It had fallen to the bottom of the sea from a ship that sank. Next to it, she planted a red tree, and its branches hung over the statue like soft arms. In the water, the shadow of the tree moved on the blue sand. It looked as if the top of the tree and the roots were kissing each other.

Nothing made her so happy as to hear about the world of the people above. She made her grandmother tell her everything: about ships and towns, about people and animals. It seemed so wonderful to her that the flowers up there had a sweet smell. The flowers at the bottom of the sea had none. And it seemed strange that the woods were green, and that the "fish" in the trees sang so loudly and sweetly. The grandmother called the birds "fish," because the little princesses had never seen a bird.

"When you are fifteen years old," said the old lady, "you can go up to the top of the sea. You can sit on the rocks in the moonlight, and watch the big ships sail past. Then you will see forests and towns with your own eyes."

The little mermaid listened with all her heart. She wished that she was fifteen already.
