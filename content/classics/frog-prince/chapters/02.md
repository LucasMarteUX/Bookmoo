# The Frog and the Well

When the frog heard her promise, he put his head under the water. He sank down, down, down, into the dark well. The princess waited. She leaned over the edge and looked. She saw bubbles, and then nothing at all.

She waited for a long time. The sun moved in the sky. A bird sang in the lime tree. The princess walked up and down. She said to herself, "He has forgotten. Or perhaps he is only a stupid frog, and he cannot find it. Oh, my ball!"

Then she saw the bubbles again. They came up fast. The water moved. A green head came out of the well, and in his wide mouth the frog held the golden ball.

He swam to the edge, and he threw it on the grass.

The princess ran to it. She picked it up in her two hands. It was wet and shining. She laughed with joy. She had never been so happy to see a toy.

"My ball!" she cried. "My own dear golden ball!" She held it against her chest, and she turned around and around.

"Wait! Wait for me!" said the frog. "Take me with you! I cannot run so fast as you."

But the princess did not listen. She did not even look at him. She picked up her skirt, and she ran. She ran along the forest path as fast as she could, with the ball in her hands, and she did not stop until she was home.

The frog called after her. "Princess! Princess, wait! Take me with you, as you promised!" He croaked as loudly as he could. "Croak, croak!" But the sound went out into the forest, and nobody came. The little frog was left alone by the well, with his wet head just above the water, and his big round eyes full of sadness.

"She does not keep her word," he said. "That is not nice."

He sat there for a long time. The shadows of the trees grew long. The sun went down. And when it was dark, the frog slipped back into the water.

At home, the princess forgot all about the frog. She played with her ball in the garden. She showed it to her sisters, and she told them how she had lost it. She did not tell them about the frog. She did not think that it was important. In the evening, she ate her supper, and she went to bed, and she slept well.

The next day was bright and sunny. The king and his daughters sat down to eat at the long table. The queen's servants brought the food in on golden plates. The youngest princess sat in her place, with her golden plate and her golden cup in front of her. She had just put a piece of bread in her mouth when she heard a noise.

It was a strange noise. It came from the marble stairs outside the room. It went tap, tap, plash, plash. Tap, tap, plash, plash.

The princesses stopped eating. They looked at each other.

"What is that?" said the oldest.

"I do not know," said the second. "It sounds like something wet climbing the stairs."

The noise came nearer. Tap, tap, plash, plash. It stopped at the door of the hall. There was a soft little knock. And then a small voice cried out:

"Youngest princess, my love, open the door for me! Do you not remember what you promised me yesterday, at the cool well in the forest? Youngest daughter of the king, open the door for me!"
