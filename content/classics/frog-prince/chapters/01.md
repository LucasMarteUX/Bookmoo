# The Golden Ball

In the old days, when wishing still helped, there lived a king. He had many beautiful daughters. But the youngest was so lovely that even the sun, which has seen so many things, was surprised every time that it shone on her face.

Near the king's castle, there was a big, dark forest. In the forest, under an old lime tree, there was a well. It was a deep, cool well, and the water was very clear. On hot days, the king's youngest daughter liked to go to the forest. She sat on the edge of the cool well, and she rested.

When she was bored, she took out her favorite toy. It was a golden ball. She threw it high into the air, and she caught it again. She threw it and caught it. She thought that it was the best game in the world.

One warm afternoon, it happened that the golden ball did not fall into the princess's little hand. It touched her fingers, and it fell to the ground. It bounced once, and it rolled away. It rolled across the grass, and it rolled to the edge of the well. And it fell into the water.

The princess jumped up. She ran to the edge, and she looked down. But the well was very deep. She could not see the bottom. She could see only the dark water and her own white face.

She began to cry. She cried louder and louder. She could not stop.

"Oh, my ball, my golden ball!" she said. "I will never find you again. It is my favorite thing in the world. Oh, I would give anything to have it back. I would give my fine clothes. I would give my jewels and my pearls. I would give my golden crown!"

While she was crying, she heard a voice behind her.

"Princess," said the voice, "why are you crying so bitterly? You would move a stone to pity."

The princess looked around. She saw nobody. She looked down at the ground, and she looked up in the tree. At last, she looked at the well. A frog was sitting on the edge, with his big, ugly head out of the water.

He was a fat, green frog. His eyes were big and round. His wide mouth was open, and he looked at her without moving.

"Oh, it is you, old water-splasher," said the princess. She wiped her eyes. She was not afraid of frogs, but she did not like them. "I am crying for my golden ball. It fell into the well."

"Be quiet and do not cry," said the frog. "I can help you. But what will you give me if I bring your toy up again?"

The princess stopped crying. "Whatever you want, dear frog," she said at once. "My clothes, my pearls and jewels, even the golden crown that I wear on my head."

The frog shook his head.

"I do not want your clothes," he said. "I do not want your pearls and jewels. I do not want your golden crown. But if you will love me, I will help you. I want to be your friend and playmate. I want to sit next to you at your little table. I want to eat from your little golden plate, and drink from your little cup. And I want to sleep in your little bed. If you promise me this, I will go down. I will bring you your golden ball."

The princess was very surprised. "What nonsense!" she thought. "What does this silly frog say? He sits in the water with the other frogs and croaks. He cannot be anyone's friend. He cannot even come out of the well to see me."

But she wanted her ball so much. So she said, "Yes, yes, I promise. I will do all that you want, if you only bring me the ball."

But she did not mean it at all. She thought, "He is only a frog. He cannot leave the well."
