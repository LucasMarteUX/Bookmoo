# Three Days and Three Nights

In the morning, when it was light, the frog woke up. He stretched his long green legs. He jumped off the bed and out of the door, and he hopped down the stairs. Tap, tap, plash, plash. He went out of the castle and into the garden. Nobody saw where he went.

The princess woke up in the chair. She looked at the empty bed, and she let out a big sigh. "Thank goodness," she said. "He is gone. Now I will be free of him."

She dressed and went down to breakfast. She was happy. She told her sisters that the frog was gone, and they laughed. For one day, the princess did not think about the frog at all. She played in the garden, and she helped her mother with the flowers. She read a book. She laughed with her sisters.

But when evening came, and the family sat down again at the long table, there was a sound on the marble stairs. Tap, tap, plash, plash.

The princess dropped her spoon.

There was a knock at the door. The same small voice cried:

"Youngest princess, my love,
Open the door for me!
Do you not remember what you said
At the cool well, in the shade?
Youngest daughter of the king,
Open the door for me!"

The princess looked at her father. The king looked back at her, and he did not say anything. So she got up, and she opened the door. The frog hopped in. He went to her chair, and she lifted him up. He ate from her golden plate. He drank from her golden cup. When he was tired, she carried him upstairs, and she put him on her pillow.

He slept there all night. She did not sleep at all. She lay on the hard chair, and she looked at the moon in the window. In the morning, the frog woke, and he jumped down, and he went out as he had before.

On the third evening, the frog came again. There was the same tapping on the stairs. There was the same song at the door. This time, the princess did not argue. She was tired, and she was a little ashamed. She opened the door without a word. She lifted the frog to the table. She let him eat from her plate and drink from her cup, and when he was tired, she carried him to her room.

But this time, she was so angry and so tired that she could hardly think. She was angry at herself. She was angry at the frog. She was angry at the golden ball, and the well, and the whole long day. She looked at the frog on her pillow, and he looked back at her with his big round eyes. He seemed to be smiling.

"I do not like you," she said. "I do not like your cold wet skin. I do not like that you eat from my plate and sleep in my bed. You are a nasty, ugly thing. I will not have you here any longer."

The frog said nothing. He only looked at her.

Suddenly, the princess could not bear it. She took him up in her hand. She was so angry that she did not even feel afraid. She turned around, and with all her strength, she threw him against the wall.

"Now you will have your rest, you nasty frog!" she cried.
