# The Prince in the Bedroom

The frog hit the wall. But he did not fall down as a frog. Something strange happened. There was a flash of light, like a small star. A soft sound filled the room, like the opening of a flower. And when the princess opened her eyes, which she had closed in fear, the frog was gone.

In his place, there was a young man. He was tall and handsome. His hair was brown, and his eyes were kind and gentle, the most beautiful eyes that she had ever seen. He was standing at the foot of her bed, with his hand against the wall, and he was looking at her.

The princess could not speak. She stood still with her hands at her mouth.

"Do not be afraid," said the young man. His voice was soft and low. "I am a prince. A wicked witch put a spell on me. She turned me into a frog, and she said that nobody could free me except you. I had to sit in the well for many years, and nobody came. Then you came, and you cried for your golden ball. I knew that you were the one."

"But how... how did I free you?" said the princess.

"You kept your promise," said the prince. "Three days and three nights, you let me sit at your table and eat from your plate and sleep on your pillow. You did not want to. But you did it. And even though you were angry, you gave me the chance to be free."

The princess blushed. She looked at the floor. "I am sorry," she said. "I was unkind. I did not want to keep my word. I was proud, and I was afraid. I did not think that you were a prince."

"You were not the first to be afraid of me," said the prince, and he smiled. "It is hard to love a frog. I do not blame you. But now I am myself again. And I have a question."

"What is it?"

"My father's kingdom is far away," said the prince. "It is a big country, with green hills and a high castle. Will you come with me? I would like you to be my wife."

The princess looked at him for a long time. She thought of the well in the forest, and the golden ball, and the nights that she had lain awake. She thought of the kind eyes of the frog. "I think that I can say yes," she said.

They went down to the great hall. The king was sitting in his chair, and he was awake. He had been waiting all night. When he saw the young man next to his youngest daughter, he stood up. The prince told him the story. The king listened, and he laughed. He took the prince's hand.

"A frog!" he said. "I did not guess. But I am glad that I made her keep her word."

"So am I," said the princess, and she kissed her father's cheek.

The king agreed to the marriage. All the sisters came down, and they shook the prince's hand. They said that they were sorry for being unkind. The prince said that it did not matter. "I was a very ugly frog," he said.

While they were talking, there was the sound of hooves in the courtyard. A beautiful coach rolled up to the door. It was drawn by eight white horses, and each one had white feathers on its head and a golden harness. Behind the coach stood the prince's servant.

It was faithful Henry.
