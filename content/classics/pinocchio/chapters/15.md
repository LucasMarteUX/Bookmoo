# The Donkey in the Circus

The fat man took Pinocchio to the market and sold him to the owner of a circus. The circus owner paid a good price. "I will teach this donkey to dance and to jump through hoops," he said. "The people will love him."

The circus owner took Pinocchio to a stable. For the first time in months, Pinocchio ate some hay. But it tasted terrible. He spat it out, and he wanted to cry. "I want my bread," he said. But all that came out was, "Hee-haw!"

The owner was angry. He took a long whip and hit him. "Eat!" he shouted. "You will eat hay, or you will eat nothing."

The next day, the lessons began. Every day, Pinocchio had to jump through a hoop. Every day, he fell. He hurt his legs and his back. The owner hit him with the whip when he made a mistake. Soon he was very sad and very tired. "If only I had listened to the Fairy," he thought. "I would be at home now."

After many weeks, he learned to dance. He could jump through a circle of fire, and he could stand on two legs. The owner was happy. He put up posters in the town. "COME AND SEE PINOCCHIO, THE DANCING DONKEY!"

On the night of the show, the circus was full. The lights were bright, and the band played loud music. Pinocchio came in, wearing a hat and a little coat. He danced, and the people clapped. He jumped through the hoops, and they cheered.

Suddenly, he looked up at the seats. In a box, in the front row, there sat a beautiful woman with blue hair. She looked at him with sad eyes. It was the Fairy!

Pinocchio wanted to call her name. But he could not. He wanted to run to her. But he forgot to watch his step. He slipped on the ring, and his leg broke. He fell with a loud cry.

The audience screamed. The owner ran in. He saw the broken leg, and he said, "This donkey is no good to me now. He cannot dance. Take him to the butcher, and sell his skin. We will make a drum from it."

They sold Pinocchio to a man who wanted a drum skin. The man took him to the edge of the sea. "I will drown him," he said. "When he is dead, I will take the skin." He tied a heavy stone to his neck and pushed him into the water.

Pinocchio sank, down and down into the cold dark sea. He thought about his father. He thought about the Fairy. "I am going to die," he thought. "I am sorry for everything."

But the Fairy did not want him to die. She sent a great number of little fish to eat the donkey skin. They ate it all, in half an hour. And when they finished, there was no donkey left. There was only a wooden puppet, with his strings and his joints, as he had been at the beginning.

Pinocchio was free of the stone. He swam up to the top of the water, and he laughed for joy. "I am a puppet again!" he cried.

He began to swim as fast as he could. He wanted to find land. But he saw something terrible in the water. A great monster was coming toward him. It was as big as a ship. It had a huge mouth with three rows of teeth. It was the Terrible Shark.

Pinocchio tried to swim away. But the shark opened its mouth, and the water rushed in. A strong current pulled him into the dark.

He fell down, down, down a long slippery tunnel. At last, he landed on something soft and wet. It was dark all around. He could hear the sound of water far away. There was a smell of fish and of salt. "Where am I?" he said. He sat up, and he began to cry. "I am inside the shark. I will never see my father again."
