# The Land of Toys

When Pinocchio came home, the Fairy was waiting. She was not angry. She put her hand on his cheek and said, "You are a good boy, Pinocchio. You made a mistake, but you are sorry. Tomorrow, something wonderful will happen. You will not be a puppet any more. You will become a real boy. Invite all your friends to a big party. I will make a lot of food."

Pinocchio was so happy that he ran to tell his friends. He ran from house to house, and he said, "Come to my party tomorrow!" Everyone said yes.

Last of all, he went to see his best friend. The boy's real name was Romeo, but all the children called him Candlewick, because he was as thin as a candle. He was the laziest boy in the school. He was also the worst. But Pinocchio loved him.

Pinocchio looked for him in his house, but Candlewick was not there. He looked in the school, and in the square, but he did not find him. At last, he found him behind a barn. He was sitting on a box, and he looked sad.

"What are you doing here?" said Pinocchio.

"I am waiting for midnight," said Candlewick. "I am leaving this town."

"Where are you going?"

"To the best country in the world, the Land of Toys. There are no schools there, and there are no teachers. There are no books. You play from morning to night. It is wonderful. A cart comes at midnight to take the boys there."

"But I cannot," said Pinocchio. "Tomorrow I will become a real boy. The Fairy promised."

"A real boy!" said Candlewick, and he laughed. "Who wants to be a real boy? Real boys must study and work. In the Land of Toys, you can be a child forever. Come with me. Play for just one week, and then you can come back."

Pinocchio did not know what to say. He wanted to be a real boy. But the Land of Toys sounded so nice. "Only for one week," he said at last. "I will tell the Fairy that I went for a short trip."

At midnight, a big cart arrived. It was pulled by twelve pairs of donkeys, all of different colors. The donkeys wore leather shoes. The driver was a small, fat man with a round pink face, and he smiled all the time. His voice was sweet as honey.

The cart was full of boys. They were all between eight and twelve years old. They were packed together like fish in a box, and they were all happy. They sang and shouted and laughed. The cart went through the dark night. The donkeys did not speak, but sometimes they looked sadly at the boys.

"Why are the donkeys so sad?" Pinocchio asked.

"They are tired," said the driver. "They work hard."

In the morning, they came to the Land of Toys. It was a wonderful place. There were no schools and no teachers. The streets were full of children. They were playing and laughing and shouting. There were games in every square, and toys in every shop. A boy could play with a ball in the morning, and with a kite in the afternoon, and with drums and trumpets in the evening. Nobody ever said, "It is time to go to bed."

Pinocchio and Candlewick were happier than ever. They played all day and all night. They ate sweets and cakes, and they jumped and ran. Weeks passed. Then months passed. For five whole months, Pinocchio never opened a book, and he never thought about school.

One morning, Pinocchio woke up, and he felt something strange. His head hurt. He put his hands on his ears, and he found that they were very long. They were longer than before, and they were covered with soft grey hair.

"Oh, no!" he cried. He ran to a mirror. His ears were the ears of a donkey!

He ran to Candlewick's room. His friend was in bed, with a blanket over his head. "Candlewick," said Pinocchio, crying. "Look at my ears!"

"Look at mine," said Candlewick, in a faint voice. He took off the blanket. His ears were also big and grey.

They began to cry. But as they cried, their cries changed. Instead of crying, they began to bray: "Hee-haw! Hee-haw!" Their hands and feet turned into hooves, and their faces became long and grey. In a few minutes, they were not boys any more. They were two donkeys.

The fat little man came into the room. He was not smiling now. He looked at the two donkeys, and he laughed. "That is what happens to boys who do not study," he said. "You wanted to be lazy. Now you are a donkey. Well, I will buy and sell you. I have a good business."

He put a rope around Pinocchio's neck and took him out of the room.
