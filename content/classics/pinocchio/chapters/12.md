# The Pigeon and the Sea

Pinocchio ran to the place where the Fairy's small white house had been. But the house was gone. In its place, there was a small stone. On the stone, these words were written:

HERE LIES THE GIRL WITH BLUE HAIR,
WHO DIED OF SADNESS
BECAUSE HER LITTLE BROTHER LEFT HER.

Pinocchio read the words slowly, letter by letter. Then he fell on his face and kissed the stone. He cried all day and all night. "Oh, Fairy, my Fairy," he said. "Why did you not live? I was the bad one. I should have died, not you! And where is my poor father?"

Suddenly, a big pigeon flew down from the sky and sat near him. "Young man," said the pigeon, "I saw your father. I know where he is."

"Where? Tell me!"

"He is on the coast, near the sea. He built a little boat. He wants to cross the ocean, because he thinks that you went to the New World. He has looked for you for four months. He walked to every town, and he did not find you."

"How far is it?"

"More than a thousand miles. But I can carry you there on my back. Would you like to come?"

Pinocchio did not wait. He jumped on the pigeon's back, and they flew all day and all night. In the morning, they came to the sea. The water was grey and rough, with big, white waves. On the beach, a crowd was looking at something far away.

"What is happening?" asked Pinocchio.

"There is a poor old man in a small boat," said a woman. "He is going out to sea. The waves are very high. He will drown."

Pinocchio looked, and he knew the old man at once. It was Geppetto. "Father!" he shouted. "Father, I am here!"

But the wind was loud, and Geppetto did not hear. A big wave came. It turned the boat over. The old man disappeared. The people on the beach shouted and cried. "He is dead!"

Pinocchio did not think. He jumped into the sea. He was made of wood, so he floated like a cork. He swam strongly. He swam for many hours. But the waves were so big that he could not see anything. At last, a giant wave threw him up on a beach.

He was on an island. He sat on the sand, and he looked at his hands and his clothes. He was wet and tired, but he was alive. "Where am I?" he said.

A dolphin put his head out of the water near him. "Hello, young man," he said. "This is the Island of the Busy Bees. Do you want to see the town?"

"Yes, please," said Pinocchio. "And do you know about an old man in a little boat?"

"I do," said the dolphin sadly. "A great shark came and ate him. A big, terrible shark. It is the biggest in the sea. It lives near here. People call it the Terrible Shark."

Pinocchio was very afraid. He began to cry again. But he walked toward the town. The people were all working. Everyone was busy. They carried baskets and made shoes and built houses. Nobody was lazy. When Pinocchio asked for bread, they said, "First you must work. Then you can eat."

He was so hungry that he worked all day. He carried water for an old woman. She gave him a piece of bread and a cup of milk. At night, she said, "I have a cottage up on the hill. Come. You can sleep there."

On the hill, there was a small white house. In the door, a woman was waiting. She had long blue hair. It was the Fairy! She was alive! She was not a girl now, but a grown woman, beautiful and kind.

"Fairy!" cried Pinocchio, and he fell at her feet. "I thought that you were dead!"

"I was only sleeping," she said. "I felt very sad. But now I am happy. You are here, and you are a good puppet. You worked all day for an old woman. You are growing."

"I am sorry for everything," said Pinocchio. "I want to be good. I want to study, and I want to help my father."

"I believe you," said the Fairy. "I will give you a chance. If you are good, if you go to school every day, and if you obey me, then you will become a real boy."

"A real boy?"

"Yes. It will take time. But I promise. Tomorrow, you will go to school."
