# Pinocchio Gets a Face

First, Geppetto carved the hair. Then he carved the forehead. And then he carved the eyes.

When the eyes were ready, they moved. They turned in their wooden holes, and they looked straight at him. Geppetto felt strange. "Why are you looking at me?" he asked. Nobody answered.

He carved the nose. But as soon as it was finished, the nose began to grow. It grew and grew. It became longer every minute. Poor Geppetto cut it, and he cut it again. The more he cut, the longer it grew. It looked like a long wooden finger.

Next, he carved the mouth. Before he finished, the mouth began to laugh. It laughed at his old clothes, and it laughed at his wig.

"Stop laughing!" said Geppetto, angrily.

The mouth stopped. But it put out its tongue as far as it could.

Geppetto did not want to ruin his work, so he pretended not to see. He carved the chin, the neck, the shoulders, the stomach, the arms and the hands. The hands were hardly finished when they jumped up and took the yellow wig from the old man's head. Pinocchio put it on his own head. It was so big that it covered his whole face.

"You rude boy!" said Geppetto. His voice was shaking. "You are not even ready, and you already laugh at your father. That is a very bad start, my son." A tear ran down his nose.

Only the legs and feet were left. When Geppetto finished them, a foot kicked him on the nose.

"I should have expected that," he said, sadly. "It is too late now."

He took the puppet in his arms and put him on the floor to teach him to walk. Pinocchio's legs were stiff, and he could not move. Geppetto held his hand and showed him how to put one foot in front of the other. Soon Pinocchio could walk alone. Then he began to run. He ran around the room once, twice, three times. Then he saw the door. It was open. In a second, he was out in the street, and he ran away as fast as a hare.

"Catch him! Stop him!" cried Geppetto. He ran after the puppet, but he was old and slow. The people in the street only laughed. They saw a wooden boy running like a racehorse.

At last, a policeman heard the noise. He thought that a young horse had escaped from its owner. He stood in the middle of the road with his legs wide open. He wanted to stop the runaway.

Pinocchio saw the policeman from far away. He tried to run between the policeman's legs. It was a good idea, but it did not work. The policeman caught him by the nose. It was so long that it was easy to catch. He gave the puppet back to Geppetto.

Geppetto wanted to pull Pinocchio's ears. But he could not. He had forgotten to carve any ears. "Come home," he said. "We will talk about this at home."

"I will not go!" said Pinocchio. He threw himself on the ground. A crowd gathered around them.

"Poor puppet," said one man. "He is right. Geppetto will beat him."

"Geppetto looks kind," said a woman. "But he is cruel to children."

The people said such things about Geppetto that, in the end, the policeman let Pinocchio go. He took Geppetto to the police station instead. The old man could not explain. He cried like a child all the way. "My own son!" he said. "I worked so hard to make him a good boy. But it is my fault. I did not think."

Meanwhile, Pinocchio was free. He ran across the fields as fast as a bird. He jumped over ditches and bushes, and he did not stop until he was in front of his father's house. The door was still open. He went in, and he shut the door behind him. Then he sat on the floor with a big sigh of joy.

But his joy did not last long.

"Cri-cri-cri," said a small voice in the room.

Pinocchio jumped. "Who is there?" he asked, very afraid.

"It is I."
