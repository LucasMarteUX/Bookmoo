# The Field of Miracles

When Pinocchio was well again, he wanted to go home to his father. The Fairy was happy about this idea. "Go to him," she said. "But take the road through the wood, and do not stop. Your father is waiting for you."

Pinocchio kissed her and thanked her. He walked into the wood, and he sang a little song. But after a mile, he met the Fox and the Cat. They looked thin and dirty. The Fox did not limp, and the Cat was not blind now. They were two bad actors who had stopped playing their parts.

"Pinocchio, my dear friend!" cried the Fox. "How lucky to meet you! What are you doing here?"

"I am going home. I have been in trouble because of you!"

"Because of us? Never! We were so worried about you. We looked for you everywhere. We were sure that the thieves had killed you."

"You left me alone at the inn."

"We went away because of an urgent message," said the Cat. "But now we have good news. Do you remember the Field of Miracles? Tonight is the best night to go. It is only a short walk from here."

"But I have only four coins," said Pinocchio. "I gave one to the innkeeper."

"Four is enough," said the Fox. "You will plant them, and in the morning, you will have a tree full of gold. You will be rich in one night!"

Pinocchio remembered the Fairy and the Cricket and his father. But the idea of gold was so beautiful. And the Fox spoke so well. In the end, he said, "All right. I will go. But just this once, and then I will go home."

They walked all day, and in the evening, they came to a field. It was not a beautiful field. There were no trees and no flowers. There was only dry, yellow earth, and a few old sticks.

"Here it is," said the Fox. "The Field of Miracles. Dig a small hole and put the coins in it. Cover them with earth and pour some water on top. Then go and sleep for twenty minutes. When you come back, you will find a beautiful tree."

Pinocchio did exactly as the Fox said. He dug a hole with his hands. He put in the four coins, and he covered them. He brought water from a stream in his cap. Then he said goodbye to the Fox and the Cat, who said, "We will see you soon." They went away, laughing quietly.

After twenty minutes, Pinocchio came back, and his heart was beating fast. He looked at the place. There was nothing. There was no tree, and there was no gold. He dug in the earth with his hands. The hole was empty.

A Parrot sat on a branch nearby. He had watched everything, and he laughed loudly. "Ha, ha, ha! You are a silly puppet! The Fox and the Cat came back as soon as you left. They took your coins, and they ran away. They are far from here now. Ha, ha, ha!"

Pinocchio ran to the town to tell the judge. He walked into a big stone building. The judge was a very old gorilla. He had white hair and gold glasses. He listened to Pinocchio's story, and he looked very sad.

"Poor boy," he said. "I am sorry for you. Policeman, take this thief to prison."

"I am not a thief!" cried Pinocchio. "I am the one who was robbed!"

"I know," said the judge gently. "But in this town, the law is made for the clever, and not for the foolish. A foolish person who loses his money must be punished. So you will spend four months in prison. That will teach you."

Pinocchio could not speak. Two big policemen, both dogs, took him by the arms. They led him to a small, dark room. Heavy iron doors closed behind him.

He stayed in prison for four long months. He was hungry and cold, and he did not have even a window. He thought about his father every day. He thought about the Fairy, and about the Talking Cricket, whom he had killed. "If only I had listened," he said again and again.

One day, the whole town was happy. The king had won a great battle, and he wanted to show his joy. He freed all the prisoners. The policemen opened the doors one by one, and one by one, the prisoners walked out into the sun. Pinocchio ran to the door too. But the policeman said, "No, you must stay. You are not a real prisoner. You are a poor puppet who lost his money."

"But I am a prisoner too," said Pinocchio.

"Not a real one," said the policeman. But the jailer laughed. "A thief is a thief," he said. "Go on, boy! Go home!"

Pinocchio was free. He ran out of the prison and took the road toward the Fairy's house.
