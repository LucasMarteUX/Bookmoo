# The Farmer's Watchdog

Pinocchio walked and walked, but he was in a bad mood. He was thin, and he was hungry. "Which way is the Fairy's house?" he said to himself. "Was it to the left, or to the right?"

The road was long. Every time that he came to a crossing, he stopped and looked at the signs. But there were no signs. After a while, he saw something in the middle of the road. It was a very large snake, with green skin and a head like a hill. His eyes were red as fire, and his tail smoked like a chimney.

Pinocchio was so afraid that he ran away. He ran across a field, and he did not look back. But he could hear the snake coming after him.

At last, he stopped to rest at a little stream. Suddenly, the snake was in front of him. It was moving very slowly. It stopped to look at him. Then it began to laugh, and it laughed so hard that a blood vessel broke in its chest. The snake died there, on the ground. Pinocchio did not know why it laughed. He only knew that he was safe.

He came to a farm. It was already evening, and he was very hungry. He saw a vine with ripe white grapes. "Just a few," he said. "Nobody will notice." He jumped up and took a bunch.

At that moment, he felt something close tight around his leg. It was a trap, made of iron, for catching animals. Four sharp teeth went into his leg. He screamed, but nobody came. Soon it was night.

A farmer came out of the house with a lamp. "Aha!" he said. "So it was you who took my chickens every night! Now I have you."

But when he saw the wooden puppet in his trap, he laughed. "What a funny thief," he said. "Well, I have no use for a puppet. But I need a good watchdog. My old dog Melampo died yesterday. You can take his place. Come with me."

He put a collar around Pinocchio's neck. It was a big collar made of leather, with sharp points. He led him to a small house near the chicken yard. "You will sleep here," said the farmer. "If thieves come at night, you must bark. If you do not, I will cut you into small pieces and throw you on the fire."

Pinocchio was very sad. But there was nothing to do. He lay down in the small house, and he tried to sleep.

At midnight, he heard a noise. He looked out. Four small animals with bright eyes were walking slowly toward the chicken house. They were weasels. They were so thin that they looked like long shadows.

"Look," said one in a very low voice. "It is a new dog. Not Melampo."

"We know Melampo's smell," said another.

Pinocchio heard them. He was a good puppet at heart, and he felt a little angry. He wanted to bark. But then one of the weasels came to him. "Dear dog," he said, in a sweet voice, "we are poor and hungry. We only want to take a few chickens. If you stay quiet, we will give you half. You can have the best ones. What do you say?"

"No," said Pinocchio. "I cannot do that. The farmer trusts me."

The weasels laughed. "You are a poor, silly dog," they said. "Do you think that he loves you? He will throw you away when you are old."

But Pinocchio was thinking about his father. He thought about Geppetto, who had sold his coat. He thought about the Fairy, who had saved him. He did not want to be a bad boy any more. When the weasels went into the chicken house, he did not move. He closed the door of the house behind them with his paw. Then he began to bark, as loudly as he could.

"Woof! Woof! Thieves! Thieves!"

The farmer ran out with a stick. He caught the four weasels in the chicken house. He put them in a big bag, and he took them away.

In the morning, he came back. He took off the collar from Pinocchio's neck. "You are the best watchdog that I ever had," he said. "You saved my chickens. Thank you. You are free. You can go."

Pinocchio thanked him. He ran along the road with the happiest heart in the world. "I did something good," he said. "I did something good!"
