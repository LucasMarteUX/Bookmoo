# Inside the Shark

At first, Pinocchio could see nothing. It was dark. The air was thick and wet. But after a while, he saw a weak light far away. It came from a very small candle, and it was burning on a table. And next to the table, there sat an old man.

His hair was white, and his beard was long. He wore an old shirt. He was very thin. He looked up, and his eyes grew wide.

"Pinocchio?" he whispered.

"Father!" cried Pinocchio.

They ran to each other. They held each other for a long time, and they cried and laughed. Geppetto kissed his son's wooden head again and again. "My dear boy! My dear, dear boy! I thought that I would never see you again. I thought that you were dead."

"I am here, Father," said Pinocchio. "I am so sorry. I was a bad son. I left you. I did not listen to you. But now I know my mistakes."

"We will talk about it later," said Geppetto. "Tell me, how did you come here?"

Pinocchio told him everything. When he finished, Geppetto looked very serious. "We have been in this shark for two years," he said. "It swallowed my boat. It also swallowed a big ship, and I live on the food in the ship. I have a table and a candle. But the food and the candle are nearly finished."

"We must escape," said Pinocchio.

"How? The shark is so big."

"Look. The shark is old. He sleeps with his mouth open. When he sleeps, we can walk up his throat and swim out."

"But I cannot swim," said Geppetto sadly. "I am too old and too tired."

"I will carry you on my back," said Pinocchio. "I am made of wood. I can swim for hours."

They waited until the middle of the night. The shark was asleep, and his mouth was wide open. Pinocchio took the candle in one hand and his father's arm in the other. They walked up the throat of the shark, slowly, and quietly. When they reached the teeth, they stopped. The shark was snoring. His breath was cold and wet.

They went between the teeth, one step at a time. Then they came to the edge of the mouth. Outside, there was only black sea and a sky full of stars.

"I am afraid," said Geppetto.

"Do not be afraid, Father," said Pinocchio. "Put your arms around my neck."

He jumped into the water. He swam with strong, quick strokes. The old man held on to his neck. Behind them, the shark woke up, and he began to follow. But Pinocchio swam faster than the wind.

After a long time, Pinocchio was very tired. His arms were heavy, and his legs hurt. Geppetto was cold and wet and weak. "I cannot go on," said Pinocchio. "I am too tired."

Suddenly, a big tuna came up beside them. "Climb on my back," he said. "I will carry you. I know you. You saved my cousin from a net." They climbed up on the tuna's back, and he swam to the shore. When they reached the beach, the sun was coming up.

On the shore, Pinocchio helped his father to walk. They found a small path, and they went along it. Soon they met a cat and a fox on the path. They were thin and dirty, and they were begging. The Fox had really lost a leg, and the Cat had really lost her eyes. "Please," they said, "give us a little money."

Pinocchio did not stop. "I have no money," he said. "And I do not give to thieves." They walked on.

At last, they came to a small farm. A farmer let them sleep in his barn. Pinocchio worked for him every day, to pay for food. He carried water and brought wood. He worked until his hands hurt. In the evenings, he went to school. In the nights, he made baskets. He sold them, and he gave the money to his father.

Geppetto was very ill, and Pinocchio took care of him. He cooked for him and brought him water. He sang to him when he could not sleep. He gave him every coin that he earned. He never played. He never ran away. He never told lies.

One night, he had a dream. The Fairy came, and she was very beautiful. She kissed his forehead. "Pinocchio," she said, "you are a good boy. You have a good heart. You helped your father when he was sick. You worked and studied. I forgive you for every mistake. Now you will be happy."

In the morning, Pinocchio woke up. He felt strange. He looked at himself. His hands were soft and warm. His legs were round. He had hair and ears. He ran to the mirror. A boy was looking at him, a real boy, with brown eyes and a happy face.

"I am not a puppet!" he cried. "I am a real boy!"

He ran to his father. Geppetto was sitting in his chair, and he was not ill any more. He was strong and happy, and he had a new workshop and a new coat. "Pinocchio!" he said. "My dear son!"

"Father, look! I am a boy! Where is the wooden puppet?"

"There," said Geppetto, and he pointed to a chair. A puppet was sitting on it. It was made of wood. Its head was on one side, and its arms were loose.

Pinocchio looked at it. He felt a little sad, but he felt very happy too. "How ridiculous I was!" he said. "I am so glad that I am a real boy at last."

From that day, Pinocchio and Geppetto lived together in a warm house. They had enough money, and they had enough food. And Pinocchio was a good and happy boy for the rest of his life.
