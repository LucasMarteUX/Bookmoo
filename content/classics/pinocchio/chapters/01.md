# A Log That Said "Ouch"

Long ago, in a small town in Italy, there lived an old carpenter. People called him Master Cherry. They did not use his real name, because his nose was so round, shiny and red that it looked like a cherry.

One morning, a log was lying in his workshop. It was not a fine log. It had no pretty color, and it was not very big. It was only a plain piece of firewood. But Master Cherry was happy to see it. "I can use this for a table leg," he said to himself. He picked up his sharp axe and lifted it over his head.

Then he heard a voice.

"Please, not so hard!" said a tiny, thin voice.

The carpenter stopped. His arm stayed in the air. He looked around his shop. He looked under the bench, and he looked in the cupboard. He looked in the box of wood shavings and sawdust. Then he opened the door and looked up and down the street. There was nobody.

"I see," he said, and he scratched his head. His wig moved a little. "I only imagined it. Back to work."

He lifted the axe again, and he gave the log a big hit.

"Ouch! That hurts!" cried the same little voice.

This time, Master Cherry sat down on the floor. His mouth was open, and his eyes were as round as coins. The tip of his red nose turned blue.

At that moment, someone knocked on the door.

A thin old man came in. His name was Geppetto, but all the children in the town called him Polenta. Polenta is a yellow corn porridge, and Geppetto's yellow wig looked just like it. When people called him that, he became as angry as a wasp.

"Good morning, Master Cherry," said Geppetto. "Why are you sitting on the floor?"

"I am teaching arithmetic to the ants," said the carpenter. He got up slowly. "And what can I do for you, old friend?"

"I have a plan," said Geppetto. His eyes were bright. "I want to make a puppet out of wood. It will be a wonderful puppet, and it will dance and jump and turn over in the air. I will take it from town to town and earn a little bread and wine. What do you think?"

"Wonderful, Polenta!" said the little voice. Nobody could see where it came from.

Geppetto turned red, from his wig to his chin. "Why are you laughing at me?" he shouted.

"I am not laughing at you!"

"You called me Polenta!"

"It was not me!"

"It was you!"

"No!"

"Yes!"

They shouted louder and louder. Then they began to push each other, and soon they were fighting like two cats. In the end, Geppetto was holding the carpenter's grey wig, and the carpenter was holding Geppetto's yellow wig in his teeth.

"Give me my wig!" said Master Cherry.

"Give me mine!" said Geppetto. "And let us be friends again."

They gave back the wigs, and they shook hands. They promised to be friends for the rest of their lives.

"Well, Geppetto," said the carpenter, to show that all was well, "what do you want from me?"

"A piece of wood for my puppet. Do you have one?"

Master Cherry was very happy. He went to his bench and took the strange log that had frightened him so much. But when he tried to give it to his friend, the log jumped by itself. It hit poor Geppetto hard on the shin.

"Is this how you give presents, Master Cherry?" said Geppetto, holding his leg. "You almost broke my bone!"

"I swear that I did not do it!"

"Then the wood did it?"

"Yes, the wood," said the carpenter. But he did not say that the wood could speak. He only wanted to get rid of it.

Geppetto put the log under his arm. He thanked his friend, and he walked home with small, quick steps. He was thinking about his puppet. He did not know yet that this was the most unusual piece of wood in the world.

His house was very poor. It was one small room on the ground floor, with a window under the stairs. There was a table, an old chair and a bed. A fireplace was painted on the wall. In the painted fireplace, a painted fire was burning, and a painted pot was boiling on top. It looked so real that you almost wanted to put out your hands to get warm. But the room was cold, and the old man's stomach was often empty.

He sat down at the table, and he put the log in front of him. He took out his tools. "What name will I give my puppet?" he said. "I will call him Pinocchio. It is a lucky name. I once knew a whole family called Pinocchio. The father was Pine Nut, the mother was Pine Cone, and the children were all Pinocchios. They were poor, but they were always happy."

And with that, he began to work.
