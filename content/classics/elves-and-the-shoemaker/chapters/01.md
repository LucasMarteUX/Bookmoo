# The Poor Shoemaker

Once upon a time, there was a shoemaker. He lived in a small town, in a little house with a green door. In the front room of the house, there was a shop. It had a small window, a wooden bench, and a shelf full of old tools. It was a poor shop, but it was a clean and tidy one. The shoemaker worked there every day, from early morning until late at night.

He was a kind and honest man. He was not lazy, and he did not waste his money. He was good at his work, and he made strong shoes that lasted for many years. But he had bad luck. In the town, there were other shoemakers with bigger shops, and people went to them. His shop was in a quiet street, and few customers came.

His wife helped him as much as she could. She kept the house clean. She cooked simple meals of bread and soup. She mended their clothes, and she sat by his side in the evenings while he worked. She never complained. But each month, there was a little less money, and they did not know what to do.

One cold evening in the autumn, the shoemaker sat on his bench. He looked at the table. He had cut up the last of his leather, and there was only a little bit left. It was just enough for one pair of shoes. He picked it up, and he turned it over in his hands.

"Wife," he said, and his voice was heavy, "this is all that I have. I have no more money to buy more leather. If I cannot sell these shoes, we will have nothing."

"We will manage," said his wife. She put her hand on his shoulder. "You have always done your best. It will be all right."

"I hope so," said the shoemaker. He sighed. "But tonight, I am too tired to work. It is already late. I will cut the leather, and I will make the shoes tomorrow, when I am rested."

He took his big scissors. He cut the leather carefully. He cut the soles, and the sides, and the little straps. He laid the pieces on the bench, in the right order, with the needle and thread next to them. He looked at them for a moment.

"There," he said. "Tomorrow morning, I will begin early."

He said his prayers and went to bed. His wife was already asleep. He lay in the dark and listened to the clock tick. It was a long time before he slept, because he was worried. But at last, his eyes closed.

In the morning, the sun came through the window. The shoemaker woke up early. He washed his face, and he put on his apron. He went downstairs to the shop, and he stopped at the door.

On the bench, there was a pair of shoes.

He rubbed his eyes. He looked again. It was not his leather. It was not the pieces that he had cut. They were finished shoes, made of soft brown leather, with strong soles and tiny, perfect stitches. They stood side by side on the wood. They shone in the morning light.

"Wife!" he called. "Wife, come quickly!"

His wife ran down the stairs in her nightgown. She saw the shoes, and she put her hand over her mouth.

"What is this?" she said.

"I do not know," said the shoemaker. He picked up one shoe, and he turned it round. He looked at the stitches. They were so small that he could hardly see them. He had never made such fine work in his whole life. "Somebody has made them. And I have not the faintest idea who."

"It is not possible," said his wife. "But look at them. They are beautiful."

The shoemaker put the shoes in the window. He did not know what else to do. He waited for the customers.
