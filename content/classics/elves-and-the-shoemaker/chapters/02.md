# Shoes in the Morning

Before noon, a man came into the shop. He was a tall man in a good coat, and he had a kind face. He saw the shoes in the window, and he stopped. He came in and picked them up.

"What fine shoes!" he said. He turned them over and looked at the soles. "I have never seen such good work. How much are they?"

The shoemaker told him the price. It was a fair price, but it was not high.

The man shook his head. "It is too little," he said. "These shoes are worth much more. I will give you double." And he took out his purse and counted out the coins. The shoemaker could hardly believe it. He took the money in both hands. The man tried on the shoes, and they fit as if they were made for him. He wore them out of the shop, with a smile.

The shoemaker stood in the middle of his shop with the gold in his hands. He turned to his wife, who stood in the doorway. "Look!" he said. "Look at this! It is enough to buy leather for two pairs of shoes."

His wife kissed him. "It is a miracle," she said.

That afternoon, the shoemaker went to the market. He bought good leather, the best he could find. He bought enough for two pairs of shoes. He carried it home under his arm, and he put it on the bench. He cut it carefully, in the evening, as he had done the night before. He laid the pieces out in a row. This time, he did not feel so afraid.

"I will make them in the morning," he said to his wife. "Or perhaps they will make themselves."

He said it as a joke. But he lay awake for a long time that night, and he listened. He heard nothing but the wind and the clock. At last, he slept.

In the morning, when he came into the shop, there were two pairs of shoes on the bench. They stood neatly in two rows. They were as fine as the first. Every stitch was perfect, and the leather shone.

"Two pairs!" said the shoemaker. He clapped his hands. "Wife, look! There are two pairs!"

That day, two customers came, one after the other. They bought the shoes at once. Each of them paid more than the price. The shoemaker had enough money to buy leather for four pairs. He went to the market, and he came home with a big roll of leather. In the evening, he cut it into four sets of pieces. He put them on the bench, and he went to bed.

In the morning, there were four pairs of shoes.

So it went on. Every evening, the shoemaker cut out the leather. Every morning, he found the shoes. They were always ready, and they were always perfect. The people of the town began to talk. "Have you seen the shoes in the little shop at the end of the street?" they said. "They are the best in the whole country!" People came from the next town, and from villages far away. They wanted the shoemaker's shoes. The shop was full of customers. The shoemaker could not make enough pairs to meet their needs.

He was no longer poor. He bought a new coat for himself and a warm shawl for his wife. He put a new sign above the door. He mended the roof, and he bought a fat goose for their dinner. The little house with the green door was now one of the happiest houses in the town.

But the shoemaker was still puzzled. Every night, he thought about the shoes. He lay in his bed, and he asked himself, "Who is helping me? And why?"

He could not sleep well. He felt that it was not right to take so much without saying thank you. He wanted to see who the secret helper was.

Weeks went by. Autumn became winter. The days were short, and the nights were long. It was nearly Christmas. The streets were covered in snow, and the windows of the town were full of lights.

One evening, the shoemaker finished cutting out the leather. He put the pieces on the bench. He sat down by the fire, and his wife sat beside him. She looked at him with a thoughtful face.

"Husband," she said, "I have an idea."
