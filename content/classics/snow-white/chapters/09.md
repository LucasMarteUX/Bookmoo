# The Third Visit: The Apple

In the castle, the queen stood in front of her mirror and heard the same answer for the third time. She did not scream now. She was cold and quiet. Her hands did not shake. She had a plan.

"Snow White shall die," she said, "if it is the last thing I do."

She went back to the secret room. This time, she worked for three days and three nights. She did not eat or sleep. She made the most dangerous poison of all. It was a deadly poison, and nothing could stop it. No dwarf, no doctor, and no magic could save a person who tasted it.

Then she took a big red apple from the tree. It was round and shiny and perfect. She put the poison into one half of the apple. She did not touch the other half. The white half was good. The red half was deadly. It looked very delicious, and it was impossible to see the difference.

"This one will not fail," said the queen. She held the apple up and turned it in her hand. "Nobody can say no to this."

She dressed as a farmer's wife. She wore a plain brown dress and a white apron, and she covered her hair with a cloth. Her face was kind and round. She put the apple in a basket with other apples, and she set out on her third journey.

When she knocked on the door of the little house, Snow White was at the window. She did not open it. She put out her head only a little.

"I cannot let anyone in," she said. "The dwarfs told me. And I cannot take anything from you."

"That is all right," said the woman, and she smiled warmly. "I am only a poor farmer's wife. I am selling my apples. They are the best apples in the country. Would you like one? I will give it to you for nothing."

"No, thank you," said Snow White. "I am not allowed."

"Why not?" said the woman. She laughed. "Are you afraid that I will hurt you? You silly girl. Look, I will show you."

She took the red apple out of the basket. She cut it in two halves with a knife.

"Here," she said. "You eat the red half, and I will eat the white half. Then you can see that it is good."

Snow White looked at the apple. It looked so fresh. The juice was running down the knife. She had not eaten fruit for many days. She was so hungry for something sweet. And the woman was eating her half! She bit into the white part, and she chewed, and nothing happened.

The queen had made the apple in a clever way. The poison was only in the red half, and the white half was safe. She ate her piece with a smile.

"You see?" she said. "It is only an apple."

Snow White could not wait any longer. She put out her hand. She took the red half of the apple from the woman, and she put it in her mouth.

She did not even finish the first bite. A cold feeling went through her body. She opened her mouth, and she could not breathe. The piece of apple stayed between her lips, and she fell down on the floor. She did not move.

The queen stood and looked in through the window. She watched for a long time, and she saw that this time, the girl did not breathe.

"White as snow," she said. "Red as blood. Black as ebony. This time, nothing can help you."

Then she laughed, and the sound of it went out across the quiet hills.

She went home at once, and she ran up the stairs. She stood in front of her mirror, and she said, with a shaking voice:

"Mirror, mirror, on the wall,
Who is the most beautiful of all?"

And at last, the mirror said:

"You, my queen, are the most beautiful of all."

The queen's wicked heart was happy. It could be as happy as a heart like that can be.

That evening, the dwarfs came home. They saw the open window, and they began to cry out. They ran inside, and they found Snow White on the floor. Her face was white. Her lips did not move. No breath came from her.

They lifted her gently. They looked for a lace, but there was no lace. They looked for a comb, but there was no comb. They washed her face with water and with wine. They combed her hair. They spoke to her and begged her to wake. But nothing helped.

"She is dead," said the seventh dwarf. His voice was broken.

The seven little men sat down on the floor around her, and they cried for a long, long time.
