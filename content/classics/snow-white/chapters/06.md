# The Mirror Tells the Truth

Back at the castle, the queen was very happy. She had eaten the meat that the cook prepared, and she believed that Snow White was dead. For many days, she walked in the halls with her head high. She wore her best dresses, and she smiled at everyone she met. The servants were afraid of her smile.

One morning, she stood in front of her mirror, and she asked it her old question. She was sure about the answer.

"Mirror, mirror, on the wall,
Who is the most beautiful of all?"

The mirror was quiet for a short moment. Then it answered:

"You, my queen, are beautiful, it is true.
But beyond the hills, where the seven dwarfs live,
Snow White is a thousand times more beautiful than you."

The queen screamed. She hit the table with both hands, and a glass fell to the floor and broke. Her whole body was shaking.

"It is not possible!" she cried. "She is dead! The huntsman brought me her lungs and her liver!"

But the mirror could not lie. The queen knew this very well. She stood in the room with her fists closed and her eyes burning. At last, she understood. The huntsman had not killed the girl. He had tricked her. He had brought her the meat of a wild animal.

"Fool!" she said. "Stupid, stupid man! I will find him later. First, I must finish this myself."

She did not call a servant. She did not say a word to anyone. She walked quickly to a hidden room at the top of the castle. No one knew about this room, except her. It was dark and cold, and the walls were covered with old books and strange bottles. She was not only vain and cruel. She also knew a little about black magic.

For a long time, she stood at a table, and she thought. She knew that Snow White was in the house of the dwarfs, and she knew that the door would be closed. Snow White would not open the door to the queen. She must change her face.

She took a little jar of dark paste, and she rubbed it on her skin. Slowly, her smooth white face became brown and rough. She drew lines on her forehead with black paint. She put grey powder in her hair. She put on a long, dirty dress and a thick brown scarf. When she looked in a small mirror, she saw an old, tired woman. Her own servants could not know her.

Then she took a big basket. She put in it many small, pretty things that a seller might have: colored ribbons, shiny buttons, and a few long laces for dresses. Last of all, she covered everything with a cloth.

"Now," she said, "I will go and sell my things."

She left the castle by a back door, and she walked away over the hills. It was a long way. She went through the green valleys and past the little rivers. She walked up the mountains and down the other side, and she did not stop to rest. Her old clothes did not make her tired. Hate made her strong.

At last, she saw the little house. It stood alone, under the trees. A thin line of smoke rose from the chimney. Through the small window, she could see someone moving. It was a girl, and she was singing.

"There you are," the queen whispered, and a cold smile came on her face. "There you are, little Snow White."

She stopped in front of the door, and she made her voice weak and kind. She knocked three times and cried:

"Beautiful things for sale! Fine things for sale! Ribbons, laces and buttons of every color!"

Inside the house, Snow White stopped singing. She put down her broom and looked out of the window.
