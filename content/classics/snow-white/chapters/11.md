# The Prince and the Apple

The prince stayed all night on the hill. He sat next to the coffin, and he did not sleep. In the morning, the first dwarf came with bread and milk, and he found the prince still there. His face was pale and serious.

"Please," said the prince. "I have a request. Give me this coffin. I will take care of her, and I will give you anything that you want. I will give you gold. I will give you silver. I will give you a house in the mountains, bigger than this one. I will give you all my land."

The dwarfs looked at each other. The seven brothers talked together in low voices. Then the oldest one stepped forward.

"No," he said. "We cannot give her to you. We will not give her up for all the gold in the world."

"But I love her," said the prince. "I cannot live without looking at her. I will honor her. I will keep her like the most precious thing in my life. Please, I beg you."

The dwarfs were silent. They saw that the prince was telling the truth. His eyes were red, as if he had been crying. His hands were shaking. At last, the seventh dwarf looked at his brothers.

"This man loves her as we do," he said. "Maybe it is right. She cannot stay on this hill forever. Let him take her to a place where she will be looked after."

The others thought about it. Slowly, one after another, they nodded their heads.

"Very well," said the first dwarf. He took the prince's hand. "You may take her. But be good to her."

"I promise," said the prince. "On my life."

He called his servants. They came with strong arms, and they lifted the glass coffin onto their shoulders. The dwarfs stood in a circle and watched. Some of them were crying. The prince thanked them again and again. Then he turned toward his home, and the servants began to carry the coffin down the hill.

It was a rough path. It was full of stones and roots and thorn bushes. The servants walked slowly and carefully, but the coffin was heavy. At one place, the road turned sharply, and a bush with thorns grew across it. One servant did not see it. His foot caught in the roots, and he tripped.

"Careful!" cried the prince.

But it was too late. The coffin shook hard, and it moved from side to side. Inside, Snow White's body moved too. And then something happened that nobody had expected. The piece of poisoned apple jumped out from between her lips.

For a moment, nobody moved. The servants stood still, with their mouths open. The prince stared at the coffin. Then, slowly, a soft breath came from Snow White. Her chest moved up and down. Her eyelids shook, and she opened her eyes.

"Oh!" she said. She lifted the cover of the coffin with her hand, and she sat up. "Where am I?"

The prince ran to her. He fell on his knees beside the coffin, and he took her hand. He was laughing and crying at the same time.

"You are alive," he said. "You are alive! You are safe, and you are with me."

Snow White looked at the strange man. She looked at the servants, and at the green hills, and at the blue sky. She did not understand.

"Who are you?" she asked. "And where are the dwarfs? What has happened?"

The prince told her everything. He told her about the poisoned apple, and about the glass coffin on the hill, and about the long years of waiting. Snow White listened with big, wide eyes. When he was finished, she began to cry softly.

"My dear dwarfs," she said. "They were so kind. They must have been so sad."

At that moment, seven small men came running down the hill. They had seen everything from far away. They shouted and waved their caps. When they saw Snow White standing there, alive and well, they cried with joy. They took her hands. They kissed her cheeks. The first dwarf threw his cap into the air, and the seventh danced in a circle around the others.

"She is alive!" they shouted. "Snow White is alive!"

Snow White laughed. She hugged each one of them, from the first to the seventh. "Thank you," she said. "Thank you for everything. You saved my life three times, and you took care of me when I was dead."

"Dear child," said the second dwarf, "we only did what was right."

The prince stood near them, and he smiled. After a time, he turned to Snow White, and he took her hand again.

"Snow White," he said, "I love you more than anything in the world. Please come with me to my father's castle. Be my wife. You will be a queen, and I will love you all my life."

Snow White looked at his face. She saw that it was kind and good. She felt her heart become warm.

"Yes," she said. "I will."
