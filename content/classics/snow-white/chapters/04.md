# The Little House with Seven Beds

Snow White walked toward the light. It was farther than she thought. She walked up a hill and down a hill, and she crossed a small stream on stones. At last, she saw a little house under the trees. It had a roof of dark wood and a tiny round window. A thin line of smoke came from the chimney.

The window was dark now, and the light had gone out. The house was quiet. Snow White stood in front of the door, and she did not know what to do. She knocked softly. Nobody answered. She knocked again. Nobody came.

"Hello?" she called. "Is anybody here?"

There was no answer. So she pushed the door, and it opened slowly.

Inside, everything was very small, and everything was very clean. In the middle of the room, there was a table with a white cloth. On the table, there were seven little plates. Next to every plate, there was a little spoon, a little knife, a little fork, and a little cup. There were seven small loaves of bread, and in the cups there was a little wine. Along the wall, there were seven little beds. Each one had a clean white blanket.

"What a pretty house," said Snow White. "But who lives here? They must be very small people."

She was so hungry and so tired. She looked at the food on the table. Her stomach hurt. She did not want to take anything that was not hers. But she was very hungry, and she could not wait.

"I will only take a little," she said to herself. "I will take just a little from each plate, so that nobody is left with nothing."

So she took a small piece of bread from every plate, and she ate it. She drank a little wine from every cup. It tasted warm and sweet. She felt a little stronger.

Then she looked at the beds. She was so tired that her eyes were closing. She tried the first bed, but it was too long for her. She tried the second one, but it was too short. The third was too soft, and the fourth was too hard. The fifth was too high, and the sixth was too low. But the seventh bed was just right. She lay down, said her evening prayer, and fell asleep at once.

When it was completely dark, the owners of the house came home.

They were seven dwarfs. They were small men with long beards, and they wore little pointed caps. All day, they worked in the mountains. They dug under the ground, and they looked for gold and silver. Their hands were dirty, and their backs were tired, and each of them carried a tool on his shoulder.

The dwarfs lit their seven little lamps, and the room became bright. At once, they saw that something was wrong.

"Someone has been here!" said the first dwarf. "Look at my chair. It is not where I left it."

"Someone has been eating from my plate," said the second dwarf.

"Someone has taken some of my bread," said the third.

"Someone has used my spoon," said the fourth.

"Someone has touched my fork," said the fifth.

"Someone has cut with my knife," said the sixth.

"And someone has drunk from my cup," said the seventh.

They all looked around the room with big eyes. Then the first dwarf went to his bed, and he said, "Someone has been lying in my bed!"

"And in mine!" said the second.

"And in mine!" said the third. And so it went, from bed to bed, until they came to the seventh.

The seventh dwarf looked at his bed, and he stood very still. There was a little girl there, and she was sound asleep. Her black hair was on the pillow, and her face was white and quiet.

"Brothers!" he whispered. "Come here! Look!"

The other six ran to him. They held up their lamps, and the light fell on Snow White's face. For a long moment, nobody said a word.

"Oh, goodness!" said the first dwarf at last. "What a lovely child!"

"She is like a little angel," said the second.

"Look at her hair," said the third. "Black as night."

"And her cheeks," said the fourth. "Red as roses."

They were so happy and so surprised. They did not want to wake her. She looked so tired. So the dwarfs let her sleep, and they made a plan. The seventh dwarf had no bed. So he slept one hour with the first dwarf, one hour with the second dwarf, and so on, from one brother to the next, until the night was over.

The lamps burned low. Outside, the stars shone, and the wind moved gently in the trees. And in the little house under the hills, the seven small men sat and watched the girl who slept in the seventh bed.
