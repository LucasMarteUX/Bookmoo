# The Mirror on the Wall

The king married again. His new wife was a tall, beautiful woman. She had a long neck, sharp green eyes, and a proud smile. When she walked into a room, everyone looked at her. People in the country said, "Our new queen is the most beautiful woman in the world."

The queen liked to hear this. She liked it more than anything. But she was not kind, and she was not gentle. Her heart was hard and cold. She was very proud, and she could not bear it when someone was more beautiful than she was.

In her room, the queen had a special mirror. It was a magic mirror, and it could not tell a lie. It was a large mirror in a frame of gold, and it hung on the wall near her window. Every morning, she stood in front of it, and she asked it the same question:

"Mirror, mirror, on the wall,
Who is the most beautiful of all?"

And every morning, the mirror answered:

"You, my queen, are the most beautiful of all."

Then the queen smiled, and she was happy. She knew that the mirror always told the truth. So every day, she was sure that she was the most beautiful woman in the land.

The queen did not like her husband's daughter very much. She did not hate the child, but she did not love her either. She gave her food and clothes, and she left her to the servants. In the castle, Snow White played in the garden, and she listened to the stories of the old cook. She learned to read, and she learned to sing. She was a quiet, kind girl, and she loved flowers and birds.

The years went by. Every year, Snow White grew taller and more beautiful. Her skin was still as white as snow. Her cheeks were still as red as blood. Her hair was still as black as ebony.

When she was seven years old, she was already lovely. The servants said, "She is like a bright morning in spring." The old gardener gave her a rose every day. The cook gave her warm bread with honey.

One morning, the queen stood in front of her mirror. She smiled, and she asked her question, as she always did:

"Mirror, mirror, on the wall,
Who is the most beautiful of all?"

But this time, the mirror did not answer at once. The room was quiet. The queen waited. Then the mirror spoke, slowly:

"My queen, you are very beautiful, it is true.
But Snow White is a thousand times more beautiful than you."

The queen's face became white. Her hands began to shake. She stepped back from the mirror, and she held the edge of the table.

"No," she said. "No! That is not possible!"

She looked into the glass again. She saw her own face, and it was beautiful. But behind it, she saw another face. It was a small face with black hair and bright eyes. It was Snow White.

The queen turned away. Her heart was full of anger and fear. A bad, dark feeling grew inside her, and she could not stop it. It was envy. She could not eat. She could not sleep. She walked up and down her room, and she looked at the mirror again and again, but the answer was always the same.

"That girl must go," she said to herself. "I cannot live in the same house with her. Every day, she is more beautiful. Every day, she is a danger to me."

At night, she lay in her bed with open eyes. She heard the clock strike twelve. She heard the wind. And she thought and thought. She had an idea, and it was a terrible one.

In the morning, she called a servant. He was a huntsman. He was a strong man with a quiet face, and he had worked for the king for many years. He bowed in front of the queen.

"You are my most faithful servant," said the queen in a sweet voice. "I have an important job for you. I want you to take Snow White into the forest. Take her far from the castle. Go deep into the trees, where no one can see you."

"Yes, my queen," said the huntsman. "And then?"

The queen looked at him with cold eyes.

"Kill her," she said. "And bring me her lungs and her liver. I want to see them with my own eyes. Then I will know that you did what I asked."

The huntsman felt a cold pain in his chest. He wanted to say no. But he was afraid of the queen. He bowed again, and he said, in a low voice, "As you wish, my queen."
