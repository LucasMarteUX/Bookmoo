# Into the Forest

The huntsman found Snow White in the garden. She was sitting by the roses, and she was talking to a small brown bird.

"Snow White," he said. "Please come with me. The queen wants you to see something in the forest."

"In the forest?" said the girl. Her eyes opened wide. "What is it?"

"Come and see," said the huntsman. He could not look at her face. He took her small hand, and they walked out of the gate.

They walked for a long time. First, they walked along a road, and then along a path, and at last into the deep green wood. The trees were tall, and their branches closed over the sky. The air smelled of earth and leaves. Snow White was happy at first. She looked at the flowers and the mushrooms and the little streams. She asked many questions. But slowly, she began to see that the huntsman was very quiet.

"Why are you sad?" she asked.

He did not answer. He stopped under a big tree, and he took out his long knife. His hand was shaking.

Snow White looked at the knife. She was very afraid, but she did not run. She looked up at the man, and her eyes were full of tears.

"Dear huntsman," she said, "please do not hurt me. I will go far away into the forest. I will never come back. I will never go home again. Please, let me live."

The huntsman looked at her. She was so young and so pretty. She was only a child. He thought of his own family. He thought of the sound of her voice in the garden, and of the roses she loved. He felt his heart break.

"All right," he said, and he put the knife away. "Run away, poor child. Go into the forest and do not come back."

Snow White took his hand and kissed it. "Thank you," she whispered. "Thank you."

Then she ran. She ran between the trees, and soon she was gone.

The huntsman stood alone. He was sure that the wild animals in the forest would find her. Perhaps a bear would eat her, or a wolf. But he could not kill her himself. He felt that a heavy stone had gone away from his heart.

Just then, a young wild pig ran out of the bushes near him. The huntsman looked at it, and he understood what he must do. He killed the animal quickly. He took out its lungs and its liver, and he put them in a bag. He did not feel proud of himself. But it was the only way to save the girl, and to save his own life.

He carried the bag back to the castle. The queen was waiting in her room.

"Here they are," said the huntsman. He held out the bag. "I did what you asked."

The queen opened the bag and looked inside. She smiled a terrible smile. "Good," she said. "Very good. Go to the kitchen and give them to the cook. Tell him to cook them with salt. I will eat them for dinner."

The cook did not like this job, but he did as he was told. That evening, the wicked queen ate the meat. She thought that she was eating Snow White's lungs and liver. She was proud and happy.

"Now I am the most beautiful woman in the land again," she said to herself.

Meanwhile, in the forest, the sun was going down. Snow White was lost.

She had been running for hours. Her feet were tired, and her dress was torn by the thorns. She was hungry and thirsty. She could not see the path, and she could not hear the sounds of the castle. All around her, there were big, dark trees. The forest was full of strange noises. She heard birds that screamed, and leaves that moved in the wind. She heard something big walk behind a bush. Her heart beat fast.

"I am all alone," she said, and she began to cry. "I do not know what to do."

She wanted to stop and sit down, but she was afraid. So she walked on, and on, and on. The wild animals came near her. She could see their eyes shining in the dark. But none of them hurt her. A deer looked at her and walked away. A fox stood still on the path and watched her go. Even a big wolf stopped and let her pass.

Slowly, the sky became darker. The first stars came out. Snow White could hardly lift her feet. She was ready to fall down on the ground and sleep under a tree.

Then she saw it. Far away, between the hills, there was a little light.
