# The Glass Coffin

For three days, the seven dwarfs stayed at home. They did not go to the mountains. They sat next to Snow White, and they watched her. They had put her on a long table, and they had covered her with a white cloth. Every hour, one of them touched her face. But there was no breath, and there was no heartbeat.

At first, they wanted to bury her. They talked about it with low voices. They found a place on a green hill, under an old tree. They looked at the earth, and they thought of the dark hole in the ground.

But when they came back to the house and looked at her again, they stopped.

"Look at her," said the first dwarf. "Look at her cheeks."

They looked. Her cheeks were still rosy and fresh. Her lips were red. Her skin was soft and warm. She did not look dead at all. She looked like a girl who was sleeping.

"I cannot do it," said the second dwarf. "I cannot put her in the dark ground."

"Nor can I," said the third.

"She is too beautiful," said the fourth. "It is not right."

"Then what do we do?" asked the fifth.

The seventh dwarf thought for a long time. He was the one who had given her his bed on the first night. Then he said, "Let us make her a coffin of glass. Then we can see her always. We can keep her close, and she will not be hidden."

The brothers nodded. It was a good idea. So the dwarfs went to their workshop, and they worked for many days. They made a long, clear coffin of glass. It was as bright as water in the sun. On the cover, they wrote in golden letters: SNOW WHITE, A KING'S DAUGHTER.

They laid her in the glass coffin, and they closed the cover softly. Then they carried it up to the top of a high green hill. It was a quiet place, with a wide view of the mountains. The sun rose there in the morning, and the moon shone there at night. The dwarfs put the coffin on a stone in the middle of the grass.

"Now she will stay with us," said the first dwarf.

After that, one of the seven always sat next to the coffin. They took turns, day and night. In the early morning, one sat there and watched the sunrise. At noon, a different one came and brought bread and a little water. At night, another one came and lit a lamp. They talked to her softly, and they told her about their day.

Other friends came, too. The birds of the forest came to her coffin. The first to come was an owl. It sat on a stone and cried with a low sad voice. Then a black raven came and sat on the glass and stood very still. Last of all came a white dove. She stood near Snow White's face, and she looked at her for a long time. The animals of the forest loved Snow White, and they were sad too.

Time went by. The leaves fell from the trees, and the snow came, and then the spring came again. Another year went by, and another year. But Snow White did not change. Her skin was still as white as snow. Her cheeks were still as red as blood. Her hair was still as black as ebony. She did not become old, and she did not become ugly. She lay in the glass coffin, and she looked as if she were only asleep.

The people of the country heard about the girl in the glass. Some of them walked a long way to see her. They stood on the hill, and they looked in silence. Then they went away with tears in their eyes.

One day, a young prince was traveling through the forest. He was tall and handsome, and he wore fine clothes. He rode a white horse, and he had some servants with him. He was on his way to a distant country, but he lost his path in the hills. At last, in the evening, he came to the dwarfs' little house, and he asked if he could stay for the night.

"You are welcome," said the first dwarf. "But we are very sad people tonight. We have a sorrow in our house."

"What is it?" asked the prince.

The dwarf did not answer. He took the prince up to the top of the green hill, and he showed him the glass coffin.

The prince walked slowly toward it. He saw the girl. He read the letters of gold. For a long time, he stood without a word. His heart was beating strangely. He had seen many beautiful women in his life, but he had never seen a face like this one.

"Who is she?" he asked at last.

"Snow White," said the dwarf. "She was a king's daughter. A wicked woman killed her with a poisoned apple."

The prince looked at her face again, and he put his hand on the glass. "I do not know how this can be," he said quietly. "But I love her."
