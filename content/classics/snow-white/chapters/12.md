# The Wedding and the Iron Shoes

The prince's father was a great and kind king. When his son came home with a beautiful girl, he welcomed her with open arms. Soon, the whole country knew that the prince was going to be married. The king sent letters to all the lands around, and he invited kings and queens, and dukes and ladies, to come to the wedding.

The castle was busy for many weeks. Cooks baked bread and cakes in the big kitchens. Gardeners put flowers on every table. Musicians practiced in the great hall, and tailors made fine dresses for the bride. Snow White wore a white dress with a long, soft cloth behind her. It was as white as the snow she was named for.

Before the wedding, the dwarfs came down from the mountains to see her. They did not wear their dirty work clothes. They wore their best coats, and their beards were clean and brushed. They stood in a row in front of her, and she laughed with joy.

"You will come to the wedding?" she asked.

"We would not miss it," said the first dwarf, "for all the gold in the mountain."

Far away, in a distant castle, the wicked queen heard about the wedding. She did not know who the bride was. She only knew that a great party was coming, and she wanted to go. She called her servants and said, "Bring my best dress. And bring my red shoes."

She stood in front of her mirror, and she looked at her own face. She liked what she saw. Her skin was smooth, and her eyes were sharp. She felt young and strong.

"Mirror, mirror, on the wall," she said, "who is the most beautiful of all?"

The mirror answered, in its calm voice:

"You, my lady, are very beautiful, it is true.
But the young queen is a thousand times more beautiful than you."

"A young queen?" said the wicked woman. She frowned. "What young queen? I am the only queen here."

"The prince's bride," said the mirror. "She is the new queen."

The queen did not like that answer at all. She was angry, and she was afraid, and she did not know why. She wanted to stay at home. But her curiosity was too strong. She wanted to see this new queen with her own eyes. She wanted to see who was more beautiful than she was.

"I must go," she said. "I must see her."

She traveled to the castle of the prince. When she got there, the wedding party had already begun. The great hall was full of people in bright clothes. There was music, and the long tables were covered with food. At the end of the room, there were two chairs of gold. On one of the chairs sat a young prince. On the other sat his bride.

The queen came into the room, and she stopped.

The bride looked up. She saw the woman at the door, and she stood slowly. Her face was calm, and her eyes were clear. She was not afraid.

The queen stared at her. She saw the white skin. She saw the red cheeks. She saw the black hair. And she knew at once who it was.

"Snow White," she whispered. "It cannot be."

She could not move. She wanted to scream, but no sound came out. She felt the whole room turn around her. Her hands were cold, and her heart was like a stone.

The prince stood up, and he spoke in a loud, clear voice.

"Everyone in this room knows that a wicked woman tried to kill my wife," he said. "She sent a servant to the forest. She came three times with false gifts. She gave her a poisoned apple. That woman is here today."

All the people in the hall became quiet. Many turned and looked at the queen. She was white as paper. She could not speak.

"We cannot let her go away," said the old king. He raised his hand. "She must have her punishment. It must be a just one."

The servants had already prepared it. In the fire of the great fireplace, there was a pair of iron shoes. They had been in the flames for a long time, and they were red hot. The servants took them out with long tongs and put them on the floor in front of the queen.

"Put them on," said the king. "And dance."

"No!" cried the queen. "Please! Have mercy!"

But nobody answered her. Two guards took her by the arms. They forced her feet into the red-hot shoes. She screamed, and she tried to run, but the guards held her. The band began to play, and she was forced to dance. She danced and danced, and the iron burned her feet. She cried out and begged them to stop.

She danced until she fell down. And she did not stand up again.

The people of the court did not speak for a long time. Then the old king raised his cup, and he said, "Let the music begin again. Let us celebrate the wedding of my son."

The band played a happy song. The guests began to eat and drink. In a few minutes, the hall was full of joy. Snow White and the prince danced together, and the seven dwarfs danced in a circle around them.

After the wedding, Snow White and the prince lived in the great castle. When the old king died, they became the king and queen of that land. They ruled well and with kindness for many, many years. They often rode up to the mountains to visit the seven dwarfs in their little house. There was always a place for them at the table, and the beds were always ready.

And the magic mirror? Nobody ever asked it a question again. The room at the top of the castle stayed closed and dark. And in the garden, every spring, three red flowers grew in the snow.
