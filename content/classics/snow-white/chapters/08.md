# The Second Visit: The Poisoned Comb

The queen went back to her secret room at the top of the castle. This time, she worked all night. She took down an old book from the shelf. She read the strange words in the light of a single candle. She mixed dark liquids in a small pot, and she stirred them with a silver spoon.

When the morning came, she had a comb in her hand. It was a beautiful comb. It was made of shining silver, and it had little blue stones at the top. Anyone who saw it wanted to take it in their hands. But the comb was not what it seemed. The queen had put a deadly poison on the teeth of it. If anyone put it in their hair, they would fall down at once.

"This will do," she said, and she wrapped it in a piece of silk.

She changed her face again. This time, she made herself look like a different woman. She put on a long, green dress and a big hat with a feather, and she painted her face with red and white. She did not look like the old seller from before. She looked like a rich lady from a faraway town. She took her basket, and she went over the hills for the second time.

At the little house, Snow White was washing the dishes. She heard a voice outside, and she looked out of the window with a smile. But when she saw the stranger, she stopped smiling.

"Fine things for sale!" called the woman. "Beautiful things from the city! Come and look!"

Snow White remembered the dwarfs' words. She remembered the laces, and how she had fallen on the floor. Her heart was beating fast. She stepped back from the window.

"No, thank you," she said. "I cannot let anyone in. The dwarfs have told me, and I promised."

"Of course, of course," said the woman. She laughed softly. "You are a good girl. But you do not have to let me in. You can look at my things from the window. Is that not enough? Look, I have something very special."

And she took the comb out of the silk. She held it up in the sun, and it shone like silver fire.

"Look at this comb," she said. "It is the finest comb in the world. I made it myself. It is a present for a pretty girl like you."

Snow White could not take her eyes off the comb. It was so beautiful. She wanted it so much. She thought for a minute. "I will not open the door," she said to herself. "But I can look at it. I can take it in my hand. That is not dangerous."

"May I see it?" she said.

"Of course, my dear," said the woman. She put it into Snow White's hand through the window. "Why do you not try it? Put it in your hair. It will look so lovely with your black hair."

Snow White put the comb in her hair.

At once, the poison went into her head. The room began to turn. She tried to take the comb out, but her hands did not obey. She sank to the floor, and she did not move again.

"There you lie, my beauty," said the queen, and her eyes shone with evil joy. "This time, there is no one to help you."

She walked away from the house, and she laughed as she went.

But the dwarfs were lucky. That evening, they came home earlier than usual. They had a feeling that something was wrong. Before they could see the house, they began to run.

The door was closed this time, but the window was open. They climbed in, and they saw the girl on the floor.

"Snow White!" cried the first dwarf. "No!"

They looked at her face. It was white and still. They looked for a wound, and they looked for a lace, but there was nothing. Then the fourth dwarf saw something in her black hair.

"What is this?" he said. "A comb!"

"Take it out!" said the fifth.

The dwarf pulled out the comb, and he threw it on the table. A moment later, Snow White drew a long breath. Her eyes opened. She looked up at the seven faces.

"The woman," she whispered. "She had a comb. It was so pretty."

"That was the queen again," said the second dwarf. "She wants to kill you. Do you understand now?"

"I understand," said Snow White. Her eyes were full of tears. "I am so foolish. I will not do it again."

"Do not blame yourself," said the third dwarf. "It is her wicked magic. But, child, you must be very careful now. Do not open the door. Do not open the window. Do not take anything from anyone. Not even a flower."

"Not even a flower," Snow White repeated. "I promise."

The dwarfs took the comb, and they threw it into the fire. It burned with a blue and green flame, and a thin black smoke went up the chimney. Then the room became quiet.

That night, nobody slept well in the little house. The dwarfs sat around the table and watched the door.
