# A New Home in the Mountains

In the morning, Snow White opened her eyes. For a moment, she did not know where she was. The ceiling was low and dark. The room was bright with yellow light. Then she saw seven small faces around her bed, and she sat up quickly with a little cry.

"Do not be afraid," said the first dwarf. "We will not hurt you."

"Who are you?" asked Snow White. She pulled the blanket up to her chin.

"We are the owners of this house," said the second dwarf. He had a kind, round face and a long grey beard. "We work in the mountains. We dig for gold. And you? What is your name, little one?"

"My name is Snow White," she said.

"Snow White," said the third dwarf. "That is a pretty name. How did you come to our house?"

Snow White looked at their friendly faces, and she began to cry. Then she told them everything. She told them about the queen, and the mirror, and the huntsman, and the long, dark walk in the forest. The dwarfs listened in silence. When she finished, they looked at each other with big, sad eyes.

"Poor child," said the fourth dwarf. "Your stepmother is a wicked woman."

"How terrible," said the fifth. "And so you ran all night?"

"Yes," said Snow White. "I did not know where to go. I only wanted to find a place to sleep. Please forgive me. I ate some of your bread, and I drank some of your wine. I took your bed."

"There is nothing to forgive," said the sixth dwarf, and he smiled. "We are glad that you came."

The seven brothers went into a corner and talked together in low voices. Snow White waited, and her heart was beating fast. At last, the seventh dwarf came back to her.

"Snow White," he said, "we have an idea. If you want, you can stay here with us. This house is small, but it is safe. If you keep our house in order, and if you cook for us and wash our clothes, and sew and knit, then you can live here as long as you want. We will take care of you."

Snow White's face became bright with joy. "Oh, yes!" she said. "I will be so happy to stay. I can cook a little, and I can sweep and wash. I will do everything that you ask."

"Then it is decided," said the first dwarf. "You are one of us now."

So Snow White stayed in the little house. Every morning, the dwarfs got up early. They ate a quick breakfast of bread and milk, and they took their tools and went away to the mountains. Every evening, they came home tired and hungry. And every evening, they found the house warm and clean, and a good dinner on the table.

Snow White worked hard all day. She swept the floor. She washed the plates. She made the beds, and she picked flowers and put them in cups. She baked bread, and she made soup from the vegetables in the garden. She sang while she worked, and the little house was full of her voice. Sometimes, a bird sat on the window and listened.

The dwarfs loved the evenings. They sat around the table, and they ate and laughed. Then they told Snow White about their day. They told her about the rocks and the dark tunnels, and they showed her small pieces of gold. In return, Snow White told them stories. She was happy.

But the dwarfs were also worried. Before they left in the morning, they always gave her the same warning.

"Listen to us carefully," said the first dwarf, one morning. "Your wicked stepmother will soon learn where you are. The mirror will tell her. She will send someone to hurt you."

"Be careful," said the second dwarf. "Do not open the door to anyone. Not for any reason."

"Not for a poor woman," said the third.

"Not for a kind old man," said the fourth.

"Not even for someone you know," said the fifth.

"We are not here all day," said the sixth. "We cannot save you if we are far away."

"Please promise us," said the seventh, and he took her hand.

Snow White looked at them, and she felt warm and safe. "I promise," she said. "I will not open the door to anyone. I will be careful."

The dwarfs nodded. They kissed her hand, one by one, and they went out into the early light. Snow White stood at the door, and she waved until the last small shape was gone among the trees.

Then she closed the door, and she put the bar across it. She turned around and looked at the little room. The seven beds, the seven chairs, and the seven cups were waiting for her to clean them. She took the broom and began to sing.
