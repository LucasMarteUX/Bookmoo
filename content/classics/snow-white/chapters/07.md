# The First Visit: The Laces

"Good day, good woman," said Snow White through the window. "What do you have for sale?"

"Many pretty things, my dear," said the old woman. She lifted the cloth from her basket. "Look at these ribbons. Look at these buttons. And here, these are the finest laces in the country. They are made of bright colored silk."

Snow White looked at the pretty things, and her eyes shone. She had no ribbons in the little house. She had no pretty things at all. She thought about the dwarfs' words, but the old woman looked so poor and so tired. She did not look dangerous.

"This woman is old," she thought. "She cannot hurt me. And I can look at her things from the window. I will only let her in for a moment."

So she took the bar from the door, and she opened it.

"Thank you, my dear," said the old woman. She came in slowly, with her basket on her arm. "What a nice, clean house. But oh, look at you! Your dress is not laced well. It is so loose. Come here, let me help you. I will put in one of my new laces, and then you will look beautiful."

"Thank you," said Snow White. She did not feel afraid. She stood in front of the old woman, and she turned around, and she let her work.

The woman was very fast. Her old hands were quick and strong. She took the new lace, and she pulled it, and she pulled it again. She pulled it tighter and tighter. Snow White tried to speak, but she could not. She could not breathe. Her head became light. The room turned around her, and her knees became weak.

Then she fell to the floor, and she lay quite still.

"That is the end of your beauty," said the queen. She stood over the girl with a cruel smile. "Now I am the most beautiful woman again."

She took her basket, and she ran out of the house. She walked quickly away through the trees, and she did not look back.

The sun went down behind the hills. Soon it was evening, and the seven dwarfs came home from the mountains. They were tired, and their feet were heavy. But as they came near the house, they saw that the door was open.

"That is strange," said the first dwarf.

"Where is Snow White?" asked the second. "She always waits for us at the door."

They ran into the house. The first dwarf cried out. Snow White lay on the floor, white and still, as if she were dead. The dwarfs came around her. They did not say anything for a moment. Then they saw that her dress was laced so tight that it was cutting into her.

"Quick!" said the third dwarf. "Her dress! Cut the lace!"

The first dwarf took out his knife. He cut the lace with one quick movement. A moment later, Snow White drew a deep breath. She coughed. Slowly, the color came back to her face. She opened her eyes.

"Where... what happened?" she said.

"Thank goodness!" said the second dwarf. "You are alive!"

Snow White told them about the old woman with the pretty laces. The dwarfs listened carefully, and when she finished, the first one shook his head.

"That old woman was the queen," he said. "I am sure of it. She is not an old woman at all. She changed her face."

"I am so sorry," said Snow White. Her voice was small. "I promised you. I did not keep my promise."

"You are safe now. That is the important thing," said the third dwarf. He held her hands. "But please, please, listen to us. Do not let anyone in when we are away. Not for any reason. She will come again."

"I promise," said Snow White. And this time, she meant it.

The queen, meanwhile, was at home. She walked quickly to her mirror, and she asked her question, and she smiled, because she was sure of the answer.

"Mirror, mirror, on the wall,
Who is the most beautiful of all?"

The mirror answered, in the same calm voice:

"You, my queen, are beautiful, it is true.
But beyond the hills, where the seven dwarfs live,
Snow White is a thousand times more beautiful than you."

The queen's face went white. She could not speak. Her blood turned cold in her heart. She knew now that the girl was alive. And she knew that the dwarfs had saved her.

"Again!" she cried. "She lives again!"

She looked at her hands, and they were shaking. But she was not afraid. She was angry, so angry that she could not think of anything else. She turned and looked out of the window at the dark hills.

"I will find a way," she said. "I will not stop. Snow White will die, even if it costs me my life."
