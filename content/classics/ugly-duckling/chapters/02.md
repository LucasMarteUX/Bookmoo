# The Duck Yard

The mother duck led her children to the duck yard. It was a big, noisy place, full of ducks. There was a great quarreling there, because two families were fighting for the head of an eel. In the end, the cat got it.

"You see," said the mother duck. "That is the way of the world." She licked her beak, because she also wanted the eel's head. "Now use your legs. Stay close together, and show your best manners. Bow your necks to the old duck over there. She is the most important one in the yard. She is of Spanish blood, and that is why she is so fat. You see that red rag on her leg? That is the finest thing that any duck can wear. It means that people do not want to lose her. Everybody must treat her with respect. Come, do not turn in your toes. A well-brought-up duckling puts his feet far apart, just like his mother and father. Now bow your necks and say 'quack.'"

The ducklings did as they were told. But the other ducks in the yard looked at them, and they said loudly, "Look! Now we are going to have another family, as if there were not enough of us already. And, oh dear, how ugly that duckling is! We will not put up with him." And one of the ducks flew at him and bit him on the neck.

"Leave him alone!" said the mother. "He is not doing any harm."

"Yes, but he is so big and strange," said the duck who had bitten him. "He needs a good beating."

"What pretty children you have, madam," said the old duck with the red rag on her leg. "They are all pretty, except that one. He is not good-looking. I wish that you could make him over again."

"That is not possible, Your Grace," said the mother duck. "He is not pretty, but he has a very good heart, and he swims as well as any of the others. Better, I might even say. I think that he will grow up pretty, and become smaller in time. He stayed too long in the egg, and that is why he is not the right shape." Then she patted his neck and smoothed his feathers. "Besides, he is a drake," she said, "and a drake does not matter so much. I think that he will be strong, and he will be able to take care of himself."

"The other ducklings are graceful enough," said the old duck. "Now make yourselves at home. If you find the head of an eel, you may bring it to me."

So they made themselves at home. But the poor duckling who had come last out of his egg, and who looked so ugly, was bitten and pushed and teased. He was teased by the ducks and by the hens too.

"He is too big!" they all said. The turkey cock, who was born with spurs and thought himself an emperor, blew himself up like a ship in full sail. He went up to the duckling and gobbled at him until he was red in the face. The poor little duckling did not know where to stand or where to go. He was so sad because he was ugly, and he was the laughing stock of the whole duck yard.

So it went on from the first day. As time passed, it got worse and worse. The poor duckling was chased about by every one. Even his brothers and sisters were unkind to him. They said again and again, "If only the cat would catch you, you ugly creature!" His mother sighed, "If only you were far away." The ducks bit him. The chickens pecked him. The girl who fed the birds kicked him with her foot.

At last, he ran away. He flew over the fence, and the little birds in the bushes flew up in fear. "They are afraid because I am so ugly," he thought. He shut his eyes, and he ran on.

He ran until he came to a large marsh, where the wild ducks lived. He was so tired and so miserable that he lay there the whole night.

In the morning, the wild ducks flew up and looked at their new friend. "What kind of duck are you?" they asked. The duckling bowed to each of them, as politely as he could.

"You are very ugly," said the wild ducks. "But that does not matter to us, as long as you do not marry into our family."

Poor thing! He did not think of marrying. All he wanted was to be allowed to lie among the reeds and drink a little water from the marsh.

He stayed there for two whole days. Then two wild geese came, or rather two ganders, because they were both males. They had not been long out of the egg, and that was why they were so bold.

"Listen, friend," said one of them. "You are so ugly that we like you. Will you join us and be a bird of passage? In another marsh nearby, there are some sweet wild geese, all unmarried, who can say 'quack.' You may have a chance to be a success, ugly as you are."

"Bang! Bang!" went a gun in the air. The two wild ganders fell dead among the reeds, and the water became red with blood. "Bang! Bang!" went the gun again, and whole flocks of wild geese rose up from the reeds. The hunters were all around the marsh. Some of them sat in the branches of the trees. A blue smoke rose between the dark trees and hung over the water. Hunting dogs came splashing through the mud. The reeds and the rushes shook on every side.
