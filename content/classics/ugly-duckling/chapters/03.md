# The Old Woman's House

The little duckling lay very still among the reeds. He did not move. He did not even breathe. A big dog came splashing through the water, and he stopped right next to him. The duckling could see the dog's wet nose and his big white teeth. He could feel his hot breath.

"This is the end," thought the duckling.

But the dog turned around. Splash, splash, he went away through the water, and he did not touch the little bird.

"Oh, thank goodness," whispered the duckling. "I am so ugly that even a dog does not want to eat me."

The guns went on all day. Bang! Bang! The sound filled the whole marsh. Only in the evening was everything quiet. Even then, the duckling waited for many hours before he lifted his head. Then he ran away from the marsh as fast as his legs could carry him. He ran across fields and over meadows. A strong wind blew in his face, and it was hard to walk.

When the sun went down, he came to a small, old house. It was so crooked that it did not know which way to fall, and so it stayed standing. The wind was so strong that the duckling had to sit down and hold on to the ground. Then he saw that the door of the house was broken. It hung from one hinge, and there was a small space at the bottom. The duckling pushed himself through it, and he was inside.

An old woman lived in the house. She lived there with a cat and a hen. The old woman called the cat "my little son." He could make his back round, and he could purr. When you stroked his fur the wrong way, he even made sparks. The hen had very short legs, and so she was called Short-legs. She laid good eggs, and the old woman loved her like a daughter.

In the morning, the cat and the hen saw the strange new guest. The cat began to purr, and the hen began to cluck.

"What is that noise?" said the old woman. She looked around, but her eyes were weak. She saw the duckling, and she thought, "What a fat duck! It must have lost its way. Now I will have duck eggs. I hope that it is not a drake."

So for three weeks, the duckling stayed in the house. No eggs came. The cat was the master of the house, and the hen was the mistress. They always said, "We and the world." They thought that they were half of the world, and the better half. The duckling did not agree. But the hen did not let him say so.

"Can you lay eggs?" she asked.

"No," said the duckling.

"Then be quiet."

"Can you make your back round? Can you purr? Can you make sparks?" said the cat.

"No," said the duckling.

"Then you cannot have an opinion, when sensible people are talking."

So the duckling sat in a corner and was sad. One day, the fresh air and the sunshine came in through the crack of the door. Suddenly, he wanted so much to swim in water that he could not stop himself. He told the hen.

"What is the matter with you?" said the hen. "You have nothing to do. That is why you have such strange ideas. Lay some eggs, or learn to purr. Then you will forget about water."

"But it is so wonderful to swim," said the duckling. "It is so good to put your head under the water, and to dive down to the bottom."

"Wonderful!" said the hen. "You are crazy. Ask the cat. He is the cleverest animal that I know. Ask him if he likes to swim. Ask our mistress, the old woman. There is nobody wiser than she is. Do you think that she wants to swim and to dive?"

"You do not understand me," said the duckling.

"We do not understand you? Then who will? Do you think that you are wiser than the cat and the old woman? And wiser than me? Do not be silly, child. You have a warm room. You have friends who can teach you. Be thankful! I do not say nice things to you, but that is because I am your friend. I tell you the truth. Now, learn to lay eggs, or learn to purr."

"I think that I will go out into the wide world," said the duckling.

"Yes, do," said the hen.

So the duckling left. He soon found a lake, and he swam and dived with joy. But all the animals that he met walked past him, because he was so ugly.
