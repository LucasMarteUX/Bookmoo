# The Biggest Egg

It was a beautiful summer day in the country. The corn in the fields was golden, and the oats were green. In the meadows, the hay was piled up in tall yellow stacks. A stork walked slowly on his long red legs through the grass. He talked in a strange language that he had learned from his mother. Around the fields, there were big dark woods, and in the middle of the woods, there were deep blue pools. It was a very good day to walk in the country.

In a sunny place, there was an old farm. It stood next to a deep river. Near the river, a hundred big leaves grew in a green wall. They were so high that a small child could stand under the biggest one. It was a quiet, hidden place, as wild as the middle of a wood.

Under one of those big leaves, a mother duck sat on her nest. She was waiting for her little ducks to come out of their eggs. It was a long wait, and she was tired and a little bored. The other ducks did not come to see her. They liked to swim in the river. They did not want to climb the slippery bank, and sit under a leaf, and talk to her. So the mother duck sat alone, day after day, and she kept her eggs warm.

At last, one egg cracked. Then another. And another. From each egg, a small yellow head came out, and a little voice said, "Peep, peep!"

"Quack, quack!" said the mother duck. And all the little ducks tried to quack, too, as well as they could. They looked about them in every direction at the big green leaves. Their mother let them look as long as they wanted, because green is good for the eyes.

"How big the world is!" said the little ducks. They had much more room now than in the egg.

"Do you think that this is the whole world?" said the mother duck. "Oh no. It goes far, far away, on the other side of the garden. It goes all the way to the priest's field. But I have never been so far. Are you all out?" She stood up and looked at her nest. "Oh dear, no. The biggest egg is still there. How long will this take? I am so tired of it!" And she sat down again.

"Well, how are you getting on?" asked an old duck who came to visit.

"One egg is taking such a long time," said the mother duck. "It will not break. But look at the others. They are the prettiest little ducks that you ever saw. They all look like their father. And he does not come to see me."

"Let me look at the egg that will not break," said the old duck. She looked at it with her head to one side. "I think that it is a turkey's egg. I was fooled like that once. I had a lot of trouble with the little one, because turkeys are afraid of water. I could not get him into it. I quacked at him, and I pushed him, but it was no use. Let me look at it again. Yes, it is a turkey's egg. Leave it, and teach your other children to swim."

"I will sit on it a little longer," said the mother duck. "I have been there so long that I can stay a few more days."

"As you like," said the old duck, and she went away.

At last, the big egg cracked. "Peep, peep!" said the young one, and he tumbled out. But how big and ugly he was! The mother duck looked at him in surprise.

"That is a very large duckling," she said. "None of the others look like him. Can he really be a turkey? Well, we will soon see. He must go into the water, even if I have to push him in."

The next day, the weather was beautiful. The sun shone brightly on the green leaves. The mother duck took her whole family down to the river. Splash! She jumped into the water. "Quack, quack!" she cried, and one little duck after another jumped in. The water closed over their heads, but they came up again at once and floated beautifully. Their legs moved by themselves, and they all went into the water. Even the ugly grey duckling swam with them.

"No, he is not a turkey," said the mother duck. "Look how well he uses his legs. See how straight he holds himself. He is my own child. And he is not so bad if you look at him properly. Quack, quack! Come with me now. I will take you into the world, and I will show you the duck yard. But keep close to me, so that nobody steps on you. And beware of the cat!"
