# Spring and the Beautiful Birds

One morning in spring, the young bird lifted his wings. They were bigger and stronger now. They beat the air, and they carried him up, up and away. Before he knew it, he was flying over a big garden.

It was a lovely garden. The apple trees were full of white flowers. The elder trees bent their long green branches down to a stream, and the stream wound slowly across a smooth green lawn. Everything was fresh and bright.

From the bushes near the water, three beautiful white swans came out. They shook their feathers, and they floated on the water. The young bird knew them. They were the birds that he had seen in the sky. He felt a deep sadness in his heart.

"I will fly to them," he said. "They are kings among the birds. They will probably kill me, because I am so ugly, and I dare to come near. But I do not care. It is better to be killed by them than to be bitten by the ducks, and pecked by the hens, and kicked by the girl, and to be so cold and hungry in the winter."

He flew down to the water, and he swam toward the swans. They saw him, and they came to meet him with their feathers raised.

"Kill me," said the poor bird. He lowered his head to the water, and he waited.

But what did he see in the clear water under his face?

He saw himself. He was not a grey, clumsy, ugly bird anymore. He was a swan.

It does not matter that you were born in a duck yard, if you came from a swan's egg.

He was glad now that he had suffered so much. Now he understood how good his new happiness was. The great swans swam around him, and they touched him gently with their beaks. They were kind to him, as they were to all of their own.

Some children came into the garden. They had bread and corn, and they threw it into the water. The smallest child saw the young swan, and she cried out, "Look, there is a new one!" The other children cried, "Yes, a new one! A new one!" They clapped their hands and danced. They ran to find their father and mother, and soon more bread and cake came into the water. Everyone said, "The new one is the most beautiful of all. He is so young and so lovely!"

The old swans bowed their heads to him.

He felt very shy. He hid his head under his wing. He did not know what to do. He was so happy, but he was not proud. He remembered how everyone had laughed at him. He remembered the cold and the hunger. And now they all said that he was the most beautiful bird of all. The elder trees bent their branches to the water in front of him. The sun shone warm and bright.

Then he shook his feathers. He lifted his slender neck. And from his heart, he cried:

"I never dreamed of so much happiness, when I was the ugly duckling!"
