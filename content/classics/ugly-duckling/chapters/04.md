# The Swans in the Sky

Autumn came. The leaves in the wood turned yellow and brown, and the wind picked them up and made them dance. The sky was grey and heavy. A raven stood on a fence, and it cried, "Caw! Caw!" because of the cold. The poor duckling shivered when he heard it. He was very unhappy.

One evening, the sun was going down in a beautiful red sky. Suddenly, a flock of large birds rose from the bushes. The duckling had never seen such lovely birds. They were as white as snow. They had long, graceful necks. They were swans.

They cried out in strange, sweet voices. They opened their long, bright wings, and they flew up into the sky. They were leaving the cold country. They were going to warm lands and to the open sea.

The swans rose higher and higher. The ugly little duckling watched them, and a strange feeling came into his heart. He turned in the water, round and round, like a wheel. He stretched his neck up to the sky, and he gave a cry so loud and so strange that he was afraid of it himself. He could not forget those beautiful, happy birds. When they were out of sight, he dived to the bottom of the lake. When he came up again, he felt like a different bird.

He did not know the name of those birds. He did not know where they were going. But he loved them more than he had ever loved anyone. He was not jealous. How could he ever hope to be so beautiful? He only wished that the ducks could be kind to him, and let him live among them.

The winter was very cold. The duckling had to swim all day to keep a hole open in the water. But every night, the hole became smaller. The ice cracked and groaned around him. He moved his legs and moved them, and he did not stop. At last, he was so tired that he could not move any more. He was caught in the ice.

Early the next morning, a farmer came by. He saw the poor bird. He broke the ice with his heavy wooden shoe, and he carried the duckling home to his wife. In the warm house, the duckling woke up. He was alive.

The farmer's children wanted to play with him. But the duckling thought that they wanted to hurt him. He was so afraid that he jumped into the milk bowl, and the milk went everywhere. The woman screamed, and she clapped her hands. That frightened him more. He flew into the butter, and then into the flour. What a mess! The woman ran after him with a big iron stick. The children laughed and shouted and fell over each other. They tried to catch him.

Luckily, the door was open. The duckling ran out through it, into the bushes, and he fell into the soft new snow. He lay there, very tired.

It would be too sad to tell you about all the troubles of that long, hard winter. The duckling hid in the reeds of the marsh. He was cold, and he was hungry, and he was alone. He did not give up. He waited. And one morning, he felt the sun on his back. It was warm. He heard the larks sing. At last, spring had come.
