# A Basket for Grandmother

"Little Red Cap!" her mother called from the kitchen. "Please come here."

The girl ran inside. On the table, there was a round cake with golden brown edges. Next to the cake, there was a bottle of red wine. Her mother was putting them into a basket, and she was covering them with a white cloth.

"What a lovely smell!" said Little Red Cap. "Is that cake for me?"

"No, my dear," said her mother. "It is for your grandmother. She is ill, and she is very weak. Yesterday was baking day, so I made this cake for her. The wine will make her strong again. I want you to take this basket to her house today."

"Today?" said the girl. Her eyes opened wide. "By myself?"

"Yes, by yourself," said her mother. "You are a big girl now. You know the way, and the weather is good. But you must listen to me very carefully. Will you do that?"

"Yes, Mother. I promise."

Her mother sat down next to her and took her hands. "First," she said, "go early. Leave now, before the sun is too hot. A long walk in the heat is not good for anyone."

"Yes, Mother."

"Second, walk nicely and quietly. Do not run. If you run, you can fall, and then the bottle can break. Then your grandmother gets nothing. And you can hurt yourself, too."

"I will walk slowly," said the girl.

"Third, stay on the path. The path goes straight through the wood to your grandmother's house. Do not go into the trees. Do not leave the path for any reason. The wood is big, and it is easy to get lost there."

"I will stay on the path," said Little Red Cap.

"Fourth, when you go into your grandmother's room, say 'Good morning' first. Do not forget this. And do not look into every corner of her room before you say it. That is not polite."

The girl laughed a little. "I always look into every corner," she said. "I like to see everything."

"I know," said her mother. "That is why I am telling you. Say 'Good morning' first. Then you can look."

"I understand."

"Good." Her mother stood up. She took the red cap from the hook by the door, and she put it carefully on her daughter's head. "Now, give me your hand, and promise me."

Little Red Cap put her hand in her mother's hand. "I promise," she said. "I will take great care."

Her mother smiled. But for one small moment, there was a worried look in her eyes. She did not say anything about it. She only kissed her daughter on the forehead.

"Give your grandmother a big hug from me," she said. "And tell her to rest and get well. Now go. And come home before it is dark."

"Yes, Mother. Goodbye!"

The girl took the basket and went out of the door. The basket was not very heavy, but she held it with both hands. She walked down the garden path, and she opened the little gate. Then she turned around and waved. Her mother stood at the window and waved back.

Little Red Cap walked along the village road. The baker was putting fresh bread in his window. "Where are you going so early, Little Red Cap?" he asked.

"I am going to see my grandmother," she said. "She is ill, and I have a cake for her."

"What a good girl," said the baker. "Please say hello to her for me. Tell her that we all hope she gets well soon."

"I will," said the girl, and she walked on.

At the end of the village, the houses stopped, and the green fields began. A little farther on, she could see the dark line of the wood. The trees were tall and thick, and the leaves moved slowly in the wind. The path went into the trees like a brown ribbon. It looked small and quiet.

For a moment, Little Red Cap stood still. She had been to her grandmother's house many times before, but always with her mother. Now she was alone. Her heart beat a little faster. But then she heard a bird singing in the trees, and she smiled.

"It is a beautiful day," she said to herself. "There is nothing to be afraid of."

She said it aloud, and her own voice sounded strong. She lifted the basket a little higher, and she walked into the wood.
