# The Girl with the Red Cap

Once upon a time, there was a small village near a big green wood. In the village, there was a little girl. Everyone loved her. The baker smiled when she walked past his shop. The old man at the well said, "Good morning, little one," and the neighbors waved from their windows. But her grandmother loved her most of all.

Her grandmother lived alone in a small house in the wood. She was kind and gentle, and she had many gifts for her granddaughter. She gave her sweet apples in the autumn. She gave her warm socks in the winter. She told her old stories by the fire, and she always had time to listen.

One day, the grandmother made a very special gift. She took a piece of soft red velvet and made a little cap. She worked slowly, night after night, with a needle and thread. When it was ready, she brought it to the village.

"This is for you," she said. "Put it on."

The girl put the cap on her head. It fitted her perfectly. It was soft and warm, and the red was bright and beautiful.

"Oh, Grandmother!" she said. "I love it. Thank you, thank you!"

The grandmother laughed. "Now you look like a little flower in the grass."

After that day, the girl never wore anything else on her head. She wore the cap in the morning, and she wore it in the afternoon. She wore it when it was sunny, and she wore it when it rained. Her mother often said, "You must take it off for bed!" And the girl always did, but only at night. In the morning, the cap was on her head again.

Soon, nobody in the village used her real name. They called her Little Red Cap. The baker said, "Here comes Little Red Cap!" The children said, "Come and play, Little Red Cap!" At first, the girl felt a little surprised. But then she liked her new name very much. It reminded her of her grandmother every day.

Little Red Cap lived with her mother in a neat little house at the end of the village road. The house was small, but it was warm and clean. There was a garden at the back, with beans and carrots and a little apple tree. Her mother worked hard every day. She cooked, she cleaned, and she took care of the garden. In the evenings, mother and daughter sat together, and the girl told her about her day.

The girl was a good child. She helped her mother with the dishes. She gave water to the plants. She was kind to the animals and said hello to everyone. But she was also a curious girl. She loved to look at things. She loved to watch the birds, the clouds, and the little insects in the grass. Sometimes, she looked at one thing for so long that she forgot all the other things she had to do. Her mother often smiled and shook her head.

"You are always looking and dreaming," she said. "One day, your eyes will find something wonderful. But please remember, my dear, a girl must also listen."

"I always listen, Mother," said Little Red Cap.

"Yes," said her mother. "Most of the time."

Little Red Cap laughed, and her mother laughed with her.

In the village, people liked to say that Little Red Cap was the happiest girl in the world. And that was almost true. There was only one thing that made her sad. Her grandmother lived far away, in the middle of the wood, and the girl could not see her every day. She wanted to visit her more often. But the wood was big, and the road was long, and her mother did not like her to go alone.

So the girl waited. She waited for a day when her mother would say, "Yes, you can go." She did not know that the day was very near. She did not know, too, that this day would be different from all the days before it.

It was a warm morning in early summer. The sun was already high, and the sky was blue. In the garden, the bees were buzzing. In the kitchen, her mother was making something that smelled very sweet.
