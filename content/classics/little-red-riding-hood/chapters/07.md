# The Huntsman

At that moment, a man was walking along the path through the wood. He was a tall man with a strong back and a brown coat. A long gun hung from his shoulder, and a leather bag hung from his belt. He was a huntsman. All the people in the village knew him. He knew the wood better than anyone, and he knew every path, every river, and every cave.

For a long time, the huntsman had been looking for a wolf. The wolf was big and grey. Many farmers had lost their sheep to him, and everyone was afraid of him. The huntsman had followed his tracks for many days. He had found them near the river, and under the old bridge, and by the edge of the fields. But he had never seen the wolf himself.

Today, he was walking along the path, and he was thinking about his lunch. He was tired and hungry. Then he came to the three oak trees, and he stopped.

He heard a loud sound.

It was coming from the little house. It was a very strange sound. It went up and down, up and down. It was a kind of deep, rough snore.

"What is that?" said the huntsman to himself. "It sounds like a big animal. But this is the grandmother's house."

He listened for a moment. Then he laughed a little. "Ah. It is only the old woman. How loudly she snores! I did not know that. Well, she is ill. Perhaps she needs something. I will just look in and see if she is all right."

He walked up to the door. He saw that it was open. That was strange. He knew the old woman well, and she always kept her door closed. He walked inside slowly, and he looked around.

The room was dim. On the table, there was a basket. On the floor, there were flowers. And in the bed, there was a big shape under the blanket.

The huntsman went to the bed, and then he stopped. His mouth opened.

It was not the old woman in the bed. It was a wolf.

The wolf was lying on the pillow with a white nightcap on his head. His mouth was a little open, and his big stomach moved up and down with every breath. His snores were so loud that the whole bed shook.

"So here you are!" whispered the huntsman. His eyes were hard. "You old sinner! I have looked for you for so long. At last, I have found you."

He lifted his gun and pointed it at the wolf. His finger was on the trigger. In one moment, it would all be over.

But then he stopped.

He looked at the wolf's stomach. It was very big and round, much bigger than a wolf's stomach should be. He looked at the empty bed curtains. He looked at the basket and the flowers on the floor. And he thought about something.

"Where is the grandmother?" he said, very quietly. "And whose basket is that? And whose flowers?"

He knew that the old woman was very ill. He knew that she could not go out. Then he remembered something. That morning, in the village, the baker had told him about Little Red Cap. She had gone to see her grandmother with a cake in her basket. Now there was a basket on the table, with a white cloth, and a cake inside it.

"Little Red Cap," he whispered.

The huntsman felt cold. He took a deep breath. He understood now. "This wolf is not only a thief of sheep," he thought. "He has eaten the grandmother. And perhaps the girl too! But perhaps... perhaps they are still alive. A wolf eats fast, and he does not chew. If I shoot him now, they can never come out."

So he did not shoot. He put his gun down very slowly on the floor, and he did not make a sound. The wolf snored on.

The huntsman looked around the room. In a corner, next to the sewing basket, there was a pair of scissors. They were long and sharp. He took them in his hand.

"Be quiet, old sinner," he said under his breath. "Sleep well. Sleep deeply."

He went back to the bed. He put one hand on the wolf's stomach, and he held the scissors in the other. His hands were steady. He took a deep breath.

Then, very carefully, he made the first cut.
