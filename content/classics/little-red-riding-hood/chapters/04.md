# Flowers, Flowers, Flowers

"And do you hear the birds?" said the wolf. His voice was very sweet. "Listen. They are singing so happily today. But you walk along this path so seriously, Little Red Cap, like a child on the way to school. Everything in the wood is merry. Only you are not."

The girl listened. It was true. In every tree, a bird was singing. There was a high song and a low song, a long song and a short song. The sun was dancing between the leaves, and the flowers were shining like small lamps in the grass.

"He is right," she thought. "It is such a beautiful morning. And I have not looked at anything." She remembered her mother's words about the path. But the path was right there, only three steps away. She could see it behind her. And the wolf was so friendly.

Then another thought came to her. "What if I take some flowers to Grandmother? A fresh bunch of flowers will make her so happy. She is ill, and she stays in bed all day. She cannot go out to look at flowers. I can bring the flowers to her. Yes! And it is still very early. I have a lot of time. I will arrive in good time."

She looked at the wolf. He was nodding his head slowly.

"I think that is a very good idea," he said.

So Little Red Cap put down her basket for a moment, and she stepped off the path.

She picked a red flower. It was small and soft. She smelled it, and then she held it in her hand. But just a few steps away, there was another flower, and it was bigger and prettier. She walked to it and picked it. Then she saw a yellow one near a tree. She picked that one too. After that, she saw a white one, and then a blue one, and then another red one.

Every time she picked a flower, she saw a better one a little farther away. "Just one more," she said to herself. "Only one more, and then I go back." But there was always one more.

She went deeper and deeper into the wood. The trees stood close together now, and the light was green and dim. She did not notice. She only saw the flowers, and she only heard the birds. She forgot the path. She forgot her mother's words. She even forgot, for some time, that the wolf was there.

And the wolf was not there. While the girl was busy with the flowers, he stood still for a moment and smiled to himself. Then he turned around, and he ran.

He ran fast, faster than any dog. He ran along the path between the trees, and he did not make a sound. His long legs jumped over the roots and stones. He did not stop, and he did not look back. He knew exactly where he was going.

"The old woman first," he said to himself as he ran. "Then the girl. It will be an easy day."

Back in the wood, Little Red Cap kept walking. Her hands were now full of flowers. She had red ones, yellow ones, white ones, and blue ones. She had made a big, bright bunch. She smelled it, and it smelled like summer.

Slowly, she began to feel tired. Her arms were heavy. When she looked down, she saw that she could not carry any more flowers. She had too many.

"Now I have enough," she said, and she laughed. "Grandmother will be so happy!"

Then she looked around her. The wood was thick on all sides. She could not see the path.

For a moment, she felt a cold little fear in her stomach. But then she saw the basket. She had left it on the ground, near a tree. And behind the basket, she saw the brown line of the path, not far away.

"Oh, thank goodness," she said. She took her basket, and she walked back to the path. She did not run. She walked slowly and carefully, and she held the flowers close to her chest.

When she was on the path again, she stood still and took a deep breath. "I was silly," she said to herself. "I must not leave the path again. Mother was right. I am lucky that I can find it."

But she was not really sorry yet. She looked at the flowers in her hands, and she smiled. They were beautiful. Her grandmother would love them.

"But I must hurry," she thought. "The sun is higher now. I was in the flowers for a long time."

So she walked as fast as she could, but she did not run. She remembered the bottle in the basket. She did not want to break it.

She did not know that the wolf was already at her grandmother's door.
