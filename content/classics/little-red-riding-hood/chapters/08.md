# Stones for the Wolf

The huntsman cut slowly. He was careful, and he did not hurry. The scissors made a soft sound in the quiet room. Snip, snip. The wolf did not wake up. He only gave a small sound in his sleep, and then he snored again.

After two cuts, the huntsman saw something red in the dark. It was a little red cap. It was shining there like a small flame.

"She is here!" he said. His heart jumped.

He made two more cuts, and the opening grew wider. All at once, a small girl jumped out into the light. Her hair was wild, and her face was white. She took a great breath, and then she began to cry and laugh at the same time.

"Oh!" she said. "I was so frightened! It was so dark inside the wolf! It was the darkest place in the world!"

"You are safe now, child," said the huntsman. He lifted her gently to the floor. "You are safe."

"I am safe," she said, and she held his hand tightly. "Thank you, thank you!"

But the huntsman did not yet let himself rest. He bent over the wolf again. "Wait," he said. "There is someone else."

He made another cut. This time, an old woman's face appeared. It was pale and thin, and her eyes were closed. She was breathing very slowly, as if she was very tired.

"Grandmother!" cried Little Red Cap. She ran to the bed. "Grandmother, it is me! Please wake up!"

The huntsman put his arms under the old woman and lifted her carefully. Together, he and the girl carried her to a chair near the window. The old woman's mouth opened, and she took a long, shaky breath. Then another. Little by little, the color came back to her face.

"Where... where am I?" she said at last.

"You are at home, Grandmother," said the girl. She was crying now. "You are home, and you are alive!"

The old woman opened her eyes. She saw her granddaughter, and she put out her thin hand. "My little Red Cap," she said. "Is it really you? I had a dream... a terrible dream. I was in the dark, and there was a great shadow..."

"It was not a dream," said the huntsman. "But it is finished now."

For a moment, nobody spoke. The sun came in through the window, and it fell on all three of them. The old woman held her granddaughter's hand. The huntsman looked at them with a gentle smile.

Then the girl looked at the wolf. He was still asleep. His stomach was open, and he was not moving. His snores were slower now.

"What do we do with him?" she asked in a small voice.

"If he wakes up, he will be very angry," said the huntsman. "He may chase us. He may hurt us. We must stop him before that."

The girl thought for a moment. Then her eyes became bright.

"I have an idea," she said. "Stones! There are big stones by the garden wall. I can bring them. We can put them in his stomach, in the place where Grandmother and I were."

The huntsman looked at her. "That is a very good idea," he said. "Quick, then. Bring as many as you can."

Little Red Cap ran out of the door. She went around to the garden wall, and she picked up a big grey stone. It was very heavy, but she held it in both hands. She carried it inside and gave it to the huntsman. Then she ran out again. She brought one stone, and then another, and then another. Her hands were red and dusty, but she did not stop. This time, she did not think about flowers.

The huntsman put the stones into the wolf's stomach, one by one. When it was full, he took a needle and thread from the old woman's sewing basket. He sewed the opening closed with big, strong stitches. The wolf slept through all of it.

"There," said the huntsman. "Now we wait."

Soon the wolf began to move. He opened one yellow eye, and then the other. He yawned a very wide yawn. Something was wrong. His stomach felt very heavy, and he felt thirsty, so thirsty. "I must go to the river," he thought. "I must drink. Then I will think about my next meal."

But when he saw the huntsman standing in the room, he understood something terrible. His eyes opened wide, and he tried to jump out of the bed. He wanted to run away through the door.

But the stones were too heavy. When he stood up, his legs shook, and he fell at once on the floor. He gave one last long sigh. Then the wolf did not move again.

The huntsman, the girl, and the old woman stood together and looked at him. The room was very quiet.

"It is over," said the huntsman. "The wolf will never trouble anyone again."

The grandmother put her hand on her heart. "Thank you," she said. "Thank you, brave man. You saved us both."

"No," said the huntsman, with a smile. "Your granddaughter saved us both. It was her idea. I only held the scissors."

Little Red Cap felt her face go warm. She said nothing, but she held her grandmother's hand more tightly.
