# Cake and a Promise

The huntsman took off his coat and put it on the chair. He took the wolf's grey skin carefully from the floor, and he rolled it up. He did not want to leave anything behind.

"This is the end of his sheep-stealing," he said. "I will take the skin to the village. Then everyone can see that the big wolf is gone. The farmers will sleep well tonight. They will be glad, and so will their sheep."

"Please take it," said the grandmother. "I do not want to see it again."

The huntsman nodded. He put the skin over his shoulder and picked up his gun. Then he said goodbye to the old woman. He took the girl's hand and shook it, as if she were a grown woman.

"You are a brave girl," he said. "Listen to your mother, and stay on the path. But remember, you are also clever."

"Thank you," said Little Red Cap. "I will remember."

When he walked out of the door, the afternoon sun shone on his brown coat. Soon, he was a small shape on the path, and then he was gone in the trees.

Now the little house was quiet again. The old woman sat in her chair near the window, and the girl sat on the floor next to her. They did not say much for a long time. They only looked at each other, and they held hands.

At last, the grandmother smiled. "Child," she said, "I am so hungry. And you have come all this way. Is there not something in your basket?"

The girl jumped up. "Yes! Mother's cake! I forgot!" She ran to the table. The basket was still there, and under the white cloth the cake was still whole. The bottle of wine was not broken. The wolf had not touched it at all.

"The cake is not broken," said the girl. "And the bottle is not broken, either. I walked slowly, as Mother said. At least," she added, a little quietly, "I did not run."

The grandmother laughed softly. "You did very well," she said.

Little Red Cap cut the cake into big pieces. She poured the wine into a small cup, and she gave it to her grandmother. Then she sat down next to her with her own piece of cake. The cake was sweet and warm, with raisins inside it. It tasted better than any cake she had ever eaten.

The grandmother took a little wine, and she ate a piece of the cake. Slowly, the color came back into her cheeks. Her hands were less shaky. She sat up straighter, and she looked much stronger.

"It is a wonderful cake," she said. "Your mother is a very good cook. And the wine... I feel better already."

"Mother said it will make you strong," said the girl.

"Your mother is wise," said the grandmother. "But you know, I think you made me strong, too."

They ate in peace and quiet. Outside, the birds began to sing again, and the cat came out from under the table. It jumped on the old woman's lap and began to purr.

Then the girl put down her cake. She was looking at the flowers on the floor. They were wilted now, and some of them had lost their petals. But she gathered them up, and she put them in a cup of water on the table.

"They are for you," she said. "I picked them in the wood. I wanted to bring you something pretty. But..." Her voice became small. "But because of the flowers, I left the path. I did not do what Mother asked. Because of that, the wolf ran to your house before me."

The grandmother looked at her for a long moment. Her eyes were kind. "Oh, my dear," she said. "It was a mistake. But you are not the first person to make a mistake, and you will not be the last. The important thing is to learn from it."

"I have learned," said Little Red Cap. She said it very seriously. Then she looked out of the window at the green wood, and she said, in a low voice, "As long as I live, I will never leave the path by myself again. Not when my mother has told me not to. I promise."

"I believe you," said the grandmother. She put her hand on the girl's cap. "And now, please. Stay with me for a little while. Tell me everything that happened in the village this week."

So the girl talked. She told her about the baker and the old man at the well. She told her about the apple tree in the garden. The grandmother listened and laughed, and the afternoon went by slowly and peacefully.
