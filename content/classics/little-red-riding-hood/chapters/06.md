# What Big Ears You Have

At last, Little Red Cap saw the three tall oak trees. Under them, there was a small house with a green door. She knew it well. She stopped for a moment and took a deep breath.

"Here I am," she said happily. "And the flowers are still fresh."

She walked up to the house. But something was wrong. She stopped again.

The green door was open. It stood a little open, and it moved slowly in the wind.

"That is strange," she said. "Grandmother never leaves her door open. She is always cold, even in summer."

She went in slowly. The room was quiet and dim. She put her basket on the table, and she stood still. All at once, she had a very strange feeling. It was a feeling in her stomach, like a small cold stone.

"Oh dear," she said quietly. "I feel so uneasy today. Why? I always like to visit Grandmother."

She remembered her mother's words. "Say 'Good morning' first." So she said it, in a small voice.

"Good morning, Grandmother."

Nobody answered.

She said it again, a little louder. "Good morning!"

Again, there was no answer. Only the wind moved in the room, and the white curtains of the window moved with it.

Little Red Cap walked slowly to the bed. The curtains of the bed were closed. She reached out, and she pulled them back.

There lay her grandmother, with the white nightcap pulled far down over her face. She looked very strange.

"Oh, Grandmother!" said the girl. She tried to laugh, but her voice was small. "How are you today? You look so different."

"I am not well, my child," said a deep, rough voice from the bed.

The girl looked more closely. She could see just a little of the face under the cap. It was not like her grandmother's face. It was long and hairy, and it was very dark in the shadows.

"Grandmother," she said slowly, "what big ears you have!"

"The better to hear you with, my child," said the voice.

The girl moved a little nearer. "But Grandmother, what big eyes you have!"

"The better to see you with, my dear," said the voice.

Now she was very close to the bed. The room seemed to grow smaller around her. Her heart was beating hard.

"But Grandmother," she said, "what big hands you have!"

"The better to hug you with," said the voice.

She stepped back. A cold feeling ran down her back. But she could not stop herself. There was one more thing, and she had to ask.

"Oh, Grandmother!" she cried. "What a terrible big mouth you have!"

"The better to eat you with!"

And before the girl could scream, the wolf jumped out of the bed in one great jump. He opened his mouth wide, and in one moment, Little Red Cap was gone.

The basket stood on the table. The flowers lay on the floor, red and yellow and white and blue, and the wind from the open door moved them gently over the wooden boards.

The wolf stood in the room for a moment. His stomach was full and heavy. He licked his lips, and he smiled his long white smile.

"Two good meals in one morning," he said. "What a fine day."

He climbed back into the bed. He did not close the curtains this time. He did not care. He lay down on the soft pillow, and he closed his eyes. His breathing became slow and deep. In a little time, he was asleep.

And then he began to snore.

It was a very loud snore. It was so loud that the cups on the table shook a little. It was a sound like a big saw cutting through a tree. It went up and down, up and down, and it filled the whole house. Outside, the birds stopped singing for a moment, and then they flew away.

In the dark corner under the table, the grey cat opened its eyes, and it did not move. It was the only one who saw, and it could not say a word.
