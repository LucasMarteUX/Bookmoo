# Three Beds

Goldilocks climbed the stairs. At the top, there was a small bedroom. It was a clean, bright room with a low ceiling. In it, there were three beds. There was a great bed, a middle-sized bed, and a little bed. Each one had a warm blanket and a soft pillow.

She was so tired that she could hardly keep her eyes open. She yawned a big yawn.

"I will only lie down for a minute," she said. "I will close my eyes. Then I will go home."

She went to the first bed, the great bed. She climbed up on it and lay down. It was big and high, and the pillow was very thick.

"This bed is too high at the head," she said. Her neck hurt. "I cannot sleep like this."

She got down, and she went to the second bed, the middle-sized bed. She lay on it and put her head on the pillow.

"This one is too high at the foot," she said. Her feet were in the air. "This is not comfortable at all."

She got up, and she tried the third bed, the little bed. It was low and neat. It had a patchwork blanket, and a small pillow that smelled of flowers. She lay down in it, and she pulled the blanket up to her chin.

"Ah," she said. She sighed happily. "This bed is not too high at the head, and it is not too high at the foot. It is just right!"

She closed her eyes. The room was warm and quiet. She could hear the wind in the trees. In a few minutes, she was fast asleep, and she began to dream. She dreamed of a green wood, and a house with a blue door, and a table full of porridge.

Meanwhile, the three bears were coming home.

They had had a lovely walk. The Little, Small, Wee Bear had picked a bunch of wild flowers. The Middle-sized Bear had found some nuts. The Great, Huge Bear was hungry again. "The porridge will be cool now," he said. "I think that we can eat."

They walked up the path to the little house. The Great, Huge Bear went first. He opened the door, and he stopped.

"Something is wrong," he said.

"What is it?" said the Middle-sized Bear.

"I do not know," said the Great, Huge Bear. "But I do not like it."

They went in, and they looked at the table. The Great, Huge Bear looked at his great pot. The spoon was standing in the porridge, and he had not left it like that.

"Somebody has been at my porridge!" he said, in his great, rough, gruff voice.

The Middle-sized Bear looked at his pot. His spoon was in it too, and the porridge was a little less.

"Somebody has been at my porridge!" he said, in his middle voice.

Then the Little, Small, Wee Bear looked at his little blue pot. His spoon was there. But the porridge was gone. There was not even a little left.

"Somebody has been at my porridge," he said, in his little, small, wee voice, "and they have eaten it all up!"

His voice shook. He was very sad. His porridge was his favorite breakfast, and now it was gone.

The three bears looked around the room. Somebody had been in their house. They did not know who it was, or why. They moved slowly toward the fireplace. The Great, Huge Bear looked at his chair. The hard cushion was out of place.

"Somebody has been sitting in my chair!" he said, in his great, rough, gruff voice.

The Middle-sized Bear looked at his chair. The soft cushion was flat.

"Somebody has been sitting in my chair!" he said, in his middle voice.

The Little, Small, Wee Bear looked at his chair. He gave a cry. His little chair was lying on the floor, and the seat was out.

"Somebody has been sitting in my chair," he said, in his little, small, wee voice, "and has broken it all to pieces!"

He picked up the broken seat. He held it in his two small paws, and big tears came into his eyes. He loved that little chair.

"Do not cry," said the Middle-sized Bear, and he put his arm around him. "We will fix it."

"We must find out who did this," said the Great, Huge Bear. His voice was very low and angry. "Let us look upstairs."
