# "Somebody Has Been Lying in My Bed!"

The three bears went up the stairs, slowly, one by one. The steps made a creaking sound. The Great, Huge Bear went first, the Middle-sized Bear came next, and the Little, Small, Wee Bear came last, with his broken chair seat in his arms.

They stopped at the door of their bedroom, and they looked in.

The Great, Huge Bear looked at his bed. The pillow was not in its place. It was pushed to one side.

"Somebody has been lying in my bed!" he said, in his great, rough, gruff voice.

The Middle-sized Bear looked at his bed. The blanket was pulled back, and the pillow was out of place.

"Somebody has been lying in my bed!" he said, in his middle voice.

Then the Little, Small, Wee Bear walked up to his own little bed. He looked at it. He put down his broken chair, and he gave a loud cry. In his bed, with her head on his pillow, there was a girl. She had long golden hair, and her face was round and pink. She was sleeping quietly, with one hand under her cheek.

"Somebody has been lying in my bed," said the Little, Small, Wee Bear, in his little, small, wee voice, "and here she is!"

The little girl had heard something in her sleep. She had heard the great, rough, gruff voice of the Great, Huge Bear, but she was sleeping so soundly that it sounded like the wind in the chimney. She had heard the middle voice of the Middle-sized Bear, but it was only like a person talking in a dream. She turned over, and she smiled, and she slept on.

But the Little, Small, Wee Bear's voice was high and sharp. It was so shrill that it went into her head like a needle. She opened her eyes at once.

At first, she did not know where she was. She looked up at the low ceiling. Then she looked to the side. She saw three faces looking down at her. There was a great, huge bear, and a middle-sized bear, and a little, small, wee bear. They were all looking at her very hard.

Goldilocks gave a scream. She sat up in the bed. Her heart was beating like a drum.

"Oh!" she said. "Oh, please!"

The Great, Huge Bear opened his mouth. But before he could say a word, the little girl jumped out of the bed. She did not stop to put on her shoes. She ran across the room as fast as she could. There was a window there, and it was open. She did not think. She climbed on the sill, and she jumped.

It was not a long way down. She landed on the soft grass, and she rolled over, and she stood up. She did not look back. She ran into the wood with her yellow hair flying behind her.

The three bears ran to the window. They looked out. They saw the girl running between the trees, smaller and smaller, until she was gone.

"Well!" said the Great, Huge Bear. "What a strange child."

"She was very frightened," said the Middle-sized Bear.

"She ate my porridge," said the Little, Small, Wee Bear, "and she broke my chair."

The Middle-sized Bear patted his head. "That is true," he said. "But she was only a little girl. She did not know any better. And I think that she has learned a lesson. She will not go into a stranger's house again."

The Great, Huge Bear looked at the broken chair seat. He rubbed his chin. "I can mend it," he said at last. "It is only a little crack. Come, we will do it together."

So they went downstairs, and the three bears sat at the table. The Great, Huge Bear made a new pot of porridge, and this time, they did not leave it alone. They ate it together, with a smile. The Little, Small, Wee Bear ate two bowls, because he was so hungry. Then the Great, Huge Bear took out his box of tools, and he mended the little chair. By evening, it was as good as new.

What happened to the little girl? Nobody knows. Some people say that she ran all the way home and told her mother everything. Some say that she did not stop until she reached the village. Others say that she was lost in the wood for a long time, until a kind woodcutter found her. But everyone agrees on one thing. The three bears never saw her again.

And the little house in the wood stayed as it was, with its blue door and its flowers. Every morning, the three bears made their porridge, and they went for a walk in the wood while it cooled. But after that day, the Great, Huge Bear always locked the door.
