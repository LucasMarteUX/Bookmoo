# Goldilocks Comes In

At that moment, a little girl was walking through the wood. She had long yellow hair that shone like gold in the sun. People called her Goldilocks. She lived in a village far away, and she was out for a walk. She was not supposed to go far from home. But she was a curious girl, and she liked to see new places. She had walked all morning, and she had no idea where she was.

She came to a little house. It stood in a clearing, with flowers in front of the door. A thin line of smoke came from the chimney, and a good smell came from inside.

"What a pretty house," said Goldilocks. "I wonder who lives here."

She went up to the window and looked in. She could see a table with three pots on it. Steam was rising from them. The room was empty. Then she went to the door and looked through the keyhole. She saw nobody inside.

She knocked. Nobody came.

She knocked again, louder. Still nobody came.

Goldilocks knew what she should do. She should go away, and she should wait for the people to come home. But she was hungry, and the smell was so good. She put her hand on the latch, and she lifted it. The door was not locked. It opened slowly, with a small sound.

"Hello?" she called. "Is anybody home?"

There was no answer. The little house was quiet.

"Well," said Goldilocks. She stepped inside. "I will wait here for a moment. I will not touch anything."

But she looked at the table. There were three pots of porridge. They were a great pot, a middle-sized pot, and a little blue pot. The porridge was golden and creamy, and it smelled of honey. Her stomach made a loud noise.

"Just one little taste," she said.

She went to the great pot first. She picked up the big wooden spoon, and she took a mouthful.

"Ouch!" she said. "That is much too hot!"

She put the spoon down. Then she tried the porridge in the middle-sized pot. She took a spoonful and put it in her mouth.

"Ugh," she said. "That is too cold!"

She looked at the little blue pot. She took the little spoon, and she tasted the porridge.

"Oh!" she said. Her face became bright. "That is not too hot, and it is not too cold. It is just right!"

It tasted so good that she could not stop. She ate one spoonful, and then another, and then another. In a few minutes, the little pot was empty. She had eaten every bit.

"That was delicious," she said. She wiped her mouth. "I wish that there was more."

Then she looked at the room. There were three chairs near the fireplace. She was tired from her long walk, and her feet hurt. She went to the first chair, the great chair. She climbed up on it. It was so big that her feet did not touch the floor.

"This chair is too hard," she said. She slid down. "It is like sitting on a rock."

She went to the second chair, the middle-sized chair. She sat down on the cushion. It was so soft that she sank into it.

"Oh dear," she said. "This one is too soft. I cannot get out."

She struggled, and she climbed out of the soft chair. At last, she came to the third chair, the little chair. It was made of light wood, and it was pretty. She sat down in it.

"This one is neither too hard nor too soft," she said. "It is just right!"

She was very comfortable. She began to swing her legs. She leaned back and she moved from side to side. But the little chair was small. It was built for a Little, Small, Wee Bear, not for a girl. It made a sharp noise: crack! Then the seat came out, and Goldilocks fell, plump, on the floor.

"Oh!" she said. She sat there with her mouth open. She looked at the broken chair. The seat lay on the floor, and the legs stood up in the air.

"Oh no," she said. "I did not mean to do that."

She looked at the door. She thought, "I should go home now." But she was tired. She was very, very tired. And there was a staircase in the corner of the room.
