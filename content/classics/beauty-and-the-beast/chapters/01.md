# A Rich Man Becomes Poor

Once upon a time, in a country far away, there lived a rich merchant. He had big ships that carried silk and spices across the sea. He had a large house in the city with a garden, many servants, and fine horses in the stable. He was a kind man, and he worked hard. All his business went well, and every year he became a little richer.

The merchant had six children: three sons and three daughters. His wife had died many years before. He loved all his children, and he gave them everything. They had the best food and the best clothes. They had teachers and books and musical instruments. Every year, there were parties in the house, and the rooms were full of friends.

The three daughters were all pretty. But the youngest was the loveliest of all. When she was small, everyone called her "the little beauty." As she grew up, the name stayed with her. Soon, nobody remembered her real name. They called her Beauty, and she was happy with it.

But Beauty was not only pretty. She was also good. She had a gentle heart and a clear mind. She loved to read, and to walk by herself in the garden, and to play the piano in the evening. She was kind to the servants and to the poor people who came to the door. She never said an angry word.

Her two sisters were different. They were proud and selfish. They thought only of themselves and of their beautiful clothes. They laughed at poor people. They did not want to talk to the daughters of smaller merchants. They went to every party in the city, and they wore a new dress each time.

"Beauty is so dull," they said. "She stays home and reads books. She does not care about fashion. She will never find a husband."

"I do not need a husband," said Beauty with a smile. "I am happy with my father and my books."

Many rich young men wanted to marry her. But she said, "I am too young. I want to stay with my father a few more years." Her sisters were angry about this. They thought that she was only showing off.

Then, one terrible day, everything changed.

The merchant's ships were far away at sea. A great storm came and sank the first ship. A week later, another ship was lost to pirates. Then a fire started at night in the big warehouse by the harbor, and it burned all the goods. One bad piece of news came after another, like waves. The merchant sat in his study and read the letters. His hands were shaking.

"I have lost everything," he said at last. "Every ship. Every box. I have nothing left but this house."

But even the house was not safe. He owed money to many people. To pay them, he had to sell the house and all that was in it. The beautiful furniture, the horses, the pictures on the walls, and the piano all went to other people.

At the end, there was only a small cottage in the country. It stood far away from the city, at the edge of a dark forest. It had been in the family for a long time, but nobody had lived in it for years. It was the only place that was left for them.

"We must go there," said the merchant to his children. "It will be hard. We will have to work with our hands. But we are still together, and that is the most important thing."

The two oldest daughters began to cry. "We cannot live like peasants!" they said. "We have always had everything! Our friends will help us. They will not let us go away."

But when the family lost its money, the friends also went away. Nobody came to say goodbye. Nobody offered help. Some people even said that the merchant's children had spent too much money and that they deserved it.

So the merchant and his six children went to the cottage in the forest. It was small and cold. The roof was old, and the floor was made of earth. There were two little rooms upstairs and one big room downstairs. The garden was full of weeds. There was no servant, no cook, and no cart. Everyone had to work.

The three sons went out each day to the fields, and they worked in the hard ground. The merchant mended the roof and the doors. The two oldest daughters sat by the fire and cried. They did not want to touch the pots. They said that they were too tired, and that it was too sad.

Only Beauty got up early. She put on a plain brown dress and tied back her hair. She lit the fire. She swept the floor and washed the dishes and cooked the meals. She brought water from the well and baked the bread. At first, she was tired, and her hands hurt. She cried a little on the first night.

But in the morning, she said to herself, "Crying will not bring our money back. I will be happy. I will make this a good home."
