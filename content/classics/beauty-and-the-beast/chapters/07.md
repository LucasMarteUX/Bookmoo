# A Week at Home

The next evening, when the Beast came to supper, Beauty did not eat. Her eyes were red. She sat with her hands on her knees, and she looked at the table.

"What is the matter, Beauty?" said the Beast.

"Beast," she said, "I have a favor to ask. I do not know if you will say yes. I saw my father in the mirror. He is very ill. He is lying in bed, and he is calling my name. I think that he is dying of sorrow because I am gone. I would like to see him once more. Please let me go home."

The Beast did not answer. He looked at the floor. After a long time, he said, in a heavy voice, "Beauty, I would rather die than make you unhappy. But if you go home, I think that I will die of grief."

"Oh, no," said Beauty. She put her hand to her mouth. "I do not want that. I promise that I will come back. I only want to see my father, and tell him that I am well. My sisters are married now, and my brothers are soldiers. My father is alone. Let me stay with him for one week. Then I will return. I give you my word."

The Beast looked at her. It was clear that he suffered. At last, he said, "You will go tomorrow. You will stay for a week. Not one day longer. If you do not return, I will know that you do not want to return. Then I will die."

"I will return," said Beauty. "I promise."

"Here," said the Beast. He took a ring from his finger. It was a small gold ring with a bright red stone. "Put this on the table by your bed tonight. In the morning, when you wake up, you will be in your father's house. When you want to come back, you only have to put the ring on your finger, and say, 'I wish to go back to my Beast.' It will bring you here. Good night, Beauty. Do not be afraid. Sleep well."

He left the room.

That night, Beauty put the ring on the table by her bed. She closed her eyes. When she woke up, she was no longer in the castle. She was lying in a small bed in her father's cottage, and the sun was shining through the window.

She jumped up. She ran downstairs. Her father was sitting by the fire in his old chair. He was thin and gray, but he looked at her, and for a moment he did not believe what he saw. Then he cried out. He opened his arms.

"Beauty! My child!"

She ran into them. They held each other for a long time. They both were crying, and they did not say anything. When they were quiet, he looked at her face and touched her hair.

"You are not hurt?" he said. "The Beast has not eaten you?"

"No, Father," she said. "He has been kind to me. I live in a beautiful room. I have everything that I want. I read, and I play the harpsichord, and I walk in the garden. He is not what he looks like. He has a gentle heart."

Her father could hardly believe it. But he saw that her face was bright, and her cheeks were rosy. She looked healthy and happy. He felt that a great weight was lifted from his heart. In a few hours, he was out of bed, and by the evening, he was sitting at the table and eating a good dinner.

Beauty told them everything. Her brothers were away, but her sisters came to visit the next day. They had married rich men some months earlier, and they lived in the town not far away. But neither of them was happy.

The oldest sister had married a young man who was very handsome. But he was so vain that he thought only of his own face. He looked into the mirror all day, and he did not speak to his wife. The second had married a clever man, who made jokes all day long. But his jokes were unkind. He laughed at everyone, and most of all at his wife.

When the sisters came into the cottage, they saw Beauty in a beautiful dress, and her face was shining and happy. They were very angry. They did not say so. They only smiled and kissed her. But when they were alone in the garden, they began to talk in low voices.

"Look at her," said the oldest. "She is more beautiful than ever. And she wears silk. She has been living like a princess, while we have such husbands."

"She is very happy," said the second. "It is not fair. She was the poor one, the one who worked in the kitchen. Now she has a castle, and we have nothing."

"We must do something," said the oldest. "She has promised to go back in a week. If she does not go back at the end of the week, the Beast will be angry. He will come here and eat her."

"Yes," said the second. A cruel smile came on her face. "Yes. We must make her stay. We will be very kind. We will cry when she says goodbye. We will say that we cannot bear to lose her. Then she will not have the heart to leave. And the Beast will tear her to pieces."

They went back into the house, and they smiled sweetly at their sister.
