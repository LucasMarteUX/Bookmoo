# The Sisters' Trick

The week went by very fast. Beauty was happy at home. She sat with her father in the evenings, and she told him about the castle and the roses and the invisible voices. She went for long walks with her brothers when they came for a visit. She cooked a good dinner for everyone, and she laughed at the old jokes of her family. She felt that she was a child again.

But she did not forget the Beast. Every night, before she went to sleep, she looked at the ring on the table. She thought, "Seven days. I said seven days. I must be back on time."

On the seventh day, she began to pack. She put on her plain brown dress, and she folded the silk one in a bag. Her father stood at the door and watched her. He was sad, but he did not say anything. He knew that she had made a promise.

Then her two sisters came in. They looked very sad. They had red eyes, and their noses were red too. The truth is that they had rubbed their eyes with onions, and they were weeping real tears.

"Beauty!" said the oldest. She threw her arms around her. "You cannot go! We cannot bear it! We have been so happy to have you here!"

"It is only one week," said the second, and she sobbed. "Stay a little longer. Only a few days. We will miss you terribly."

"Please, Beauty," said the oldest. "Please. We never had a chance to talk. We want to know you better. We want to be good sisters to you."

Beauty looked at them. She had never seen them cry. She had never heard them say such kind words. A voice inside her said, "Why are they so kind now?" But she was soft-hearted, and she could not bear to see her sisters weep.

"I promised the Beast that I would be back in a week," she said. "He is kind and gentle. He has been good to me."

"But what will happen if you stay one day longer?" said the second. "He will not be angry for one day. He is a beast, but he cannot be so unreasonable. Stay until tomorrow. Then you can go."

"One day?" said Beauty.

"Only one day," said the oldest.

"All right," said Beauty, after a moment. "One more day. But no more."

The next day, the sisters were even more loving. They said that their husbands were cruel to them, and they needed their little sister. On the third day, they brought sweets from the town. On the fourth day, they said that the oldest sister was ill and wanted her sister to nurse her. Every day, there was a new reason. Every day, Beauty said, "Only one more." She did not want to hurt her sisters' feelings. She did not want to leave her father when he was still thin and weak.

Slowly, the days went by. It was ten days, and then it was twelve. Beauty did not think about the number. She had so many things to do. But sometimes, she looked at the ring, and a small cold feeling came into her heart.

On the tenth night, she had a dream.

She was in the garden of the castle. It was night, and there was no moon. She walked along the avenue of orange trees, and everything was very still. The fountains did not sound. The birds did not sing. The flowers had closed their petals.

She walked to the end of the garden, to a place that she had never seen. There was a small cave, with a heap of dry leaves in it. And on the leaves, there was something large and dark. It was the Beast. He lay on his side, and his eyes were closed. He was not breathing. He was cold as stone.

"Beast!" she cried. She fell on her knees. She took his big head in her arms. "Beast, what has happened to you?"

His eyes opened slowly. They were dull and sad. His voice was no more than a whisper.

"Beauty," he said. "You did not come back. You said one week. I waited, and I waited. I have not eaten for days. I have not wanted to live. I told you that I would die of grief. And now I am dying."

"No!" she cried. "Do not die! I am here!"

But the dream faded. The Beast and the cave and the dark garden became grey and thin. She tried to hold on to them, but they were gone.

Beauty woke up with a scream. She was in her bed in the cottage, and the first light was coming through the window. Her face was wet with tears. Her heart was beating so hard that she could hear it.

She sat up. She looked at the table. The ring lay there, shining. And at that moment, she understood how wrong she had been.

"What have I done?" she said. "How could I be so cruel to someone who was so good to me? Is it his fault that he is ugly? He is kind. He is gentle. He gave me a room and a garden. He gave me my father back. I do not want to marry him, but it is not because he is a bad man. It is only that I do not love him in that way. But I love him as a friend. And a friend should not die of grief."

She got out of bed and dressed. Her hands were shaking. She wrote a letter to her father, and she left it on the table. In the letter, she said, "Do not be sad. I will come to see you again soon. I must go back to the Beast. He needs me."

Then she took the ring. She put it on her finger.

"I wish," she said, "to go back to my Beast."
