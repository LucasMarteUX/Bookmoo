# The Beast in the Garden

Beauty closed her eyes. She felt a rush of wind, and a soft sound like many wings. When she opened her eyes, she was in her room in the castle, in her own bed, with white curtains around it. It was dark outside. She heard the clock of the castle strike twelve.

She jumped up. She put on her dress and ran out into the corridor. "Beast!" she called. "Beast!"

Nobody answered. The castle was dark. There was no fire in the fireplaces and no light in the halls. There was no sound of music. There were no invisible voices to say good evening. The silence was so deep that it frightened her.

She ran from room to room. She looked in the dining hall, where the Beast sat every evening. The table was empty. She looked in the library. She looked in the great hall. She ran up the stairs and down again. She called his name until her voice was weak.

"He is dead," she said. "I have killed him."

Then she thought of her dream. She remembered the garden, and the dark cave at the end of the avenue. "Perhaps," she said. "Perhaps it was not only a dream."

She took a candle, and she ran out of the castle. The garden was black and quiet. The flowers were closed. The fountains were still. Her feet made no sound in the grass. She walked as fast as she could along the paths, between the hedges of roses and the orange trees. She had a strange feeling that she knew the way.

At the end of the garden, she saw the cave.

It was very small. It was hidden behind a rock, in a place that she had never visited. And in it, on a heap of dry leaves, lay the Beast. He was exactly as she had seen him in her dream. He lay on his side with his eyes closed. He did not move.

"Oh!" she cried. She dropped the candle. It rolled away and went out. She fell on her knees beside him. "Beast! Beast, I am here! I have come back!"

She touched his face. It was cold. She put her ear to his chest. For a moment, she heard nothing. Her whole body was cold with fear. Then, very faintly, deep inside him, she heard a beat. It was slow and weak, but it was there.

"He is alive," she whispered. "Oh, thank God! He is alive!"

She ran to a fountain nearby. She filled her hands with water, and she brought it back, and she poured it gently on his face. She rubbed his hands. She spoke to him softly. She said his name again and again.

The Beast opened his eyes. They were dull and tired. For a long time, he looked at her without understanding. Then he whispered, "Beauty?"

"Yes. It is me."

"You came back," he said. He could hardly speak. "You said a week. It was too long. I was so lonely. I thought that you would never come. I did not want to eat. I did not want to live. I knew that I should die of grief. But now I can die happy, because I have seen you again."

"No," said Beauty. She held his big paw in her two hands. "You will not die. You will live, and you will be my friend. I cannot bear to lose you. I did not know how much I loved you until I almost lost you."

"Do you mean that?" said the Beast.

"I do," said Beauty. "I have been so foolish. I thought that I did not love you because you are ugly. But it is not true. Your face does not matter. You are good, and you are kind, and I would rather have you than all the handsome and clever men in the world. I am not ashamed of you. I love you."

Her tears fell on his face. She bent down and kissed his rough cheek.

The Beast looked at her. His voice was very small. "Beauty," he said, "will you marry me?"

"Yes, Beast," she said. "I will. I will marry you."

For a moment, nothing happened. The Beast did not move. The wind did not blow. Then, quite suddenly, there was a great light.

It was as bright as the sun. It came from every side. The whole castle was shining. Beauty heard a sound of music, a great noise of trumpets, and she saw fireworks in the sky. Every window of the castle was lit with golden light. Every tree in the garden was full of little flames.

She put her hands over her eyes. She did not know what was happening. She only knew that she was not afraid.

When she looked again, the Beast was gone.

A young man lay on the leaves in his place. He was tall and slender, and he wore a coat of blue velvet. His face was handsome and pale. His hair was dark, and his eyes were gentle and brown. He was looking at her with so much love that she could hardly breathe.

"Beauty," he said.
