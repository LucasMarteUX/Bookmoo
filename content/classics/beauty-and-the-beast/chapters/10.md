# The Prince

Beauty stared at the young man. She could not speak. She looked at his face, and she looked at the place where the Beast had lain, and she looked at his face again. She did not understand.

"Where is my Beast?" she said at last. Her voice was a whisper. "What has happened to him? Who are you?"

The young man sat up. He took her hand in both of his. His eyes were bright with tears.

"I am the Beast," he said. "Or I was. A wicked fairy put a spell on me, long ago. I was a young prince then, and I was proud and cruel. She told me that I would stay in this ugly shape until a beautiful girl agreed to marry me in spite of my face. It seemed impossible. For many years, I lived here alone. I forgot how to be a man. I forgot how to be kind. And then you came."

"Me?" said Beauty.

"Yes, you," said the prince. "You came to this castle to save your father. You were not afraid of my face. You gave me your friendship, and then your love. You could not know that I was a prince. You only knew that I was a beast, and you loved me anyway. Your goodness has broken the spell."

Beauty looked at him. She was crying, but she was laughing too. She did not know what to say. She put out her hand and touched his face. It was warm and real.

"I do not care if you are a prince," she said. "I loved you as a beast. I will love you as a man."

"And I will love you for all my life," said the prince.

They sat together on the leaves for a long time, with the golden light all around them. Then, at last, they heard a sound behind them. It was the sound of a gentle voice.

"Well done, Beauty."

They turned round. A woman stood in the garden. She was tall and beautiful, and she wore a white dress that seemed to be made of light. Her hair was long and silver. Her face was kind, and her eyes were very bright. Beauty knew her at once. It was the lady that she had seen in her dream in the castle on her first night, the one who had said, "You will not be sorry for your good heart."

"You are the fairy," said Beauty.

"I am," said the lady. She smiled, and she walked toward them. "I have watched you for a long time. I saw you go away from this castle, and I saw you come back. You chose the right thing, although it was hard. You did not choose a man because he was handsome, or rich, or clever. You chose a good heart. That is the best way to choose, and the most rare. You have your reward."

"Thank you," said Beauty.

"Do not thank me," said the fairy. "It was your own goodness that did this. I only watched and waited." She turned to the prince. "And you, young man. You were punished for your pride. Remember what it cost you. A person is not only the shape that he has. Be kind to everyone, and be a good husband."

"I will," said the prince, and he bowed.

The fairy raised her hand. All around them, the castle began to move and change. From every door, people came out. There were servants in blue and gold. There were ladies and gentlemen in fine clothes. There were cooks and gardeners, and grooms and guards. They were the people of the court, who had been turned into invisible hands and voices by the spell. Now they were free. They cheered, and they ran to the prince, and they knelt before him, and they cried for joy.

"It is over," said the prince. "I am a man again, and all of you are free."

The orange trees shook their flowers. The fountains began to play. The birds sang. In the great hall, the lamps were lit, one by one, and the music of a hundred instruments began.

"Come with me, Beauty," said the prince. "Let me show you my palace as it should be seen."

He gave her his arm, and they walked together to the castle.

But the fairy did not follow at once. She stood in the garden, and she looked thoughtfully at the dark sky. She said quietly, "There is one more thing to settle." And she raised her hand again.

Far away, in two small houses in the town, two women were asleep in their beds. They were two sisters. They had cried false tears, and they had told lies. They had wished to keep their sister from a happy ending. They were fast asleep, and they did not know what was coming.
