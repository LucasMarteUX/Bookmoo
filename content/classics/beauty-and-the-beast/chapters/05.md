# Beauty Goes to the Castle

The merchant did not want to think about the chest. He put it in a corner of the room, and he did not open it for many days. When at last he did, he found that it was full of gold, and he took out a few coins. He did not say a word about it to his children, because he thought that his oldest daughters would ask to go back to the city. But one night, when his daughters had gone to bed, Beauty found him sitting beside the chest.

"Father," she said, "I know what you are thinking. But the money is for your family. Do not be afraid to use it. You will need it."

"My dear child," said the merchant, "I have it only because I sold you."

"No, you did not sell me," said Beauty. "I am going of my own will. I chose it. I want you to be happy."

So the merchant told his children about the gold. As he feared, the two oldest daughters at once asked to go back to the city. "We have a fortune!" they said. "We can live like ladies again. Let us leave this miserable place!" But the merchant said, "We will go when Beauty has gone." And this made them quiet.

The three months passed quickly. At first, the days seemed long. Then they seemed to fly. Beauty worked as usual. She baked bread. She mended clothes. She sat with her father in the evenings, and they talked about many things. She tried to smile and keep her heart light.

On the last day, she cooked a special dinner. She put a clean white cloth on the table. She lit candles. When her brothers came in from the field, they saw her in her best dress, with the rose in her hair. They did not know what to say. One of them cried. The father did not eat a single bite.

In the morning, they said goodbye. The merchant and his three sons put Beauty on a horse. They rode with her, all four, along the forest road, and they did not speak much. The two sisters stood at the door and waved. They rubbed their eyes with onions to look sad, but they were smiling behind their hands.

When they came to the castle avenue, the snow stopped. The wind went away. The orange trees were in flower, and the sun was shining. Beauty looked up at the great white castle on the hill. She felt a little less afraid. It was so beautiful.

"It is a very kind place for a monster to live," she said.

"Do not be deceived," said her father. His voice was sad. "He is not what he seems."

At the gate, Beauty got down from her horse. She hugged her father and her brothers, one by one. "Do not be sad," she said. "I will be all right."

The merchant could not speak. He held her for a long time. Then her brothers took him away. Beauty stood in the great court, and she watched them go. When the last one was gone, she turned around, and she went up the steps and into the castle.

The doors closed behind her.

She stood in the great hall, alone. She expected the Beast to appear at once, and her heart was beating fast. But nobody came. She waited for a moment, and then she began to walk slowly through the castle.

It was the most beautiful house she had ever seen. There were wide stairs of white stone, and long galleries with painted ceilings. There were rooms with silk hangings, and rooms with mirrors from floor to ceiling, and rooms with gold chairs. In every room, a fire was burning. A smell of flowers was in the air. Soft music seemed to come from somewhere far away.

At last, she came to a door with a sign above it. The sign was written in letters of gold: BEAUTY'S ROOM.

She opened the door. She stood still.

The room was large and bright. It was a lovely room, with a bed with white curtains, and a desk, and a window that looked over the garden. There were chairs by the fireplace. And along one wall, there was a great bookcase, full of books. There was also a harpsichord, and a pile of music.

"But how can this be?" said Beauty. "This is for me? The Beast does not intend to eat me right away? He has prepared a room for me!"

She walked to the bookcase. On a shelf, there was a big book, bound in gold. She took it down and opened it. On the first page, in letters of gold, she read:

Your wishes shall be commanded. Here you are queen and mistress. Say what you desire. Everything will be done.

Beauty read the words twice. "What a strange place," she said. "I only wish that I could see my poor father. I wish that I knew what he is doing now."

She had spoken only to herself. But as she turned to the mirror that hung on the wall, she saw something moving in it. It was her father's house. She saw him arriving at the cottage on his horse. His face was very sad. His sons stood around him. Then the picture faded, and she saw only her own face.

Beauty stood quite still. "It is magic," she said. "But it is kind magic. I think that I will not be so afraid here."

That evening, at nine o'clock, a table was prepared in the dining room. The food was delicious. But she did not see anyone who served it. The plates seemed to come by themselves. A soft voice said, "Good evening," and she looked around, but nobody was there.

She ate a little. She was not very hungry. When she finished, she heard a heavy step in the hall. She sat very still, and her hands began to shake.

The door opened. The Beast came in.
