# Through the Roses

The next morning, the prince got up before the sun. He thanked the innkeeper and paid for his bed. The old man was already sitting by the fire, and he did not say anything. He only lifted his hand in a small goodbye. The prince took his horse, and he rode out of the village toward the hills.

It was a clear spring morning. The grass was wet, and the sky was pale blue. He rode for two hours, up a long hill and down the other side. At last, he came to the top of a small ridge. He stopped, and he looked down.

In front of him, in the middle of a green valley, there was a great hedge. It was bigger than he thought. It was as high as a mountain, and it was as wide as a village. It was dark green, and it shone in the sun. The branches were thick and twisted, and the thorns were long as a man's finger. But on the top, and on every branch, there were flowers. They were white and pink, and there were thousands of them.

"How beautiful," said the prince. He did not expect it. He thought that he would see something black and dead. Instead, it was like a garden.

He got off his horse, and he tied it to a tree. Then he walked slowly down the hill, toward the hedge.

As he came closer, he saw that the ground was full of old things. There were rusty swords in the grass. There were pieces of helmets, and broken shields, and old gold buttons. They were the things of the princes who had come before him. He stepped carefully around them. He felt a chill. But he did not turn back.

He stood in front of the hedge. It was very quiet. He could hear the bees in the flowers. He could hear his own heart beating.

"Well," he said, "here I am."

He put his hand out slowly, and he touched a branch. He waited for the thorns to hurt him. But nothing happened. The branch moved a little in his hand, like a living thing. Then, to his great surprise, it bent back. The thorns closed, and the flowers opened wider. The whole hedge began to move. The branches pulled apart, slowly, and in front of the prince, a narrow path appeared. It was a green tunnel full of white and pink flowers, and at the end of it, there was a light.

The prince stood for a moment and could not believe what he saw. He thought of the old man's words, and of all the princes who had died here. But this morning, the hedge did not want to kill him. It was welcoming him. For the hundredth year had ended, and the thorns had turned into roses.

He took a deep breath, and he stepped into the green tunnel.

The flowers brushed his face and his hands as he walked. They smelled sweet and fresh. Behind him, the branches closed again, as thick and wild as before, and nobody could follow. But he did not look back. He only walked on, step by step, with his eyes on the light ahead.

After a long time, the tunnel ended. He came out onto a wide stone court, in front of a great castle.

The prince stopped. He had never seen a place so quiet. The sun shone on the stones, and nothing moved. In the middle of the court, a big brown dog lay on the ground with its head on its paws. Near the door of the stable, four horses stood with their heads down. On the roof of the castle, a great number of white pigeons sat in a row, and each of them had its head under its wing. It was as if someone had made a picture and then forgotten to finish it.

The prince walked on. He saw an open door, and he went into the castle. He walked through a long hall. On every side, there were people, and all of them were asleep. A soldier leaned on his spear. A servant stood with a tray in his hands. A lady sat on a bench with a book on her lap. Their clothes were old-fashioned, and they were covered with a thin layer of dust. They looked like statues. But when he listened, he heard that each of them was breathing softly.

In the kitchen, the fire in the big stove was out, but the pot over it still hung on its hook. The spit with the goose stood still, and the goose was golden brown. The cook stood with her hand up. In front of her, a kitchen boy stood with his shoulders pulled up, and his eyes were closed as if he waited for a blow. In a corner, a maid held a chicken in her lap.

In the cellar, the prince found the butler. He sat on a barrel with a cup at his lips. The prince looked at him and smiled, but then he felt a pang of fear. He walked faster. It was so quiet that he could hear himself breathe.

In the great hall, he found a king and a queen. They sat on the floor close together, with their arms around each other. They were old, much older than the people who slept around them. But they, too, were asleep, and a small smile was on their faces.

"Where is the princess?" the prince whispered. "Where is Briar Rose?"

Nobody answered. But he looked at the stairs, and he felt that they were calling him. He began to climb.

He went up and up. He passed one floor and another. At last, he found an old tower. It had a narrow turning staircase. He climbed it slowly, with his hand on the cold wall. At the top, he found a small door.

It stood a little open. A little light came from it.

He put out his hand, and he pushed it softly.
