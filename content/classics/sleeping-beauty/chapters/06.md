# The Princes and the Thorns

The story of the sleeping princess did not stay in the village. It went along the roads, from town to town and from country to country. Travelers told it in inns. Sailors told it on ships. Old women told it to their grandchildren by the fire. In time, it came to the ears of every young prince in the land.

"A beautiful girl sleeps behind a wall of thorns," they said. "She is the most lovely woman in the world. And anyone who goes through the hedge can wake her and marry her."

Of course, many young men wanted to try.

The first prince came when the hedge was only twenty years old. He was a brave and proud young man. He rode up to the thorns on a tall black horse, and he looked at them for a long time.

"This is only a hedge," he said. "I have fought in wars. I have killed wolves. I am not afraid of some little plants."

He took out his sword, and he began to cut. He cut a branch, and then another. At first, it was easy. The branches fell to the ground, and the hedge seemed to open. The prince smiled. He pushed his horse forward into the green.

But the thorns closed behind him. They moved by themselves. They reached out like long arms, and they took hold of his coat. They took hold of his sword and his hands and his hair. The more he pulled, the tighter they held. He shouted. His horse ran away. The thorns pulled him in, deeper and deeper, and there was nothing that he could do. He was caught there, and he never came out again.

A year later, a second prince came. He was a clever young man, and he said, "I will not fight the hedge. I will use fire." He brought a big torch, and he put it to the thorns. For a moment, the leaves burned bright. But the green branches did not catch fire. They were wet and thick. The fire went out, and the thorns closed around him as they had closed around the first. He was caught there too.

After that, others came. A third prince tried to climb over the top of the hedge, but it grew faster than he could climb. A fourth tried to dig under it, but the roots were too deep. A fifth brought a hundred workers with axes, but the thorns grew back at night as fast as the men could cut them in the day. In the end, the men ran away in fear.

Year after year, more princes came, and they all had the same end. They went into the hedge, and they never came out. Soon people said that the hedge was cursed. They said that no one could pass. Fathers told their sons, "Stay away from the thorns." Mothers told their daughters, "Do not go near the sleeping castle."

The years went by. The hedge became so thick and high that nobody could tell what lay behind it. It was a green wall, wide as a hill, and in the spring it was covered with small white flowers. In the autumn, it was full of dark red berries. Birds made their nests in it. Foxes lived under it. But nobody went inside.

A long time passed. The people who first heard the story were dead, and their children were dead too. Then the grandchildren's children grew old. The kings who ruled the country were changed, and the roads were changed, and even the language that people spoke was a little different. But the old song was still sung in the villages:

Briar Rose, Briar Rose,
Sleeping in the thorns,
When will she wake up?
When will the rose bloom?

Nobody knew the answer. Nobody, that is, except the twelfth wise woman. She was very old now, but she had not forgotten the child. In a quiet house far away, she looked at the calendar, and she counted the years. She smiled a thin smile.

"Soon," she said to herself. "It will be soon."

Slowly, the hundredth year came near.

On the first day of the last spring, the snow melted on the hills. The ground became soft. Small green leaves came out on the thorn bush, and little white flowers opened, one by one. The air smelled of apple blossoms. A strange feeling was in the wind. The birds sang louder. The sleeping castle seemed to listen.

Inside the castle, the dust lay still. But in the little room in the tower, the light on the floor was shining a little brighter.
