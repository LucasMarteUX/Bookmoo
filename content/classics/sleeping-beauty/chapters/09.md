# The Kiss and the Wedding

The room was small, and it was full of golden light. In the corner, an old woman sat at a spinning wheel. Her head was bent, and her hands lay still in her lap. On the floor next to her, there was a thin spindle.

And on a couch under the window, there lay a girl.

Somebody had laid her there, long ago, on a soft cushion. She was lying on her side with one hand under her cheek. She wore a pink dress, and her long golden hair lay around her like a cloth. Her face was calm. Her lips were a little open, and her cheeks were rosy. She looked as if she had fallen asleep only a minute ago.

The prince stood in the door and could not move. He looked at her, and he forgot to breathe. He had seen many people in his life, but he had never seen anyone like this. She was not just beautiful. It was something in her face that was kind and gentle and good, and it made his heart ache.

"Briar Rose," he said softly.

He walked across the room, and he knelt next to the couch. He looked at her for a long time. The sunlight made a warm line across her face. He could see a small, round mark on her finger, as small as the head of a pin.

"A hundred years," he said. "How long you have waited."

He could not take his eyes from her. He wanted to say something, and he did not know what. He did not want to wake her roughly. Then, slowly, he bent down, and he kissed her.

For a moment, nothing happened.

Then Briar Rose opened her eyes.

She looked at the ceiling, and then she looked at the prince. Her eyes were blue and deep, and they were full of surprise. She smiled, a slow, sleepy smile, like someone who wakes up from a good dream.

"Oh," she said, "it is you. I knew that you would come. I saw you in my dream."

The prince took her hand. "You have slept for a very long time," he said. "Do you know where you are?"

"In my tower," said Briar Rose. She sat up, and she looked around. "There was an old woman... a spindle... I pricked my finger. I remember. And then there was a very long dream." She looked at him, and her cheeks became pink. "But who are you? I do not know you."

"I am a prince from a faraway land," he said. "I heard about you, and I came to see you. I am glad that I did."

She smiled again, and she stood up. Her legs were a little shaky, but the prince held her arm. They went out of the little room together, and they walked slowly down the stairs.

At that same moment, a great thing happened in the castle.

In the great hall, the king opened his eyes. He saw the queen's face next to his, and she opened her eyes too. For a moment, they looked at each other and could not speak. Then they looked up, and they saw their daughter coming down the stairs with a young man. Both of them cried out in joy.

"Briar Rose!" said the queen. "My child!"

They ran to her, and they held her in their arms. They laughed and cried at the same time. The king shook the prince's hand again and again. "Who are you?" he asked. "Where do you come from? How did you pass the hedge?"

All around them, the castle was waking up.

In the courtyard, the horses shook their heads, and they stamped their feet. The dogs jumped up and barked. The pigeons took their heads from under their wings, looked around, and flew over the fields. In the garden, the bees began to buzz again. Flies on the walls began to move. In the kitchen, the fire in the stove sprang up, and the flames danced bright and red. The spit began to turn, and the goose began to sizzle. The butler finished his drink, and he looked around with big eyes. The maid went on with her chicken. And the cook, who still had her hand in the air, gave the kitchen boy the blow that she had wanted to give him a hundred years before.

"Ow!" said the kitchen boy.

"That is for being so lazy," said the cook. She stopped, and she looked around. "Where is everybody? What time is it? Why is it so dusty in here?"

The people of the castle looked at one another. They did not understand. They only knew that they had slept, and that something wonderful had happened. They hugged each other, and they laughed, and a few of them cried.

The king and the queen took Briar Rose and the prince into the great hall. They sat on their old thrones, and they listened to the whole story. When the prince had finished, the king stood up. His eyes were wet.

"My son," he said, "you have given me back my daughter. You have given me back my home and my people. I do not know how to thank you."

"There is only one thing that I want," said the prince. He looked at Briar Rose. "If she will agree, I would like to marry her."

Briar Rose looked at him. She was not afraid, and she was not sorry. She felt that she had known him for a long time.

"Yes," she said. "I will."

The wedding was held a few days later. It was the most beautiful wedding that anyone had ever seen. The castle was cleaned and decorated with flowers. Musicians played, and people danced in the garden. The twelve wise women came, and the oldest of them, who was now very, very old, sat next to the queen and smiled. And, strangely, the black-capped one did not come. Nobody ever saw her again.

On the evening of the wedding, the prince and Briar Rose stood on the top of the old tower. They looked down at the garden. The hedge of thorns was still there, but it was changed. It was a great wall of roses. Thousands of them, red and white and pink, were in bloom, and the sweet smell came up to the tower in the warm air.

"Will it ever be a thorn hedge again?" asked the prince.

"No," said Briar Rose. She put her head on his shoulder. "It has done its work."

They lived together for many, many years, in that castle, with the king and the queen. They had children and grandchildren. They were happy every day of their lives. And every year, on the day that the hedge first bloomed, the whole country came to the castle for a great feast, and the people sang the old song:

Briar Rose, Briar Rose,
Sleeping in the thorns,
Now she is awake,
And the rose has bloomed.
