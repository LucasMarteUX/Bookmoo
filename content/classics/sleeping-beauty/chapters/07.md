# The Prince and the Old Man

On the first day of that spring, a young prince came riding into the country. He was the son of a king from a land beyond the mountains. He was tall and strong, and he had a kind, open face. He did not carry a big sword or a golden shield. He wore plain traveling clothes, and his horse was brown and tired.

The prince was traveling to see the world. He had left his father's castle in the winter, and he had ridden over many roads. He had slept in inns and in barns. He had eaten bread and cheese by the road. He liked it very much. For the first time, he could do what he wanted, and nobody told him what to do.

One evening, he came to a small village at the foot of some hills. He stopped at the inn, and he asked for a room. In the common room of the inn, an old man sat near the fire. He was very, very old. His hair was white, and his face was full of lines, and his hands shook a little. But his eyes were bright, and he looked at the prince with interest.

"You are a stranger here," said the old man.

"I am," said the prince. He sat down at the table, and the innkeeper brought him a bowl of soup. "I come from far away. I am traveling to see the country. Is there anything here that I should see?"

The old man smiled slowly. "There is something," he said. "But I do not know if you will want to see it."

"Tell me," said the prince.

The old man leaned forward. "Beyond those hills," he said, "there is a hedge of thorns. It is the highest and thickest hedge in the world. Behind the hedge, there is a castle, and in the castle, they say, there is a princess who has slept for a hundred years. Her name is Briar Rose."

The prince put down his spoon. "A sleeping princess?" he said. "How can that be?"

So the old man told him the whole story. He told him about the king and the queen and the golden plates. He told him about the thirteenth wise woman and her curse. He told him about the spindle, and the long sleep, and the thorns. His voice was soft and slow, and the people in the inn became quiet to listen.

"My grandfather told me this story," said the old man at the end. "And his grandfather told it to him. And he told me something else. He said that many, many princes tried to pass the thorns. They were brave and strong. But the hedge held them fast, and they all died there. Many of their bones are still in the hedge."

The prince was silent for a long time. He looked into the fire.

"And nobody has ever come through?" he said.

"Nobody," said the old man.

"And nobody has ever seen the princess?"

"Nobody has seen her for a hundred years," said the old man. "She is only a story now."

The innkeeper's wife came and put more wood on the fire. The other men in the room began to talk of other things. But the prince sat very still. He felt something in his heart that he did not understand. It was a kind of pull, like a rope that goes from your chest to a faraway place.

"Is she beautiful?" he asked at last.

"They say she is the most beautiful girl in the world," said the old man. "But who knows? It is a very old story."

The prince stood up. "I will go and see," he said.

"Do not do it!" said the innkeeper, and he put down his cloth. "Young man, you do not know what you are saying. The hedge will kill you."

"Listen to him," said the old man, and he took the prince's sleeve. "I told you the story, but I did not tell it so that you would go. You are young. You have a long life in front of you. Do not throw it away."

The prince looked at the old man's hand on his sleeve, and he smiled gently.

"I am not afraid," he said. "All this does not frighten me. I do not know why, but I feel that I must go. I will see Briar Rose with my own eyes."

The old man sighed. He knew that he could not stop him. "Then go," he said, "and may God go with you."

That night, the prince lay in his bed and did not sleep. He heard the wind outside, and he thought about the hedge and the princess. He did not know that this very day was the last day of the hundred years. And he did not know that the thorns were waiting for him to come.
