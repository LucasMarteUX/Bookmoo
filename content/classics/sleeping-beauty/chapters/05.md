# The Whole Castle Sleeps

At that moment, the king and the queen came home.

They had been away all day. They had gone to a faraway town, and they had walked and talked with the people there. But their minds had not been in the town. All day, they thought about their daughter. And when the sun began to go down, they hurried home.

They ran through the gate. They ran into the great hall. "Briar Rose!" the queen called. "Briar Rose, we are home!"

There was no answer.

"Perhaps she is in the garden," said the king. But when he looked around the hall, a strange feeling came over him. Everything was too quiet. The servants stood still in their places. One stood with a plate in his hand. One stood with a candle. Nobody spoke, and nobody moved. They were all asleep, standing on their feet.

"What is this?" said the queen.

The king did not answer. He was looking at the stairs, and his face was gray. Then a great heaviness fell on him. His eyes closed. He tried to say something, but his voice was gone. He sat down slowly on the floor, and the queen sat down next to him, and they fell asleep in each other's arms.

The sleep went through the whole castle.

In the courtyard, the horses stood in their stables. They put their heads down, and they closed their eyes. The dogs, who were running around the yard, lay down in the sun. The pigeons on the roof put their heads under their wings. In the garden, the bees stopped in the middle of the flowers. The flies sat still on the walls.

In the great hall, the fire in the fireplace stopped burning. The red flames stood still, like a picture. In the kitchen, the fire went to sleep too. The big spit that was turning a goose for the king's dinner stopped in the middle of its turn.

In the kitchen, the cook was angry. The kitchen boy had made a mistake, and she was pulling his hair. She had her hand up to hit him. But the sleep came, and she stopped. Her hand stayed in the air. The boy closed his eyes, and he fell asleep with his mouth open.

The butler was in the cellar, and he was secretly trying the wine. He had the cup at his lips. He did not drink it. He sat on a barrel, and his head fell forward. A maid sat by the door with a chicken in her lap. She was ready to take off its feathers. She did not finish.

Everyone and everything in the castle slept. The clocks stopped. The wind stopped. A leaf, which was falling from a tree in the garden, hung in the air and did not fall. In every room, in every corner, there was a deep and heavy silence.

Outside, the sun went down. The sky became red and then purple. The first star came out. The old castle stood against the sky like a picture of itself.

And then, slowly, something began to grow.

It began as a small green thing at the foot of the castle wall. A thorn bush pushed out of the ground, and it grew very fast. Its stems were as thick as a man's arm, and they were covered with long, sharp thorns. The bush grew up the wall. It grew along the roofs and over the windows. It grew across the doors and between the towers.

Every year, it grew higher. Every year, it grew thicker and stronger. First it covered the lower windows. After ten years, it covered the walls. After thirty years, it climbed to the top of the towers. After fifty years, it was like a great green mountain, and the old castle was hidden under it, with only the point of the highest tower showing. And in time, even that was gone. There was no roof, no window, no flag. There was only a great hedge of thorns, wide and high and wild, that stood alone in the middle of the land.

The people of the country were afraid of the hedge. They did not go near it. They told stories about it in the evenings. They said that a king and a queen and a beautiful princess lay asleep behind it. They said that a wicked wise woman had put a spell on them. They did not know the whole truth. But they knew the name of the girl who slept in the tower. They called her Briar Rose.

Years went by. The old people died, and the children became old. The story went from father to son and from mother to daughter. It became a legend, and then it was only a song.

Inside the hedge, nothing changed. The king slept on the floor of his great hall. The queen slept at his side. The cook still held up her hand, and the boy still waited for the blow. The butler held his cup, and the maid held her chicken. And at the top of the tower, in the little room, the old woman bent over her wheel, and Briar Rose lay on the floor with a smile on her face.

Dust fell slowly on everything, soft and gray and thick. It fell on the floors and on the tables. It fell on the hair of the sleepers, and it fell on the face of the princess, but she did not feel it. She slept on, and on, and on.
