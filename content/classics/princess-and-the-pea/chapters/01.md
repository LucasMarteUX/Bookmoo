# A Prince Who Wanted a Real Princess

Once upon a time, there was a prince. He lived in a big castle with his father, the old king, and his mother, the old queen. He was kind and clever, and he was ready to be married. But there was one problem. He wanted to marry a princess, and she had to be a real princess.

"A real princess?" said the old king. "Are there not enough princesses in the world?"

"There are many," said the prince. "But I want a real one. I do not know how I will know. But I will know."

So the prince packed his bag. He put on his best coat, and he took his strong horse. He said goodbye to his parents, and he rode out of the castle gate. He traveled all over the world to find a princess.

He went to far countries across the sea. He crossed high mountains and deep rivers. He stopped in big cities and small towns. He visited many castles, and in every castle, there was a princess. He saw tall princesses and short princesses, young ones and older ones. Some were pretty and some were clever. Some played the harp, and some wrote poems.

But the prince was never happy. When he talked with a princess, he always saw something that was not right. One of them laughed too loudly. One was unkind to her servants. One said that she loved the prince, but she looked only at his horse. One told lies. There were many princesses, but he could not be sure that any of them was a real one.

"Is she a real princess?" he asked himself after every visit. "I do not know."

He traveled for many months. At last, he was tired, and he was sad. He turned his horse toward home. He rode through the same mountains and rivers, with a heavy heart. The wind was cold, and the road was long.

When he came back to the castle, his mother ran out to meet him. She took his hands.

"My son," she said. "You are so thin. Did you find her?"

"No, Mother," said the prince. He shook his head. "There were princesses enough. But I could not find out if they were real ones. There was always something wrong with them."

"Perhaps you ask too much," said the old king. He put his arm around his son's shoulder.

"Perhaps," said the prince. "But I cannot marry a person who is not what she says. I will wait."

He was very sorry, because he wanted a real princess very much. He went up to his room, and he sat at the window and looked at the sky. For many days, he did not smile.
