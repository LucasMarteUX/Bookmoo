# The Stormy Night

One evening, a terrible storm came. The sky turned black. There was thunder, and there was lightning, and the rain poured down in great streams. The wind shook the windows of the castle. In the great hall, the old king and queen sat by the fire, with a cup of hot milk, and they listened to the storm.

"What a terrible night," said the old queen. "I am glad that we are inside."

"I am sorry for anyone who is out in this weather," said the old king.

Just then, they heard a sound. Knock, knock, knock. It was a knocking at the gate of the city. It was so soft that at first they thought that it was only the wind. But it came again. Knock, knock, knock.

"Who is out there?" said the old king. He stood up. "Nobody goes out in a storm like this, unless he is in trouble."

He took a lamp, and he went down the long stairs. He crossed the courtyard in the rain, and he opened the big gate.

A girl was standing in front of it.

Oh, what a sight she was! The wind and the rain had made her look terrible. Her hair was wet and flat. The water ran down her face, and it ran down her clothes. It ran into the toes of her shoes, and it ran out again at the heels. Her dress was heavy and dirty. She was shaking with cold.

"Please," she said. Her voice was thin. "May I come in? I have walked a very long way, and I lost my road in the storm."

"Come in, come in, child!" said the king. He took her arm, and he led her out of the rain. "What is your name? Where do you come from?"

"I am a princess," said the girl. She held her head high, even in her wet clothes. "I am a real princess."

The old king looked at her. He was a polite man, and he did not say what he thought. But he thought, "She does not look like a princess. She looks like a beggar." He took her into the hall.

The old queen looked at the girl, too. She saw the wet hair and the muddy dress. She heard the girl's tired voice. But she also saw something in her eyes. They were clear and honest.

"Well," thought the queen. "We will soon find out if you are a real princess."

She did not say a word. She called a servant. "Please bring dry clothes and hot soup for our guest," she said. "And bring a warm drink."

While the girl was eating and getting warm, the queen went to the guest bedroom. She took all the bedding off the bed. She put a single little green pea on the bottom of the bed, on the wooden boards. It was a very, very small pea. Then she put twenty mattresses on the pea, one on top of the other. And on top of the mattresses, she put twenty soft beds made of the feathers of a duck. It was the highest bed that anyone had ever seen. The princess would need a ladder to climb into it.

"That will do," said the queen. She looked at the tall bed, and she smiled to herself.

That night, the princess climbed into the bed on the ladder. She was very tired. She said good night to the king and the queen. The wind was still howling outside. She lay down on the soft feathers, and she closed her eyes.
