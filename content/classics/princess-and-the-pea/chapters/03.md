# The Morning Question

In the morning, the sun was shining. The storm was over, and the sky was clear and blue. The old king and queen and the prince sat at the breakfast table. The prince had not yet seen the girl. He had only heard about her from his mother.

The door opened, and the princess came in. She wore a clean dress now, and her hair was dry and combed. She was a pretty girl, with a gentle face. But she looked tired. She had dark rings under her eyes.

"Good morning," said the old queen. "I hope that you slept well."

"Oh, very badly!" said the princess. She sat down at the table. "I have hardly closed my eyes all night. I do not know what was in the bed. Heaven knows! But I was lying on something hard. It hurt me so much that I am black and blue all over my body. It was horrible!"

The old king looked at the old queen. The old queen looked at the old king. Nobody said anything for a moment. Then the queen smiled.

"My dear," she said, "I see that you are very tired. But you have told us the most important thing. Now we know that you are a real princess!"

"What do you mean?" said the princess.

The queen told her about the little green pea. She told her about the twenty mattresses and the twenty soft beds. "Only a real princess," she said, "could feel a pea through twenty mattresses and twenty soft beds. Nobody but a real princess has such a tender skin."

The princess laughed. She had never been tested in such a way. "Well," she said, "I am glad that I passed. I think that I would have been an ordinary girl if I had slept well."

"No," said the prince. He was looking at her with bright eyes. He had been quiet until now. "You would still be a princess. I believe you. I believed you when I heard your voice. Will you tell me about your country?"

So the princess told him. She told him about a small kingdom on the other side of the mountains, and about her father the king and her mother the queen, and about her long journey. She told him that she had been traveling for many days, and that her carriage had broken in the storm. She had walked the last part on foot, until she saw the lights of the castle.

The prince listened to everything. When she finished, he took her hand.

"I looked all over the world for a real princess," he said. "I came home without one. And now she came to me, in the rain, at my own gate. It is a strange thing. But I think that I am the luckiest man alive."

The old king laughed. "I think that he is right," he said. "My son, you must not let her go."

And so the prince married the princess. It was a happy wedding, with music and dancing. And the little green pea? It was put in a museum, in a glass box, with a small sign. It is still there today, if no one has stolen it.

There, that is a true story.
