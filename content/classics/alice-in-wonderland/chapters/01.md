# Down the Rabbit-Hole

Alice was sitting on the river bank with her sister. It was a hot afternoon, and she was very bored. Her sister was reading a book. Alice looked at it once or twice. There were no pictures in it, and there was no talking. "What is the use of a book," she thought, "without pictures or talking?"

She felt sleepy, because the day was so warm. She was thinking about making a chain of daisies. But she did not want to get up and pick them. Then, suddenly, a White Rabbit with pink eyes ran past her.

That was not very strange. Alice did not think it was strange when the Rabbit said, "Oh dear! Oh dear! I will be late!" But then the Rabbit took a watch out of its waistcoat pocket. It looked at the watch, and it hurried on. Alice jumped up. She had never seen a rabbit with a waistcoat. She had never seen a rabbit with a watch! She was very curious, so she ran across the field after it.

She was just in time to see the Rabbit jump into a big hole under a hedge. A second later, Alice went in after it. She did not think about how she would get out again.

At first, the hole went straight, like a tunnel. Then it went down, and down. Alice could not stop. She was falling down a very deep well.

The well was very deep, or she was falling very slowly. She had plenty of time to look around. First, she looked down, but it was too dark to see anything. Then she looked at the walls of the well. They were full of cupboards and shelves. She saw maps and pictures on small hooks. She took a jar from a shelf as she fell past. The label said ORANGE MARMALADE. But the jar was empty, and Alice was sad. She did not want to drop it, because it could hit someone below. So she put it in another cupboard as she went by.

"Well!" she thought. "After this long fall, I will not be afraid of falling down the stairs! My family will think that I am very brave."

Down, down, down. Would the fall never end?

"I wonder how many miles I have fallen," she said aloud. "Maybe I am near the center of the Earth. That is about four thousand miles down, I think." She had learned this at school. It was not a good time to show her knowledge, because nobody was listening. But it was good practice.

"I wonder if I will fall right through the Earth! It will be funny to come out among people who walk upside down." She tried to make a small bow as she fell. "Excuse me, please. Is this Australia?" Then she laughed. "No, that is a silly question. I will look for a sign."

Down, down, down. There was nothing else to do, so Alice talked again. "Dinah will miss me tonight," she said. Dinah was her cat. "I hope they will give her some milk at tea time. Dinah, my dear, I wish that you were here! There are no mice in the air, but you could catch a bat. A bat is like a mouse, you know. But do cats eat bats?" She began to feel sleepy, and she said again and again, "Do cats eat bats? Do cats eat bats?" Sometimes she said, "Do bats eat cats?" She did not know the answer to either question, so it did not matter.

Alice was falling asleep, and she was dreaming that she walked with Dinah. Suddenly, bump! She landed on a pile of sticks and dry leaves. The fall was over.

She was not hurt at all. She stood up and looked up, but it was dark above her. In front of her, there was a long, low passage. The White Rabbit was running along it. "Oh, my ears and whiskers! How late it is getting!" he said. He turned a corner and was gone.

Alice ran after him as fast as the wind. She turned the same corner. But the Rabbit was not there. She was in a long, low hall with a row of lamps on the roof. There were doors all around the hall, but every door was locked. Alice walked down one side and up the other. She tried every door, and none of them opened. Then she walked sadly to the middle of the room. "How will I ever get out?" she thought.

Suddenly, she saw a little table with three legs. It was made of glass. There was nothing on it except a tiny golden key. Alice thought, "This key may open one of the doors." But the locks were too big, or the key was too small. It did not open any door.

But when she walked around the hall again, she saw a low curtain that she had not seen before. Behind it, there was a very small door, only fifteen inches high. She put the golden key into the lock. It fit!

She opened the door. It led to a small passage, as narrow as a rat's hole. She knelt down and looked along the passage. At the end of it, there was the loveliest garden that she had ever seen. She wanted so much to get out of that dark hall and walk among those bright flowers and cool fountains! But she could not even get her head through the little door.

"Even if my head could go through," she thought, "it would be of little use without my shoulders. I wish that I could shut up like a telescope. I think that I can, if I only know how to begin." So many strange things had happened that Alice had started to believe that nothing was really impossible.

There was no use in waiting by the little door, so she went back to the table. She hoped to find another key, or a book of rules about shutting up like a telescope. This time, she found a small bottle on the table. It was not there before. There was a paper label around its neck, and it said DRINK ME in big, beautiful letters.
