# The Queen's Croquet-Ground

A big rose tree stood near the entrance of the garden. The roses on it were white. But three gardeners were busy and painted them red. Alice thought that this was very strange. She went closer to watch them. She heard one of them say, "Be careful, Five! Do not throw paint on me like that!"

"I could not help it," said Five, in a bad mood. "Seven pushed my arm."

Seven looked up and said, "That is right, Five! You always blame other people!"

"You should not talk!" said Five. "I heard the Queen say yesterday that you should lose your head."

"Why?" said the one who spoke first.

"That is not your business, Two!" said Seven.

"Yes, it is his business!" said Five. "And I will tell him. It was because you brought the cook tulip roots instead of onions."

Seven threw down his brush. He began, "Well, that is the most unfair thing..." But then he saw Alice, who was watching them. He stopped. The others looked around, and all three bowed low.

"Please tell me," said Alice a little shyly, "why are you painting those roses?"

Five and Seven said nothing. They looked at Two. Two began in a low voice. "The truth is, miss, this should have been a red rose tree. We put a white one by mistake. If the Queen finds out, we will all lose our heads. So we are doing our best, before she comes, to..." At that moment, Five, who had been looking across the garden, shouted, "The Queen! The Queen!" The three gardeners threw themselves flat on their faces.

There was a sound of many feet. Alice looked around. She wanted to see the Queen.

First came ten soldiers with clubs. They were all shaped like the three gardeners, flat and long, with their hands and feet at the corners. Next came ten courtiers, covered with diamonds. They walked two by two, like the soldiers. After them came the royal children, ten of them. The little dears jumped happily along, hand in hand. They were covered with hearts. Next came the guests, mostly Kings and Queens. Alice saw the White Rabbit among them. He was talking fast and nervously, and smiling at everything. He passed without seeing her. Then came the Knave of Hearts. He carried the King's crown on a red velvet cushion. And last of all in this grand procession came THE KING AND QUEEN OF HEARTS.

Alice was not sure if she should lie on her face like the three gardeners. But she did not remember any rule like that for processions. "And what is the use of a procession," she thought, "if everybody lies on their face and cannot see it?" So she stood still and waited.

When the procession came to Alice, they all stopped and looked at her. The Queen said sharply, "Who is this?" She asked the Knave of Hearts. He only bowed and smiled.

"Fool!" said the Queen, and she shook her head. She turned to Alice. "What is your name, child?"

"My name is Alice, Your Majesty," said Alice very politely. But she thought, "They are only a pack of cards. I do not need to be afraid of them!"

"And who are these?" asked the Queen. She pointed at the three gardeners on the ground. They were lying on their faces, and the pattern on their backs was the same as all the other cards. So she could not tell if they were gardeners, or soldiers, or courtiers, or three of her own children.

"How should I know?" said Alice. She was surprised at her own courage. "It is not my business."

The Queen's face became red with anger. She looked at Alice like a wild animal. Then she screamed, "Off with her head! Off with..."

"Nonsense!" said Alice very loudly. The Queen was silent.

The King put his hand on her arm. "Think, my dear," he said shyly. "She is only a child!"

The Queen turned angrily away from him. She said to the Knave, "Turn them over!" The Knave did it very carefully with one foot.

"Get up!" screamed the Queen. The three gardeners jumped up at once. They began to bow to the King, the Queen, the royal children and everybody else.

"Stop that!" screamed the Queen. "You make me dizzy!" Then she looked at the rose tree. "What were you doing here?"

"If it pleases Your Majesty," said Two, kneeling on one knee, "we were trying..."

"I see!" said the Queen. She had been looking at the roses. "Off with their heads!" The procession moved on. Three soldiers stayed to kill the unlucky gardeners. But Alice quickly hid them in a big flowerpot. The soldiers looked for them for a minute or two, and then they walked away after the others.

"Are their heads off?" shouted the Queen.

"Their heads are gone, Your Majesty!" shouted the soldiers.

"Good!" shouted the Queen. "Can you play croquet?"

The soldiers did not answer. They looked at Alice, because the question was for her.

"Yes!" shouted Alice.

"Come on, then!" roared the Queen. Alice joined the procession. She wondered very much what would happen next.

"It is a fine day!" said a shy voice by her side. It was the White Rabbit. He was looking at her face nervously.

"Very fine," said Alice. "Where is the Duchess?"

"Shh! Shh!" said the Rabbit in a low, quick voice. He looked over his shoulder. Then he stood on his toes, put his mouth close to her ear, and whispered, "She has a death sentence."

"What for?" said Alice.

"Did you say 'What a pity'?" asked the Rabbit.

"No, I did not," said Alice. "I do not think that it is a pity. I said 'What for?'"

"She hit the Queen's ear," the Rabbit began. Alice laughed. "Shh!" the Rabbit whispered in fear. "The Queen will hear you! You see, she came late, and the Queen said..."

"Get to your places!" shouted the Queen, in a voice like thunder. People began to run in all directions and fell over each other. But after a minute or two, they were all in their places, and the game began.

Alice thought that she had never seen such a strange croquet ground. It was all hills and holes. The balls were live hedgehogs, and the mallets were live flamingos. The soldiers had to bend over and stand on their hands and feet to make the arches.

The hardest thing for Alice was her flamingo. She got its body under her arm without trouble, with its legs hanging down. But when she had its neck nicely straight and she was ready to hit the hedgehog with its head, the flamingo turned around and looked up at her with such a puzzled face that she laughed. When she put its head down again and was ready to begin, the hedgehog had opened up and was walking away. There was always a hill or a hole between the hedgehog and the place where she wanted to send it. And the soldiers kept getting up and walking to other parts of the ground. So Alice decided that it was a very difficult game.

All the players played at the same time. They did not wait for their turn. They argued all the time and fought over the hedgehogs. Very soon, the Queen was in a terrible temper. She stamped around and shouted, "Off with his head!" or "Off with her head!" about once a minute.

Alice began to feel uneasy. She had not had an argument with the Queen yet. But she knew that it could happen at any minute. "And then," she thought, "what will happen to me? They love to cut off people's heads here. It is a wonder that anyone is still alive!"
