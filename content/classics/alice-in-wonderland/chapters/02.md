# The Pool of Tears

It was easy to read the words DRINK ME. But clever Alice did not want to drink in a hurry. "First I will look," she said, "and see if it says poison." She had read some stories about children who had accidents. They were burned, or they were eaten by animals. This happened because they did not remember simple rules. For example, a hot piece of metal burns your hand if you hold it too long. And Alice never forgot this rule: if you drink a lot from a bottle marked "poison," it usually makes you ill.

But this bottle did not say "poison." So Alice tasted it. It was very nice! It tasted of cherry cake, hot pudding, pineapple, roast chicken, toffee and warm toast, all at once. She drank all of it.

"What a strange feeling!" she said. "I think that I am shutting up like a telescope."

She was. She was now only ten inches tall. Her face became bright with joy. "Now I am the right size to go through the little door into the garden!" she thought. But first she waited a few minutes. She wanted to see if she would get any smaller. This made her a little afraid. "I might disappear like a candle," she thought. "I wonder what I would look like then."

Nothing else happened, so she went to the door. But, oh dear! She had forgotten the golden key. It was on the glass table, and now she was too small to reach it. She could see it through the glass. She tried to climb up a leg of the table, but the glass was too slippery. At last, she was so tired that she sat down and cried.

"Come now, crying is no use!" she told herself. "Stop it at once!" Alice often gave herself good advice. But she did not often follow it. Sometimes she was so hard on herself that she cried.

Then she saw a small glass box under the table. She opened it. Inside there was a very small cake. On the cake, the words EAT ME were written in little dried fruits.

"I will eat it," said Alice. "If it makes me bigger, I can get the key. If it makes me smaller, I can go under the door. Either way, I can get into the garden. I do not care which!"

She ate a small piece and asked herself, "Which way? Which way?" She held her hand on top of her head to feel if she was growing. She was surprised that she stayed the same size. That is what usually happens when you eat cake. But Alice expected strange things now, and an ordinary cake seemed boring. So she ate the rest of it.

"Curiouser and curiouser!" she cried. She was so surprised that she forgot how to speak good English. "Now I am growing like the longest telescope in the world! Goodbye, feet!" She looked down, and her feet were very far away. "Oh, my poor feet! Who will put on your shoes and socks now? I cannot do it. I will be too far away. But I must be kind to them, or they may not walk where I want to go. I know! I will give them a new pair of boots every Christmas."

She planned how to send them. "They will go by post," she thought. "How funny to send presents to my own feet! And how strange the address will look. To Alice's Right Foot, on the carpet, near the fire. From Alice, with love. Oh dear, what nonsense I am talking!"

Just then, her head hit the roof of the hall. Now she was more than nine feet tall. She took the golden key and hurried to the little door.

Poor Alice! She could only lie on one side and look into the garden with one eye. It was more impossible than ever to go through. She sat down and began to cry again.

"You should be ashamed," she said. "A big girl like you, crying like this! Stop at once!" But she went on crying, and big tears fell on the floor. Soon there was a big pool around her. It was four inches deep, and it covered half of the hall.

After a while, she heard small feet far away. She quickly dried her eyes to see what was coming. It was the White Rabbit again. He was wearing fine clothes. In one hand, he had white leather gloves, and in the other, a big fan. He was hurrying along and talking to himself. "Oh! The Duchess, the Duchess! She will be so angry if I keep her waiting!"

Alice was so desperate that she would ask anyone for help. When the Rabbit came near, she said, in a low and shy voice, "Excuse me, sir..." The Rabbit jumped. He dropped the gloves and the fan, and he ran away into the dark as fast as he could.

Alice picked up the fan and the gloves. The hall was very hot, so she used the fan while she talked to herself. "Oh dear, everything is so strange today! Yesterday, everything was normal. Did I change in the night? Let me think. Was I the same when I woke up? I think that I felt a little different. But if I am not the same, then who am I? That is the big puzzle!"

She thought about the girls that she knew. "I am not Ada," she said, "because Ada has long curly hair, and mine is not curly. And I am not Mabel, because I know many things, and Mabel knows very little. Oh dear, it is all so confusing!"

She looked at her hands. She saw that she was wearing one of the Rabbit's small white gloves. "How did I put it on?" she thought. "I must be getting small again." She went to the table to measure herself. She was now about two feet tall, and she was getting smaller fast. She soon saw why. It was the fan in her hand! She dropped it at once. Now she was safe, but only just.

"That was close!" said Alice. She was frightened, but she was glad to be alive. "Now, to the garden!" She ran back to the little door. But, oh no! The little door was shut again. The golden key was on the glass table, as before. "Things are worse than ever," she thought. "I have never been so small! It is too bad!"

As she said the words, her foot slipped. Splash! She was up to her chin in salty water. At first, she thought that she had fallen into the sea. "Then I can go home by train," she said. But she soon understood. It was the pool of tears that she had cried when she was nine feet tall.

"I wish I had not cried so much!" she said, swimming around. "Now I will drown in my own tears. That is a strange thing!"

Something splashed in the water near her. She swam closer to see what it was. At first, she thought that it was a walrus or a hippo. But then she remembered how small she was. It was only a mouse. It had fallen in, like her.
