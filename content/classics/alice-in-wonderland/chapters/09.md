# The Mock Turtle's Story

"I am so glad to see you again, you dear old thing!" said a voice. It was the Duchess. She put her arm in Alice's, and they walked off together. Alice was happy to see her in a good mood. She thought, "Maybe it was only the pepper that made her so angry in the kitchen."

"When I am a duchess," she said to herself, "I will not have any pepper in my kitchen. Soup is good without it. Maybe pepper makes people hot-tempered. Maybe vinegar makes them sour, and sugar makes children sweet."

She forgot the Duchess for a moment. She was surprised when she heard her voice close to her ear. "You are thinking, my dear, and that makes you forget to talk. I cannot tell you now what the lesson of that is. But I will remember in a minute."

"Maybe it has no lesson," said Alice.

"Nonsense, child!" said the Duchess. "Everything has a lesson, if you can find it." And she moved closer to Alice's side.

Alice did not like to be so close to her. First, because the Duchess was very ugly. Second, because she was just the right height to put her chin on Alice's shoulder. It was a very sharp chin. But Alice did not want to be rude, so she did not say anything.

Then the Queen came. "I warn you," she shouted, and she stamped her foot. "Either you or your head must go, and quickly! Choose!" The Duchess chose, and she was gone in a second.

"Let us go on with the game," the Queen said to Alice. Alice was too afraid to speak. She followed her slowly back to the croquet ground. The other guests were resting in the shade. When they saw the Queen, they hurried back to the game. The Queen told them that one more minute of delay meant their lives.

Soon the Queen said to a strange creature that lay in the sun, "Get up, lazy thing! Take this young lady to see the Mock Turtle, and to hear his story." She walked away and left Alice with the creature. It was a Gryphon, half lion and half eagle. Alice did not like its look. But she thought that it was as safe to stay with it as to go after that cruel Queen. So she waited.

The Gryphon sat up and rubbed its eyes. It watched the Queen until she was out of sight. Then it laughed. "What fun!" it said, half to itself and half to Alice.

"What is the fun?" said Alice.

"The Queen," said the Gryphon. "It is all in her mind. Nobody ever cuts off a head, you know. Come on!"

"Everybody says 'Come on' here," thought Alice, and she followed slowly. "Nobody ever gave me so many orders in my life!"

They did not go far. Soon they saw the Mock Turtle. He was sitting sad and alone on a little rock. When they came closer, Alice could hear him sigh as if his heart was breaking. She felt very sorry for him. "What is his sorrow?" she asked the Gryphon. The Gryphon answered almost in the same words as before. "It is all in his mind. He has no sorrow, you know. Come on!"

So they went up to the Mock Turtle. He looked at them with big eyes full of tears, but he said nothing.

"This young lady wants to know your story," said the Gryphon.

"I will tell it to her," said the Mock Turtle in a deep, hollow voice. "Sit down, both of you, and do not say a word until I finish."

They sat down. Nobody spoke for some minutes. Alice thought, "How can he finish, if he does not start?" But she waited patiently.

"Once," said the Mock Turtle at last, with a deep sigh, "I was a real Turtle."

After these words, there was a long silence. Only the Gryphon made a strange noise now and then, and the Mock Turtle sobbed heavily. Alice almost got up and said, "Thank you, sir, for your interesting story." But she thought that there must be more. So she sat still and said nothing.

"When we were little," the Mock Turtle went on at last, a little calmer, but still sobbing, "we went to school in the sea. The teacher was an old Turtle. We called him Tortoise."

"Why did you call him Tortoise, if he was not one?" asked Alice.

"We called him Tortoise because he taught us," said the Mock Turtle angrily. "You are really very slow!"

"You should be ashamed to ask such a simple question," said the Gryphon. They both sat in silence and looked at poor Alice. She wanted to sink into the ground.

At last, the Gryphon said to the Mock Turtle, "Go on, old friend! Do not take all day!" He went on with these words. "Yes, we went to school in the sea, although you may not believe it..."

"I never said that I did not!" said Alice.

"You did," said the Mock Turtle.

"Be quiet!" said the Gryphon, before Alice could speak again.

The Mock Turtle went on. "We had the best education. We went to school every day."

"I went to a day school too," said Alice. "You do not need to be so proud."

"With extras?" asked the Mock Turtle, a little nervously.

"Yes," said Alice. "We learned French and music."

"And washing?" said the Mock Turtle.

"Certainly not!" said Alice angrily.

"Ah! Then yours was not a really good school," said the Mock Turtle, relieved. "At our school, at the end of the bill, it said, 'French, music and washing: extra.'"

"You did not need it," said Alice, "living at the bottom of the sea."

"I could not pay for it," said the Mock Turtle sadly. "I only did the normal course."

"What was that?" asked Alice.

"Reeling and Writhing, of course, to begin with," said the Mock Turtle. "Then the different parts of Arithmetic: Ambition, Distraction, Uglification and Derision."

"I never heard of Uglification," said Alice. "What is it?"

The Gryphon lifted both its paws in surprise. "Never heard of uglifying!" it said. "You know what 'to beautify' means, I think?"

"Yes," said Alice, not sure. "It means to make something prettier."

"Well, then," said the Gryphon, "if you do not know what 'to uglify' means, you are a fool."

Alice did not want to ask more about it. She turned to the Mock Turtle. "What else did you learn?"

"Well, there was Mystery," said the Mock Turtle. He counted the lessons on his flippers. "Mystery, old and new, with Seaography. Then Drawling. The Drawling teacher was an old eel. He came once a week. He taught us Drawling, Stretching and Fainting in Coils."

"What was that like?" asked Alice.

"I cannot show you," said the Mock Turtle. "I am too stiff. And the Gryphon never learned it."

"I had no time," said the Gryphon. "But I went to the teacher of old languages. He was an old crab."

"I never went to him," said the Mock Turtle with a sigh. "People said that he taught Laughing and Grief."

"He did, he did," said the Gryphon, and sighed too. Both creatures hid their faces in their paws.

"And how many hours a day did you have lessons?" asked Alice quickly. She wanted to change the subject.

"Ten hours the first day," said the Mock Turtle. "Nine the next day, and so on."

"What a strange plan!" said Alice.

"That is why they are called lessons," said the Gryphon. "They get less every day."

This was a new idea to Alice. She thought about it for a moment. "Then the eleventh day was a holiday?"

"Of course," said the Mock Turtle.

"And what did you do on the twelfth?" Alice asked.

"That is enough about lessons," said the Gryphon firmly. "Now tell her about the games."
