# The Rabbit Sends in Little Bill

After a while, Alice heard small steps again. She looked up quickly. She hoped that the Mouse was coming back. But it was the White Rabbit. He walked slowly, and he looked worried. He was looking at the ground, as if he had lost something.

"The Duchess! The Duchess! Oh, my paws! Oh, my fur and whiskers! She will have me killed! Where did I drop them?"

Alice understood at once. He was looking for his fan and his white gloves. She began to look for them too. But she could not see them anywhere. Everything had changed since her swim in the pool. The big hall, the glass table and the little door were all gone.

The Rabbit soon saw her. "Mary Ann!" he said angrily. "What are you doing here? Go home at once and bring me a pair of gloves and a fan. Quick!"

Alice was so frightened that she ran in the direction that he pointed. She did not say that he had made a mistake.

"He thinks that I am his housemaid," she said as she ran. "He will be so surprised when he finds out who I am! But I will take him his fan and gloves, if I can find them."

She soon came to a neat little house. On the door, there was a shiny brass plate with the name W. RABBIT. She went in without knocking and ran up the stairs. She was afraid to meet the real Mary Ann. She did not want to be sent out before she found the fan and gloves.

"How strange," she thought. "I am running errands for a rabbit! Soon Dinah will give me jobs too!"

She found a tidy little room with a table near the window. On the table, there was a fan and two or three pairs of tiny white gloves. She took the fan and a pair of gloves. She was going to leave when she saw a small bottle near the mirror. It did not have a label this time. But she opened it and tasted it. "Something interesting always happens," she said, "when I eat or drink."

She was right. Before she finished half the bottle, her head touched the ceiling. She had to bend down so that she did not hurt her neck. She put the bottle down quickly. "That is enough," she said. "I hope that I do not grow any more. I cannot get out of the door now. I wish that I had not drunk so much!"

But it was too late. She went on growing, and growing. Soon she had to kneel on the floor. A minute later, there was not even room for that. She lay down with one elbow against the door and her other arm around her head. She was still growing. At last, she put one arm out of the window and one foot up the chimney. "Now I can do nothing more," she thought. "What will happen to me?"

Luckily, the magic bottle had no more power now, and she did not grow any bigger. But it was very uncomfortable. There was no way to get out of the room. No wonder that she was unhappy.

"It was much nicer at home," she thought. "Nobody was always getting bigger and smaller. Nobody told me what to do, like mice and rabbits. I almost wish that I had not gone down that hole. But it is strange and interesting, this kind of life! When I read fairy tales, I thought that these things never happened. Now I am in the middle of one! Somebody should write a book about me. And when I grow up, I will write one." She stopped. "But I am grown up now," she said sadly. "At least there is no room to grow any more here."

Then she heard a voice outside. "Mary Ann! Mary Ann! Bring me my gloves now!" Small feet ran up the stairs. Alice knew that it was the Rabbit. She shook so much that the whole house moved. She forgot that she was now a thousand times bigger than the Rabbit. She had no reason to be afraid.

The Rabbit came to the door and tried to open it. But the door opened inward, and Alice's elbow was pressed hard against it. He could not open it. Alice heard him say, "Then I will go around and go in through the window."

"You will not," thought Alice. She waited until she heard him under the window. Then she opened her hand and quickly grabbed at the air. She did not catch anything, but she heard a small scream, a fall and the sound of breaking glass. She guessed that he had fallen into a garden frame for growing cucumbers.

Next, she heard the Rabbit's angry voice. "Pat! Pat! Where are you?" And then another voice. "I am here, sir! I am digging for apples!"

"Digging for apples, indeed!" said the Rabbit. "Come here and help me out of this!" (More broken glass.)

"Tell me, Pat, what is that in the window?"

"It is an arm, sir!"

"An arm, you fool! Who ever saw an arm that size? It fills the whole window!"

"Yes, sir. But it is an arm all the same."

"It should not be there. Go and take it away!"

There was a long silence. Alice only heard whispers now and then. At last, she opened her hand and grabbed at the air again. This time, there were two small screams and more breaking glass. "How many cucumber frames are there?" she thought. "What will they do next? I wish that they could pull me out of the window! I do not want to stay in here any longer."

After some time, she heard small cart wheels, and a lot of voices talking together. "Where is the other ladder? I only had one. Bill has the other. Bill! Bring it here! Put it at this corner. No, tie them together first. They are not high enough yet. Oh, they will do. Do not be so careful. Bill, catch this rope! Will the roof hold? Watch that loose tile! Oh, it is falling! Heads down!" There was a loud crash. "Who did that? It was Bill, I think. Who will go down the chimney? Not me! You do it! Not me, then! Bill must go. Bill, the master says that you must go down the chimney!"

"Oh! So Bill must go down the chimney," said Alice to herself. "They always give the work to Bill! I would not want to be Bill. This fireplace is narrow, but I think that I can kick a little."

She pulled her foot down the chimney as far as she could. Then she waited. She heard a small animal scratching and climbing in the chimney above her. "That is Bill," she thought. She gave one sharp kick and waited to see what happened.

First, she heard everybody cry, "There goes Bill!" Then she heard the Rabbit's voice alone. "Catch him, you by the hedge!" Then it was quiet, and then there was more noise. "Hold up his head. Give him some brandy. Do not let him choke. What happened, old friend? Tell us everything!"

Last, she heard a small, weak voice. ("That is Bill," thought Alice.) "I do not know. No more, thank you, I am better now. But I am too confused to tell you. I only know that something came at me like a toy from a box, and up I went like a rocket!"

"So you did, old friend!" said the others.

"We must burn the house down!" said the Rabbit. Alice shouted as loud as she could, "If you do, I will send Dinah after you!"

It was suddenly very quiet. Alice thought, "What will they do next? If they had any sense, they would take off the roof." After a minute or two, they started moving again. She heard the Rabbit say, "Bring a wheelbarrow full, to start with."

"A wheelbarrow full of what?" Alice thought. She did not have to wait long. In the next moment, a shower of small stones came through the window, and some of them hit her face. "I will stop this," she thought, and she shouted, "Do not do that again!" Again there was silence.

Alice saw that the stones were turning into little cakes on the floor. A bright idea came into her head. "If I eat one of these cakes," she thought, "it will change my size. It cannot make me bigger, so it must make me smaller."

She swallowed one of the cakes, and she was delighted. She began to shrink at once. As soon as she was small enough to go through the door, she ran out of the house. A crowd of small animals and birds was waiting outside. The poor little lizard, Bill, was in the middle. Two guinea pigs were holding him up and giving him something from a bottle. They all rushed at Alice when she came out. But she ran away as fast as she could, and soon she was safe in a thick wood.
