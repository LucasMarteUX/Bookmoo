# Dame Gothel Finds Out

The summer went by, and the secret ladder grew longer. It was almost ready. Rapunzel looked at it every day, and she counted the arms of silk. Soon she would be free. Soon she would leave the tower forever.

One morning, the witch came as usual. She called from the ground, and Rapunzel let down her hair. The witch climbed up, and she stood in the room, out of breath.

"Phew," she said. She sat on the chair. "It is a long way up. I am old. My bones are tired."

"Yes, Dame Gothel," said Rapunzel. She was folding the blanket on the bed.

The witch took out some bread and cheese, and she put them on the table. She looked around the room, as she did every day. Rapunzel was thinking about something else. She was thinking about the prince, and about the ladder under the bed. She was so happy that she was not careful.

"Dame Gothel," she said suddenly, "can you tell me something?"

"What is it?"

"I have been wondering," said Rapunzel. She laughed a little. "Why are you so much heavier to pull up than the king's son? He comes up in a minute, and I hardly feel him at all."

The room was silent.

Rapunzel heard what she had said, and her face went white. She put her hand over her mouth. But it was too late. The words were in the air, and she could not take them back.

The witch stood up slowly. Her eyes were very big. They were full of a terrible light.

"What did you say?" she whispered.

"I did not mean..." said Rapunzel.

"The king's son," said the witch. Her voice grew louder. "The king's son has been here? In this tower?"

"Dame Gothel, please—"

"You wicked child!" cried the witch. "You ungrateful girl! I thought I had hidden you from the whole world. I thought you were safe. And all this time, you have been lying to me!"

"I am sorry," said Rapunzel. She was crying now. "I love him. We are going to be married. Please do not be angry."

"Married!" said the witch. She laughed, and it was an awful laugh. "Married to a man you do not know? I have given you everything. I have kept you from harm. And this is how you repay me?"

She grabbed Rapunzel's beautiful hair. She wrapped it twice around her left hand, and she pulled it tight. With her right hand, she took a pair of scissors from her pocket.

"No!" cried Rapunzel. "No, please!"

Snip, snap. The scissors cut through the golden braids. The hair fell to the floor in long shining ropes. Rapunzel screamed, and she fell on her knees. Her head felt light and cold, and she put her hands to it. She could not believe it.

The witch did not stop. She pulled Rapunzel up by her arm, and she said strange words in a harsh voice. A dark wind rose in the room, and the girl felt her feet leave the floor. The wind carried her out of the window, and over the trees, and away.

When it put her down, she was in a place that she had never seen. It was a wide and empty land. There was no tree and no house. There was only sand and stones and dry brown grass, and a hot, high sky. The witch was not there. She had only sent her away. There was no one to hear her cry.

Rapunzel sat on the ground, and she cried for a long time. She had lost her hair. She had lost her tower, and her prince. She did not know where she was. She only knew that she was alone.

And in the tower, the witch picked up the golden braids from the floor. She tied them to the hook of the window, and she sat down in the chair. She smiled a cruel smile.

"Now we will wait," she said. "We will see who comes."

The sun went down. The stars came out. It was evening, and the forest was quiet. A long way off, a horse's steps were coming nearer through the trees.
