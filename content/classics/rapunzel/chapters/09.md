# Two Tears

While the prince walked through the forest, Rapunzel lived in the wide, dry land.

It was a hard place, with little water and little food. She found a hollow under some rocks, and she made it into a home. She picked berries and dug roots. She found a small spring, and she carried the water in her hands. At first, she was very afraid. At night, the wind made strange sounds, and she lay awake and listened. Slowly, she learned the ways of the land. She learned which plants were good to eat. She learned how to make a fire with dry sticks.

She did not have a mirror, but she could feel her head. Her hair grew again, but it grew slowly. For a long time, it was short and rough, and she wore a cloth over it in the sun.

In the early weeks, she cried every day. She thought about the prince, and about the tower. She thought about the witch, and about her mother and father, whom she had never known. She did not know if the prince was alive. She did not know if he was looking for her. She sang to herself in the evenings, to feel less alone, and her voice went out over the empty land.

Some months after she arrived, Rapunzel found that she was going to have a baby. At first she was afraid. Then she was happy, and then she was afraid again. How could she raise a child in this place? But she did not have a choice. She worked harder than ever. She found a better cave. She gathered more food and kept it dry.

And in the spring, she had twins, a boy and a girl.

They were small and loud and healthy. They had her golden hair and the prince's brown eyes. Rapunzel held them in her arms, and she cried for joy. For the first time in a long time, she was not alone. She named the boy and the girl, and she sang to them at night. Her voice was stronger than ever, and the children went to sleep to the sound of it.

The years went by. The twins grew. They learned to walk and to talk. They played in the sand and chased the lizards between the rocks. Rapunzel told them stories about a tall tower and a prince who came to visit. She told them about a song that carried a man through the forest. The children laughed and asked for more.

"Is our father alive?" the little girl asked one night.

"I do not know," said Rapunzel. "But I hope so."

"Will he come?"

"I hope so," said Rapunzel again. And she looked out into the dark, where the stars were shining.

And one day, when the children were old enough to run and climb, a man came walking across the dry land.

Rapunzel was sitting in front of the cave. She was singing as she shelled some nuts. She did not see the man at first. He was a long way off, a thin shape on the road. He walked slowly, with his hands out in front of him, as people do when they cannot see. His clothes were torn. His hair was long and grey with dust. He looked like a beggar.

But when she began to sing the second verse of her song, he stopped. He turned his head toward the sound, and he stood quite still, like someone who hears a voice from a dream.

Rapunzel stopped singing. She looked at him. Something in the way that he held his head was strange and known to her. She stood up slowly. The nuts fell out of her lap.

"It cannot be," she whispered.

The man began to walk toward her. His steps were fast and unsteady. He was calling something, but she could not hear what it was. Then she could. He was calling a name.

"Rapunzel! Rapunzel!"

She ran. She ran across the sand, and she threw her arms around his neck. She cried so hard that she could not speak. He held her tightly, and he cried too. He put his hands on her face. He felt her cheeks and her hair, and he knew her.

"It is you," he said. "It is really you. I have looked for you for so many years."

"My love," said Rapunzel. "My dear love. Oh, what has happened to your eyes?"

"I fell from the tower," said the prince. "The thorns took away my sight. But it does not matter now. I have found you. I would give my eyes a hundred times to hear your voice again."

Rapunzel held his face in her hands. She looked at his poor blind eyes, red and dull, and her tears began to fall. Two of them dropped on his face. They fell on his eyelids and ran down his cheeks.

The prince blinked. He felt something warm, something that was not tears of his own. He opened his eyes wide.

The dark began to go. It went away slowly, like the night before the day. First, there was a grey light. Then there was color. And then, standing in front of him in the bright sun, there was a woman with a tired face and golden hair and loving eyes.

"Rapunzel," he said. "I can see you."

He laughed out loud. He lifted her off the ground and turned around and around. Then he saw two children standing by the rocks, with golden hair and brown eyes. They were looking at him with wide, serious faces.

"Who are they?" he asked softly.

"They are your son and your daughter," said Rapunzel.

The prince knelt down. His face was wet. The children looked at him, and then the boy came up and touched his cheek with a small hand.

"Are you our father?" he said.

"Yes," said the prince. "Yes, I am."

He took them all, one by one, in his arms. They stayed there for a long time, in the light of the afternoon, and nobody wanted to speak.

The prince led his family to his father's kingdom. It was a long way, but they were happy and they did not mind. When they arrived, the old king and queen ran out of the gate to meet them. They had been sad for so long. They cried and kissed their son, and they took their grandchildren in their arms, and they welcomed Rapunzel as their own daughter.

There was a great feast, and the whole kingdom came. The prince and Rapunzel were married in the great church, and the bells rang for three days. And from then on, they lived together in great joy for many, many years.

As for the witch, nobody ever saw her again. Some said that the tower fell down in a storm. Others said that it still stands in the forest, grey and silent, with a small window at the top. And in the evenings, when the wind is soft, you can hear a song in the trees. It is a song of love, and it is very sweet.
