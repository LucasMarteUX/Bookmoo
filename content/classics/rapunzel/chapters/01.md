# The Window at the Back of the House

Once upon a time, a man and his wife lived in a little house at the edge of a town. They were poor, but they were not unhappy. The man worked hard every day. The woman kept the house clean, and she sang while she worked. They loved each other very much. Only one thing was missing from their lives. They had no child.

For many years, they waited and hoped. Every spring, the woman looked at the new flowers, and she thought, "Perhaps this year." Every winter, she sat by the fire and said nothing. The man saw her sad face, and he did not know what to say. He only took her hand.

At last, one summer, the woman knew that her wish was coming true. She was going to have a baby. She told her husband, and he laughed and cried, and he picked her up and turned around with her in his arms.

"We will have a child!" he said. "After all these years!"

"Yes," she said, and she held his face in her hands. "I am so happy."

They began to make a little bed. The man cut the wood, and the woman made a soft blanket. They talked every night about names. They were not rich, but they felt that they had everything.

At the back of their house, there was a small window. It looked out over a wall, and behind the wall there was a garden. It was the most beautiful garden that the woman had ever seen. There were roses and lilies and tall blue flowers. There were rows of green herbs, and there were apple trees heavy with fruit. In the morning, the sun made the flowers shine. In the evening, the smell of the garden came through the window and filled the room.

But nobody went into that garden. A high stone wall went all around it, and there was no gate. It belonged to a witch. She was an old woman with a hard face and sharp eyes, and she had great power. Everybody in the town was afraid of her. If a child threw a ball over her wall, the child did not go and look for it. If a dog ran in the street near the wall, its owner called it back quickly. People did not even like to speak her name.

The woman liked to stand at the little window and look at the garden. She watched the flowers move in the wind. She watched the bees. She never thought of going in. She only looked.

One afternoon in the early autumn, she was standing at the window, as she did every day. Her eyes went slowly along the rows of plants. Then she stopped.

In one corner of the garden, there was a bed of green leaves. They were fresh and bright and very green, and the water of the morning was still on them. She knew the plant. It was called rapunzel. People in that country made a salad of its leaves, and it tasted sweet and a little sharp.

She looked at it for a long time. She did not know why, but she could not look away.

"How good that would taste," she said to herself.

That evening, she was quiet. She did not eat her supper. She sat at the window, and she looked at the green leaves in the last light.

The next day, she was pale. She did not sing. She did not speak much. She sat by the window and looked at the garden, and she did not even go outside. Every hour, her wish became stronger. It was no longer a small wish. It was a hunger that she could not control. She could think of nothing else.

On the third day, she was weak and thin, and she could not stand without help. Her husband came home from work, and he found her in bed with her face turned to the wall.

"What is the matter, dear wife?" he said. He sat next to her and touched her hand. It was cold. "Are you ill? Is it the baby?"

"No," she said in a very small voice. "It is the rapunzel."

"The what?"

"The rapunzel in the garden behind our house. The witch's garden." She turned her tired eyes to him. "If I cannot eat some of it, I know that I will die."

The man stared at her. He did not understand. "You will die for some salad leaves?" he said.

"I cannot help it," she said. "I have never wanted anything so much in my life."

He looked at her white face, and he felt a cold fear in his heart. He loved her more than anything in the world. And he thought of the baby, too.

"All right," he said at last. He took a deep breath. "I will get it for you. I do not know how, but I will get it. You will have your rapunzel. I promise."
