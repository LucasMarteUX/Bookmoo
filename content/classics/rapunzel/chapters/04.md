# The Tower in the Forest

The witch took Rapunzel by the hand, and they walked into the deep forest. The trees were old and tall. The path was narrow, and the light was green. They walked for a long time. Rapunzel did not ask where they were going. She was a little afraid, but she trusted the witch.

At last, they came to a clearing. In the middle of it, there stood a tower.

It was a very tall tower, made of grey stone. It was round, and it went up higher than the trees. The wall was smooth, and there were no steps. There was no door at the bottom. There was no window on the lower walls. Only at the very top, a long way up, there was one small window.

"This is your new house," said the witch.

Rapunzel looked up. The tower seemed to touch the clouds. "But how do I go in?" she said. "I cannot see a door."

"There is no door," said the witch. "That is the point. Nobody can enter, and nobody can leave. Here, you will be safe from the world."

"Safe from what?" asked Rapunzel.

"From everything," said the witch. "The world is a dangerous and wicked place. People will lie to you and hurt you. I know. I have lived a long time. I will take care of you here. And you will never be afraid."

She stood at the foot of the tower, and she called up in a loud voice:

"Rapunzel, Rapunzel,
Let down your hair to me."

Rapunzel did not know what to do. "Close your eyes," said the witch. She said some strange words in a low voice. A warm wind came around Rapunzel, and she felt her feet leave the ground. It was frightening, but it was very quick. When she opened her eyes, she was standing in the small round room at the top of the tower.

It was a plain room. There was a bed, a table, and a chair. There was a small stove, and a shelf with a few books. There was a mirror on the wall. The floor was bare stone. The window was small, and it looked out over the green tops of the trees.

Rapunzel stood still and looked around. She felt the cold of the stone. She felt very small.

Then she heard the witch's voice again, far below.

"Rapunzel, Rapunzel,
Let down your hair to me."

She did not understand. Then she saw the hook that was fixed to the window frame. She took off the ribbon that held her hair, and she shook out her long, golden braids. She wound them once around the hook, and she let them fall out of the window. They went down, and down, and down, twenty arm-lengths to the ground.

Below, the witch took hold of the hair. She pulled, and she began to climb. She was a strong, thin old woman, and she went up fast. Rapunzel held on to the hook, and she gritted her teeth. It hurt her head a little, but she did not cry out. In a few minutes, the witch stood in the room with her.

"There," she said. "That is how it will be. I will come every day, and I will bring you food and water and wood for the stove. And when I go, you will be safe."

"Will I be here for always?" asked Rapunzel.

"For as long as it is necessary," said the witch. She patted her cheek. "Do not look so sad, my girl. Many people would like a tower of their own."

And she climbed back down, and went away.

Rapunzel stood at the window and watched her go. The old woman walked between the trees until she was a small black shape. Then she was gone. The forest closed around the tower. The wind blew softly in the leaves.

At first, Rapunzel was very lonely. She cried for many days. She looked at the sky, and at the birds that flew past the window. She counted the stones in the wall. She read all the books on the shelf, one after another, and then she read them again. She sewed and she swept. She brushed her hair a hundred times a day.

But after some time, she began to sing. It was only a little song at first, a soft thing. She sang it to herself, to the window and the wind. She sang about the forest, and about the clouds, and about the birds. Her voice was sweet and clear, and it went out of the window and down through the trees.

The months passed. The seasons changed. In the autumn, the leaves outside the window turned red and gold. In the winter, the snow lay on the trees. The witch came every day, and she went away every day. Rapunzel said, "Thank you," and the witch said, "You are welcome."

A year went by, and then another. Rapunzel was no longer a child. She was tall and slender, and her face was grave and lovely. She sang every day, and her voice became more beautiful with each year. It floated out of the window of the tower and over the green forest, like a bird that had no cage.

She did not know that someone had heard it.
