# The Prince Climbs the Golden Hair

The golden hair fell down the wall of the tower. The prince took hold of it with both hands. It was strong and smooth, and it did not break. He put his feet against the stone, and he began to climb.

It was not easy. His arms were tired, and the tower was high. But he did not stop. Up and up he went, past the green leaves, past the place where the birds sat, until he reached the little window. He put his hands on the sill and pulled himself in.

He stood on the stone floor, and he was breathing hard.

In front of him, there was a girl.

She was standing by the window with her hands at her mouth. She was tall and slim, and her face was pale. Her eyes were very wide. She wore a simple blue dress, and her hair, her long golden hair, lay around her feet like a river of light.

For a moment, neither of them spoke. Rapunzel had never seen a man in her life, except in the pictures of her books. She did not know what to think. She was very frightened, and she stepped back.

"Do not be afraid," said the prince quickly. "Please. I will not hurt you."

"Who are you?" she said. Her voice was small. "You are not Dame Gothel."

"No," he said, and he smiled. "I am not. I am the son of the king of this country. My name does not matter. I heard you singing, many days ago, from the forest. Your voice was so beautiful that I could not forget it. I came back every day. I had to see you."

"You came every day?" said Rapunzel.

"Every single day," said the prince. "I sat behind a tree and listened. I did not know your name. Then I saw the old woman, and I heard her say it. Rapunzel. It is a beautiful name."

Rapunzel looked at him. He was young and handsome, and his eyes were gentle. He did not look like a wicked person. He did not look like what the witch had told her about the world. She felt her fear slowly go away.

"I have never spoken to anyone but Dame Gothel," she said. "I do not know what to say."

"Say anything," said the prince. "Tell me about yourself."

So Rapunzel sat down on the bed, and the prince sat on the chair. She told him about the tower, and about the books, and about her songs. He told her about his father's castle, and about the horses and the hunt. They talked until it was dark. The prince had never talked to anyone like this. She was clever, and she was gentle, and she listened to every word.

"May I come again?" he asked, when the stars came out.

"Yes," said Rapunzel. "But Dame Gothel comes in the morning. You must come in the evening, after she has gone."

"I will come every evening," said the prince.

And he did.

For many weeks, he climbed the golden hair, and he stayed with her until the night was old. They talked and laughed. He brought her small presents: a ribbon, a book, an apple, a flower. She sang to him. He told her about the world below, with its towns and rivers and ships. Her eyes shone when she listened.

One evening, the prince took her hand. "Rapunzel," he said, "I love you. I have never loved anyone before. Will you be my wife?"

Rapunzel was silent for a long time. She looked at his face, and she thought of the old witch. "She is not unkind," she thought, "but she is not warm. She does not love me as this man does." She looked at the stone walls, and at the little window, and at the empty sky.

"Yes," she said, and she put her hand in his. "I will. I would like to go with you. But I do not know how to get down. The tower has no door."

The prince thought. "I will bring a silk cord every time that I come," he said. "You can weave the cords into a ladder. When it is long enough, you will climb down, and I will take you away on my horse. We will go to my father's castle, and we will be married."

"Yes," said Rapunzel. Her face was bright with hope. "That is a good plan."

So every evening, the prince came with a skein of silk. He hid it in his coat. Every day, while the witch was away, Rapunzel wove the silk into a rope. She worked in secret, and she hid the rope under her bed. It grew a little longer every day.

They were very happy, and they were very careful. They made a rule that the prince would only come after the old woman had gone. They spoke softly. Rapunzel did not say a word to the witch. When the witch came in the morning, she sat quietly and sewed, and she hid her smile.

But Dame Gothel was not stupid. She looked at Rapunzel's face. She noticed that the girl sang more often. She noticed that her cheeks were rosy, and that her eyes were bright. But she did not guess the reason. She thought that her pupil was growing up.

She did not know that the prince had come to her tower.
