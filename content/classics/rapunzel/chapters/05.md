# The Voice in the Forest

One summer evening, the king's son was riding through the forest. He was a young man with a quiet face and kind brown eyes. He had been hunting all day, but he had not caught anything, and he did not mind. He liked to be alone in the woods. It was cool and green and peaceful, and nobody told him what to do.

His horse walked slowly along an old path. The sun was low, and the leaves made gold shapes on the ground. The prince was thinking about nothing in particular. Then he heard a sound.

He pulled his horse to a stop and listened.

It was a voice. It was a woman's voice, and it was singing. It was a clear, soft song, and it seemed to come from the sky. It was so sweet that he did not move. He forgot the hunt. He forgot the time. He only listened. It was the most beautiful thing that he had ever heard.

When the song ended, he waited for more, but the forest was quiet. He rode in a circle, and then in another, looking for the singer. Who could she be? There was no house here. There was no village for miles. He called out, "Hello?" But nobody answered.

At last, he saw something through the trees. It was a tall grey tower, rising out of a clearing. He rode toward it. He walked around it twice, and he looked at the smooth stone. He saw no door, and he saw no steps. He saw only a small window at the very top.

"Hello!" he called. "Is someone there?"

He waited. There was no answer. The window stayed dark. The voice did not sing again.

The prince did not understand. He stood there for a long time, and then, because it was getting dark, he rode home. But he could not sleep that night. He kept hearing the song in his mind.

The next day, he came back to the forest. He did not even take his bow. He sat on a log near the tower, and he waited. At sunset, he heard the voice again. It was singing a different song, softer than the first one, a song of loneliness. The prince put his hand on his heart.

"She is in there," he said to himself. "But how do I reach her?"

Day after day, he rode into the forest. He told nobody. He sat behind a big tree, and he listened. Every day, the song was the same, or it was different, and it was always beautiful. He began to feel that he knew the singer. He began to feel that he loved her.

One evening, he stood behind his tree as usual. The sun was going down, and the forest was quiet. Suddenly, he heard a sound of steps. He looked out carefully.

An old woman was walking out of the trees. She was tall and thin, and she wore a black dress. She carried a big basket on her arm. She walked straight to the foot of the tower and stopped. The prince held his breath.

The old woman looked up at the window. She raised her head, and she called in a loud, clear voice:

"Rapunzel, Rapunzel,
Let down your hair to me."

The prince could not believe his eyes. The little window opened. And out of the window came a long, golden rope. It was not a rope. It was hair. It fell down the wall of the tower, shining in the evening light, all the way to the ground.

The old woman took hold of the hair. She put one foot against the wall, and she climbed up. She went up quickly, like someone who had done it many times. At the top, she climbed in at the window, and the golden hair was pulled up after her. Then the window closed.

The prince stood behind his tree and did not move. His mouth was open. He had seen many strange things in his life, but never anything like this.

"So that is how it is done," he whispered. "And so her name is Rapunzel."

He waited until it was dark. A long time later, the old woman came out of the tower again the same way, and she walked away into the forest with her empty basket. The prince watched her go. When her steps faded, he came out from behind the tree.

His heart was beating hard. He looked at the tower, and he looked at the little window. He thought about the old woman, and about the song.

"If that is the ladder," he said, "then I will try my luck."

He waited all the next day. It seemed very long. At last, in the evening, when it was beginning to grow dark, he went to the tower. He stood where the old woman had stood. He cleared his throat, and he said, as well as he could, in the same voice:

"Rapunzel, Rapunzel,
Let down your hair to me."

For a moment, nothing happened. Then the window opened, and the golden hair came down.
